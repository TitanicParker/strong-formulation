# Contemporaneous Departmental Knowledge and the 25 March 2025 Rebuttal

**Status:** CONTROLLING EVIDENTIAL / CONTINUITY ANALYSIS — 9 September 2026

This file preserves a pairing that must not be split apart in later analysis:

1. the July 2017 discharge packet did not merely mention PRKN/PARK2 in the abstract; it expressly enclosed a Galway departmental article on Parkinson genetics written a couple of years earlier with Professor Timothy Counihan; and
2. the 25 March 2025 complaint response later relied upon a claimed longstanding Parkinsonian understanding and upon the longitudinal record when defending the historical pathway.

The significance is not that a journal article proves what any one clinician personally knew, or that the Parkinson's diagnosis was wrong. The significance is that the department's own published knowledge became part of the patient's contemporaneous discharge information state and must therefore be analysed alongside the later claim that the Parkinsonian condition had effectively been known throughout.

---

## 1. The 2015 genetics article was part of the patient's discharge information state

The July 2017 discharge material states:

> **“Another article on the genetics of Parkinsons written a couple of years ago from the Department here with Prof Counihan is enclosed within.”**

The article identified in the record is the 2015 Galway departmental paper:

> **Patrick Browne, Timothy Counihan, Patrick Cullinane and Aoife Corcoran, “Genetics in Parkinson's disease — what have we learned?”**

Public source: https://www.irishhealthpro.com/content/articles/print/name/genetics-in-parkinsons-disease--what-have-we-learned

The article was written from the Neurology Department at Galway University Hospital. It discusses young-onset and genetic Parkinsonism, including PRKN/Parkin, and identifies features including young age of onset, dystonia and symmetrical signs/symptoms.

The evidential consequence is important:

> **The 2015 departmental knowledge was not merely available somewhere in the literature. The discharge packet itself selected that knowledge for inclusion in this patient's case.**

The correct question is therefore not merely:

> **What did Galway Neurology know academically in 2015?**

It is:

> **What phenotypic knowledge did Galway expressly bring into this patient's July 2017 discharge package, and how was that knowledge applied to the phenotype actually observed at the bedside?**

---

## 2. The department's contemporary academic environment is part of the capability context

The departmental context is not limited to one enclosed article.

Publicly available sources show that the Galway neurological environment around the material period contained active work in:

- movement disorders;
- dystonia;
- PRKN/Parkin genetics;
- young/juvenile Parkinsonism;
- Parkinsonian gait;
- and ON/OFF or treatment-state movement assessment.

Examples include:

### 2017 dystonic-foot / Parkin clinical reasoning paper

Patrick Cullinane, Patrick Browne, Michael Hennessy and Timothy Counihan, all affiliated with the Department of Neurology, University Hospital Galway / NUI Galway, published:

> **“Clinical Reasoning: A demure teenager and her dystonic foot.”**

Publication: *Neurology*, August 2017.  
Public source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28808173/

Its subject matter included dystonia, abnormal gait, juvenile Parkinsonism and parkin/PARK2 genetics.

### 2017 Parkinsonian gait research

Patrick Browne and Timothy Counihan were also authors on a 2017 study of accelerometer-based detection of Parkinsonian gait. The article was received on **28 January 2017** and accepted on **8 August 2017**, placing its research/publication cycle across the period of the July admission.

Public source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2017.00431/full

### Departmental roles

Public author profiles identify Timothy Counihan as Consultant Neurologist in the Galway Neurology Department and Patrick Browne as Movement Disorders ANP Candidate in that department.

Sources:

- https://www.irishhealthpro.com/content/authors/list/id/36
- https://www.irishhealthpro.com/content/authors/list/id/35

These facts do **not** prove that any particular academic personally examined the patient, attended the Complex Case Meeting, or supplied a particular sentence in the discharge synthesis.

They establish a narrower proposition:

> **The concepts needed to ask the neurological–foot integration question were part of the contemporaneous intellectual and clinical capability of the department itself.**

## 3. This intensifies the synthesis–examination differential

The final synthesis displays sophisticated genetic and phenotypic reasoning. It entertains recessive Parkinsonism and PRKN/PARK2; emphasises very young onset, marked symmetry and other features; discusses genetic counselling, prognostication and future genomic analysis; and encloses departmental genetics material.

At the same time, the bedside record had already described:

- a **“strikingly abnormal”** gait;
- a lower-limb appearance **“somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture – NOT FOOT DROP”**;
- an **“almost dystonically unusual”** walk;
- painful abnormal feet;
- pes planus and hammertoes;
- longstanding corns/calluses and foot pain.

The audit question becomes:

> **If PRKN/Parkin knowledge was sufficiently relevant to be brought into the patient's discharge packet, where is the corresponding phenotypic operation that returned that knowledge to the recognised dystonic lower limb and painful abnormal foot before the feet were left “Not clear” and operationally separated?**

This does not prove that dystonia or the feet would have established PRKN.

It makes the selectivity of the synthesis clinically significant.

The strongest safe formulation is:

> **The sophistication of the final neurological synthesis makes the relative absence of documented interrogation of the disputed lower-limb/foot relationship more, not less, significant.**

## 4. The 25 March 2025 complaint response must be read beside the 2017 knowledge state

The 25 March 2025 complaint response contains a categorical present diagnostic statement:

> **“Whatever the underlying cause for the peripheral neuropathic pain symptomatologies that [the patient] has, it is our belief and understanding that [the patient] has idiopathic Parkinson's disease.”**

Later in the same response, the treating neurologist writes:

> **“In essence we have known that [the patient] has Parkinson's; we have known it would be Sinemet or other dopamine agonist responsive...”**

The same response invokes Professor Daniel Healy as an early second-opinion Parkinson's expert.

But the 2017 substance of Healy's opinion was more specific:

> **the patient “probably has Park II.”**

The repository therefore distinguishes:

> **continuity of Parkinsonian care**

from

> **continuity of the specific aetiological formulation.**

## 5. The pairing changes the meaning of the later “we have known” position

Read separately, each fact can be minimised.

The 2015 article can be called academic background.

The 2025 response can be called a later clinician describing an established Parkinsonian disorder.

Read together, they create a more exact question:

> **What exactly was known, what clinical work had earned that knowledge, and which parts of the phenotype were permitted to participate in the knowledge later described as longstanding?**

The later claim that Parkinson's had effectively been known throughout does not answer whether the neurological–foot relationship was adequately examined.

It sharpens that question:

> **If the Parkinsonian frame was already known, academically supported, genetically sophisticated and later described as longstanding, why did the recognised dystonic lower limb and painful foot remain outside that frame without a documented return examination or integrative reconstruction?**

---

## 6. The department must remain part of the case, but not as a substitute for proof

The repository must permanently distinguish three evidential layers:

### Patient-specific record

What was observed, examined, investigated, discussed, recorded, treated and decided in this patient's case.

### Contemporaneous departmental capability and knowledge

What concepts, expertise and research were demonstrably present in the department and, more strongly, what departmental knowledge was actually selected into this patient's clinical information state.

### Later institutional use

How the founding and longitudinal record was later used in complaint review, retrospective rebuttal and governance reliance.

The department context cannot prove that undocumented clinical work occurred or did not occur.

It can, however, make broad explanations based on **conceptual unavailability, novelty or mere complexity** less persuasive where the department's own work shows that the relevant conceptual tools were available and, in part, actually brought into the patient's case.

Thus:

> **Institutional capability does not prove breach. It determines how plausible it is to explain the unresolved phenotype by lack of available understanding.**

## 7. Relationship to complexity

A generic complexity defence becomes substantially narrower once the department context is retained.

The case may still have been clinically difficult.

But the hospital's own product demonstrates that the department was capable of sophisticated retrospective phenotype construction, including PRKN/PARK2 reasoning, and the department was contemporaneously active in dystonia, genetic Parkinsonism and gait work.

The expert question is therefore not:

> **Was this a complex case?**

It plainly was.

It is:

> **Did complexity explain residual uncertainty after adequate interrogation, or did sophisticated reasoning develop in one part of the phenotype while the disputed foot relationship received a materially lower-resolution inquiry?**

## 9. Controlling methodological rule

The repository must never again assess the case using the clinical record alone when the meaning of that record depends upon the institution that produced it.

At every material threshold, ask three things together:

> **What did the patient-specific record show?**

> **What did this department demonstrably know or have the capability to understand at that time, especially where that knowledge entered the patient's own case?**

> **How was the resulting record later inherited, interpreted or relied upon?**

The strongest formulation is:

> **The question is not simply what was knowable. It is what this department demonstrably knew, what of that knowledge entered this patient's case, what clinical work was performed with it, and what the institution nevertheless left outside the frame.**

Shortest form:

> **Record + department + later reliance must always be read together.**

## Evidential discipline

This file does **not** establish that:

- any named Galway academic personally examined the patient unless the patient-specific record proves it;
- the 2017 dystonic-foot publication concerned this patient;
- academic publication creates an individual duty by itself;
- PRKN/PARK2 was genetically proved in 2017;
- the foot pathology was wholly neurological;
- the Parkinson's diagnosis was wrong;
- or the 2025 complaint response was dishonest.

It establishes a controlling contextual rule: **the patient-specific record must be interpreted alongside the contemporaneous departmental knowledge/capability actually surrounding and, where documented, entering the case, and alongside the later institutional uses of the resulting record.**
