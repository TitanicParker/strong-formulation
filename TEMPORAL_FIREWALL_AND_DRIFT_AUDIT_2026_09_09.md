# Temporal Firewall and Drift Audit — 9 September 2026

**Status:** CONTROLLING CHRONOLOGY / ANTI-DRIFT RULE

This file records a repository audit prompted by recognition that later concepts had begun to contaminate the reconstruction of earlier information states.

It supersedes any contrary wording elsewhere in the repository.

## 1. The governing temporal firewall

Every proposition must be located at the information state in which it actually existed.

The repository must not project later knowledge, later documents, later treatment response, later genetics, later institutional interpretation or later conceptual language backwards into an earlier clinical moment.

The minimum sequence is:

> **3–10 July 2017: inpatient clinical work while the patient remains available for bedside examination**
>
> **pre-discharge Protest, if proved: the patient says in substance that the feet have not been dealt with**
>
> **patient leaves hospital**
>
> **discharge synthesis is subsequently completed/finalised as the portable specialist account**
>
> **September 2017: substantial post-discharge response to procyclidine/rasagiline is documented**
>
> **October 2017: procyclidine withdrawal/dechallenge produces contemporaneous tension + restless feet + deterioration of walking**
>
> **April 2018: the treating neurologist retrospectively records that the first rapidly changing feature during withdrawal was return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon**
>
> **later years: further mechanical, OFF-state, toe-curling, pain and dopaminergic-response evidence accumulates**

The exact date and authorship of particular elements of the discharge synthesis must be proved from source metadata where available. The repository may observe that the final document is more sophisticated than the surviving bedside examination demonstrates; it must not assume without evidence exactly which consultation or sentence was added after the patient left.

## 2. The Protest must never be represented as a reaction to the discharge synthesis

The patient had not seen the later discharge synthesis when he protested.

He was not objecting to:

- PRKN/PARK2 reasoning;
- the wording “Not clear”;
- the final genetic synthesis;
- the authority projected by the discharge document;
- or any later documentary frame.

The asserted Protest was simpler and earlier:

> **after an ordinary inpatient admission lasting about eight days, the patient was alarmed that the longstanding disabling foot problem had not received meaningful neurological / movement-disorders attention.**

The patient did not need a rival diagnosis.

He did not need to know PRKN, dystonia or the eventual theory.

His asserted information state was experiential:

> **the feet that had formed part of the reason for referral had not, from his perspective, been neurologically dealt with.**

Any repository wording that describes the Protest as **“Your frame has not accounted for this part of me”** may be retained only as a later analytical translation, never as the patient's contemporaneous object of objection.

The controlling formulation is:

> **The Protest challenged the clinical work actually experienced before discharge; it did not challenge a later synthesis the patient had not yet seen.**

## 3. The Protest changes the status of closure, not the character of the preceding admission

The repository must not retrospectively dramatise the entire admission merely because the later documentary consequences became important.

The safer reconstruction is:

> **Before the Protest: an ordinary inpatient admission involving an unusual very-young-onset neurological presentation, with clinical work still capable of being extended or corrected.**

> **At the Protest: an asserted unresolved part of the presenting problem is placed expressly before the discharging clinician while the patient is still available.**

> **After the Protest: the adequacy of professional closure becomes a distinct question.**

Thus:

> **The Protest changes nothing about the preceding admission and potentially everything about the adequacy and meaning of the discharge decision.**

This is a chronology rule, not a conclusion of breach.

## 4. Departmental sophistication belongs to the synthesis state unless earlier timing is proved

The final discharge packet contains high-resolution neurological and neurogenetic material, including PRKN/PARK2 reasoning and an enclosed Galway departmental Parkinson-genetics article with Professor Timothy Counihan.

That establishes that departmental knowledge entered the **discharge information state**.

It does **not**, by itself, establish:

- that the same material had been formulated before the Protest;
- that the patient knew it;
- that the relevant academic contributors attended the bedside;
- that the Complex Case Meeting occurred before or after any particular bedside event;
- or that every sophisticated element was present before the patient left.

The correct audit question is therefore:

> **When did each element of departmental sophistication enter the case, who supplied it, and did any of it return to the patient as further phenotype-directed bedside examination before discharge?**

The repository may state:

> **the synthesis is substantially more sophisticated than the surviving bedside foot examination demonstrates.**

It must treat the proposition:

> **departmental expertise entered principally after the patient left**

as an investigative hypothesis until timestamps, drafts, meeting records or witness evidence establish it.

## 5. The procyclidine response is post-discharge evidence

The repository must not describe procyclidine as an inpatient therapeutic experiment generating evidence before the Protest.

The July record contains treatment planning, including procyclidine/rasagiline strategy and contemplated future withdrawal.

The strong documented response belongs later, after discharge.

Therefore the procyclidine evidence performs a different job from the Protest:

> **The Protest challenges the completeness of the bedside clinical work before closure.**

> **The later treatment response and dechallenge challenge the adequacy or durability of the boundary after closure.**

Those two challenges must never be collapsed.

The later treatment evidence is an independent reopening route precisely because it did not exist at the Protest moment.

## 6. The synthesis–examination differential survives the correction

Removing the temporal contamination does not weaken the synthesis–examination issue. It makes it cleaner.

The question is not whether the patient objected to a sophisticated synthesis.

It is:

> **Why did the final discharge product display a level of neurological / PRKN sophistication not clearly matched by the surviving documented movement-disorders examination of the longstanding painful feet while the patient was still available to be examined?**

This creates a chronological loop for investigation:

> **bedside observations → Protest about unresolved feet → patient departure → sophisticated synthesis/finalisation → later biological evidence capable of testing the exported boundary.**

Where source timing is incomplete, the brackets remain open.

## 8. Mandatory future chronology labels

Future analytical writing should distinguish these states explicitly:

> **INPATIENT / PRE-PROTEST** — only information demonstrably available before the Protest.

> **PROTEST / DECISION-LIVE** — what the patient and clinician could know while the patient remained present.

> **POST-DEPARTURE / SYNTHESIS** — material appearing in the final discharge product whose precise timing or authorship may require reconstruction.

> **POST-DISCHARGE TREATMENT** — September/October 2017 treatment response and dechallenge.

> **RETROSPECTIVE CLINIC RECORD** — April 2018 and later clinician-authored interpretation of earlier events.

> **LATER REVIEW / GOVERNANCE** — complaint, SIMT, QPS and other institutional uses of the inherited record.

No proposition may migrate from a later state to an earlier state merely because it makes the narrative more coherent.

## 9. Corrected controlling sequence

The post-audit controlling chronology is:

> **integrated GP referral → ordinary inpatient work on an unusual young neurological presentation → feet remain insufficiently neurologically mapped on the surviving record → Protest that the feet have not been dealt with, if proved → discharge/closure proceeds → patient leaves → sophisticated discharge synthesis is completed/finalised as the portable specialist state → later procyclidine/rasagiline response → withdrawal/dechallenge creates new neurological-foot evidence → later mechanical and neurological evidence accumulates → later review relies upon the inherited record.**

The boldest claims inside that sequence remain conditional where the source record does not yet prove timing, authorship, breach or causation.

## 10. Shortest anti-drift rules

> **Do not let the final synthesis travel backwards in time.**

> **Do not let post-discharge treatment travel backwards into the admission.**

> **Do not make the patient protest against knowledge he did not possess.**

> **Do not infer the timing of departmental consultation merely from the sophistication of the final document.**

> **Keep bedside work, Protest, synthesis and later biological evidence as separate information states.**

These rules are controlling for all future repository work.
