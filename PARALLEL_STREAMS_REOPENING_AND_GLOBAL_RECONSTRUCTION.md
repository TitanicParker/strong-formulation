# Parallel Streams, Reopening and Global Reconstruction

**Status:** SOURCE-LEVEL REFINEMENT — 8 September 2026  
**Purpose:** Record what the patient-facing corpus presently demonstrates about the Podiatry pathway, the Neurology treatment-state stream, the genuine 2023 reopening, and the narrower question of whether the founding feet–tone–gait evidential genealogy was ever globally reconstructed.

---

## 1. The Podiatry pathway predates the Protest

The available primary record shows that Podiatry was not first conceived after the pre-discharge Protest.

The GP record on **10 April 2017** documents bilateral foot pain, abnormal gait, mallet toes/callus-pressure areas and a plan to refer to Podiatry. See `CLIN-000018–000028`.

During the neurological admission, on **6 July 2017**, the GP telephoned Podiatry and recorded that the case would be prioritised **“as Parkinson’s.”** See `CLIN-000236–000245`.

The later Podiatry system distinguishes:

- **Referral Date:** 10 Apr 2017;
- **Registration Date:** 10 Jul 2017;
- **First Treated:** 14 Sep 2017;
- **Patient Last Seen:** 25 Mar 2019.

See `CLIN-002819–002839`.

The surviving referral material identifies **Dr Aaron Brennan** as referrer. See `CLIN-003014–003016`.

The hospital discharge record separately states that a Podiatry referral **“has been sent”**, while the longer neurological plan asks for outpatient Podiatry and says the SHO had tried to arrange it. See `CLIN-000341–000345`, `CLIN-001225–001229`.

The presently supportable source-level finding is therefore:

> **The post-Protest answer “Podiatry” appears to have intersected with an already-existing GP-origin Podiatry pathway. The patient-facing Podiatry corpus presently does not demonstrate a separate surviving Neurology/Monaghan referral carrying a defined neurological question about tone, gait, dystonia, loading or the structural–dynamic relationship.**

Do not convert this into proof that no such referral or communication ever existed. It is a finding about the surviving patient-facing corpus.

## 2. Why this sharpens the meaning of “Podiatry”

Podiatry remains a legitimate destination for genuine structural foot disease.

The sharper question is not whether referral was appropriate in the abstract.

It is:

> **What additional neurological information or question, if any, was added to an already-existing Podiatry pathway after the patient directly said the neurological work on the feet was incomplete?**

On the presently identified material, the answer is not demonstrated by a distinct integrative handover document.

This makes the post-Protest fork more precise:

> **existing Podiatry destination + no identified new neurological question + neurological closure.**

## 3. Podiatry develops a stable focal anatomical object

The Podiatry pathway becomes increasingly anatomically specific.

By the first treatment in September 2017, the record identifies a **left fifth-MTPJ callus** and tight gastrocnemius/soleus. In October 2017 the fifth-MTPJ lesion is enucleated/debrided to pin-prick bleeding, the site is directly offloaded and an insole is planned specifically to redistribute pressure. The same fifth-MTPJ territory recurs through 2018 and again in 2019, with repeated clawed-toe/mechanical observations and targeted insole modification. See `CLIN-002866–002988`.

The safe proposition is:

> **Podiatry did not merely record vague sore feet. It developed a remarkably stable focal mechanical/loading object centred on the left fifth MTPJ.**

This establishes local anatomy and mechanics, not the complete cause of the loading.

## 4. Neurology simultaneously acquires a dynamic fact about the same broad territory

During the planned procyclidine withdrawal the patient contemporaneously reported increasing tension, restless feet and deterioration of gait. See `CLIN-001770–001790`.

In April 2018 the treating neurologist recorded that the **first thing** noticed changing, and changing **rapidly**, was the **return** of the outside-left-foot corn phenomenon, together with greater “shifty” / “unrelaxed” behaviour off treatment. See `CLIN-001887–001919`.

This is clinician-authored treatment-state knowledge.

It does not establish that procyclidine directly altered keratin formation or prove dystonic loading.

The safe proposition is:

> **Neurology possessed a dynamic treatment-state fact affecting the same broad outside-left-foot territory in which Podiatry was repeatedly documenting focal mechanical pathology.**

The correct formulation is therefore **not**:

## 10. By 2024 the record separates pain from motor foot phenomena

> **pain state ≠ toe-curling / motor phenomenon.**

The record is therefore capable by this point of distinguishing a pain component from a motor/postural foot component.
