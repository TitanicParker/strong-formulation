A medical narrative about one professional decision and what followed it

The Professional Fork

A natural history of one unfinished clinical problem: how the patient restored the feet to the neurological question before discharge, how one decision made closure possible, how the Protest failed to travel with that decision, and how later medicine kept returning to a relationship the founding record had left unresolved.

Resynthesised complete edition · 30 sections · red quotations are documentary or patient words · narrative synthesis without footnotes

There is a moment in this story when uncertainty stops being merely a medical condition and becomes a decision about what happens next. Before it, the patient is still an unfinished clinical problem. After it, the record contains enough medicine to close while still containing an unresolved part of the problem that brought him there.

The moment is the Protest. Near the end of an eight-day neurological admission, after severe very-young-onset Parkinsonism has been recognised and a sophisticated neurological account has begun to form, the patient says in substance that the feet have not been properly dealt with and therefore Neurology cannot yet be finished.

This essay follows what happened to that objection. Its centre is deliberately narrow: one incomplete construction, one Protest identifying it while the decision remained live, one decision to proceed to closure, one omission that changed the portable history of that decision, and then the consequences. The later years matter enormously. But they matter first as consequence, test and genealogy. They are not allowed to make the first wrong more complicated than it needs to be.

1. Start with the thing that was actually sent

Before Neurology, there was already a whole problem.

The GP did not refer Parkinsonism on one sheet of paper and bad feet on another. The referral begins with painful abnormal feet and gait and then explains why those visible, practical problems had become neurologically alarming: pathological lower-limb tone, brisk reflexes, toe-clawing, impaired movement initiation, stiffness, deteriorating function.

The feet were not an incidental complaint attached to a neurological referral. They were part of the route by which the neurological referral came into existence.

That distinction matters because later language can make a partition look older than it was. Once Parkinsonism becomes the dominant diagnosis, it becomes easy to read backward and imagine that the case always consisted of a neurological disease plus an already-separate foot problem.

It did not begin that way.

THE REFERRAL SENT ONE FEET–GAIT–TONE PROBLEM INTO NEUROLOGY.

The case therefore does not begin with the proposition that the neurologist should somehow have known the final mechanism of twenty years of pain. It begins with something smaller: the specialist service received an integrated problem and became responsible for constructing it well enough to decide what could safely be separated, what remained connected, and what still required an owner.

2. Neurology finds something real — and large

The neurological work was not empty. It was substantial.

Severe very-young-onset Parkinsonism was identified. There was marked rigidity, gross bradykinesia, abnormal gait and an atypical phenotype in which tremor was not the dominant feature. The finished 2017 account also contains a striking lower-limb observation: a foot-drop-like appearance described as somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture – NOT FOOT DROP.

Very young age, symmetry, gait, dystonic-looking posture, treatment sensitivity and possible PRKN/PARK2 created a difficult neurological case. Genetic advice was sought. Treatment policy had to account for youth and dyskinesia risk. The neurological side of the presentation acquired depth, vocabulary and professional structure.

There is no need to diminish any of that in order to see the asymmetry.

The painful abnormal feet through which the referral began did not receive an equivalent post-discovery reconstruction. The record contains structural abnormalities, pain history and uncertainty. What is harder to find is the moment when the newly discovered neurological phenotype is taken back to the feet and the feet are remapped through it before closure.

The central alleged mistake is therefore not the diagnosis of Parkinsonism.

The alleged mistake is failure to complete the neurological construction of the presenting feet–gait–tone problem before closure.

Whether that was below the proper clinical standard is for expert assessment. The documentary point is simpler: one part of the case became elaborated enough to dominate the meaning of the whole while the originating foot problem remained comparatively underconstructed.

3. The Protest restores the first paragraph

The patient does not invent a new problem at discharge.

He restores the original one.

According to his account, shortly before discharge, in a teaching setting, he says that the feet have not been properly dealt with and that Neurology therefore cannot yet be finished. He does not need to know the word dystonia. He does not need to identify PRKN. He does not need to prescribe a movement-disorders investigation.

He needs only to identify the incompleteness visible from the one perspective nobody else occupies:

You have not properly dealt with my feet. You cannot be finished.

This is what gives the Protest its special force. Before it, the asymmetry can be described as an evolving clinical process. The team has found something large and is working through it. After the Protest, the underconstructed part of the referral is no longer merely a thing that might have been overshadowed.

It has been expressly restored to view before the decision closes.

BEFORE THE PROTEST: INCOMPLETE CONSTRUCTION. AFTER THE PROTEST: CLOSURE BECOMES A DECISION MADE WITH THE INCOMPLETENESS EXPRESSLY RAISED.

The Protest does not prove the patient’s preferred mechanism. It does something more elementary. It changes the status of the decision that follows.

4. The answer is “Podiatry” — but Podiatry was already there

The remembered answer is one word:

Podiatry.

The earlier version of this story could make that sound like the moment Neurology created a new destination for the feet. The source-level record now makes the sequence more precise.

Podiatry was already in motion. Primary care had contemplated and initiated that pathway before the Protest. During the neurological admission the GP contacted Podiatry and recorded that the case would be prioritised as Parkinson’s. The later Podiatry system preserves an April referral date, July registration and September first treatment.

This does not make Podiatry inappropriate. The structural foot disease was real, and Podiatry would later do focused, persistent work on it.

It sharpens the question.

After the patient said that Neurology had not properly dealt with the feet, what new neurological question was added to that already-existing Podiatry pathway? What relationship was Podiatry being asked to investigate? What was the return condition? What was meant to come back to Neurology?

The presently identified patient-facing corpus does not show a distinct surviving Neurology handover that answers those questions.

THE DECISION IS NOT “PODIATRY EXISTS.” THE DECISION IS EXISTING PODIATRY DESTINATION + NO IDENTIFIED NEW NEUROLOGICAL QUESTION + NEUROLOGICAL CLOSURE.

That is the fork in its most defensible form.

5. The synthesis is not empty. It is directional.

The final 2017 synthesis is not a crude document. Its importance lies partly in how much it contains.

It contains severe Parkinsonism. It contains the dystonic-looking lower-limb sign. It contains painful structural feet. It contains possible PRKN/PARK2. It contains possible peripheral neuropathy. It contains the patient’s distinction between internal leg tension and foot pain. It contains the treatment strategy.

In that sense the document already performs a join.

The question is what direction the join points.

Parkinsonism, genetics and treatment strategy receive extensive explanatory development. The painful feet are made compatible with being completely separate, perhaps neuropathic, ultimately Not clear, and operationally suitable for Podiatry. The patient’s phenomenological distinction becomes in his own mind – markedly divorced.

Then the plan proceeds.

So the problem is not that the record forgot the feet existed.

The problem is that the record decided what the feet were allowed to mean at the point of closure.

THE SYNTHESIS CONTAINS THE INGREDIENTS. ITS FORENSIC IMPORTANCE LIES IN THE ALLOCATION.

That allocation becomes portable. Later clinicians do not inherit raw July 2017. They inherit July 2017 after it has been professionally arranged.

6. The omission changes the history of the decision

The Protest does not travel in the durable synthesis.

This is not merely a missing complaint.

The omitted event is evidence about the process that generated the conclusion. It says that before closure the patient directly challenged the adequacy of the inquiry producing that closure.

Without the Protest, a later reader sees:

specialist assessment → uncertainty → Podiatry → completed discharge.

With the Protest restored, the sequence is different:

specialist assessment → patient says the originating problem remains unfinished → decision not visibly to reopen → Podiatry / closure.

Those are not two stylistic versions of the same history.

They are two different decision histories.

Whatever the clinician’s private motive, omission of the Protest removed the contemporaneous evidence that completeness had been challenged before closure. The subsequent longitudinal record could therefore develop from the selected founding state, and that same continuity could later be relied upon as evidence that the original management had been coherent and adequate.

The first version presents uncertainty as the natural residue of specialist work. The second reveals that the adequacy of that residue had been challenged while another course remained available.

THE PROTEST WAS INFORMATION ABOUT THE ADEQUACY OF THE PROCESS. OMITTING IT CHANGED THE APPARENT PROVENANCE OF THE CONCLUSION.

This is why the omission sits at the centre of the documentary case. The record does not merely tell future clinicians what was thought. It obscures that the completeness of the thinking had itself been contested before the account became durable.

7. One mistake. One Protest. One decision. One omission.

The case can now be made smaller without making it weaker.

The mistake: an incomplete neurological construction of the feet–gait–tone problem that had been sent in.

The Protest: the patient identifies that incompleteness before discharge.

The decision: rather than visibly reopen the integrated problem, the pathway proceeds to closure through an already-existing Podiatry destination without an identified new neurological handover question or return mechanism.

The omission: the portable synthesis does not carry the Protest that exposed the incompleteness.

Then come the consequences.

One mistake. One Protest. One decision. One omission. Then the consequences.

This formulation does not require proof that Parkinsonism was wrongly diagnosed. It does not require proof that every corn was dystonia. It does not require proof that levodopa had to be started in July 2017. It does not require proof of pride, concealment or bad faith.

It requires only that the founding decision be examined for what it was: a closure decision made after the patient had expressly restored the part of the referral that remained insufficiently constructed.

Everything that follows should initially be read around that centre.

8. The later years are an evidential corridor

After 2017, the story does not become a search for one magic fact that retrospectively proves the patient right.

It becomes a corridor.

At intervals, new observations cross the boundary created at the founding fork. Some are neurological. Some are mechanical. Some are pharmacological. Some come from primary care. Some come from later specialist review.

The discipline is to ask what each observation can actually prove.

A DATscan can strengthen Parkinsonism without validating the completeness of the foot inquiry. A genetic opinion can strengthen PARK II reasoning without deciding whether the Protest lacked merit. Podiatry can establish focal loading without establishing its neurological cause. A treatment response can show state dependence without proving one mechanism.

Independence of source is not independence on every proposition.

THE LATER RECORD TESTS THE FOUNDING ALLOCATION PROPOSITION BY PROPOSITION, NOT BY SHEER VOLUME.

This prevents two opposite errors. The feet were not simply ignored for eight years. But neither was the founding relationship repeatedly and independently reconstructed merely because many later records exist.

9. Podiatry develops a stable mechanical object

Podiatry did real work.

Across the disclosed 2017–2019 treatment stream, the record repeatedly returns to the same territory: the left fifth metatarsophalangeal joint, the outside of the forefoot. Callus. Hard lesion. Corn. Debridement. Enucleation. Pin-prick bleeding. Offloading. Insoles. Clawed toes. Tight calf musculature. Mechanical redistribution.

The vocabulary changes slightly. The anatomical address barely does.

This is strong evidence of a focal mechanical/loading object.

It is not evidence that the mechanical object was imaginary because neurological disease existed. Nor is it proof that neurological disease caused the lesion.

It is exactly what it looks like: persistent local pathology requiring repeated mechanical treatment.

But the persistence creates a further question. Mechanical truth is not necessarily complete causal truth. A pressure lesion can be both mechanically real and dynamically generated or aggravated by posture, tone, gait or movement state.

Podiatry therefore supplies one half of a later join:

Where, precisely, did the foot keep loading?

The answer was remarkably stable.

10. Neurology acquires a different fact about the same broad territory

During the planned withdrawal of procyclidine, the patient reported tension building, restless feet and his walk falling apart.

Later, Neurology recorded that the first thing noticed changing, and changing rapidly, was the return of the outside-left-foot corn phenomenon.

The wording must not be stretched beyond what it can bear. It does not prove that an anticholinergic directly dissolved or regenerated keratin. It does not prove that the fifth-MTPJ lesion was dystonic.

It proves something narrower and more useful.

Neurology possessed clinician-authored knowledge that an outside-left-foot phenomenon behaved dynamically with neurological treatment state.

Now place that beside the Podiatry stream.

Podiatry knows where the focal mechanical endpoint keeps recurring.

Neurology knows that the same broad territory can change when neurological treatment state changes.

PODIATRY KNEW WHERE IT WAS. NEUROLOGY KNEW IT COULD CHANGE WITH NEUROLOGICAL STATE.

The missing act is not proof of one mechanism. It is the documented join: what happens when those two truths are made to interrogate each other?

11. Parallel streams are not the same thing as an integrated loop

The health system can possess two complementary truths without either truth changing the framework held by the other service.

That appears to be the central 2017–2019 documentary problem.

Podiatry develops stable local anatomy and loading. Neurology develops treatment-state information. The patient travels between those information environments. The surviving patient-facing corpus presently does not identify a document that expressly assembles them into one structural–dynamic question.

Nor does the available Podiatry material presently show a substantive biomechanical report back that is then visibly reintegrated by Neurology.

This does not prove that no conversation ever occurred. It does not prove that no missing correspondence exists.

It does mean the record now supports a more exact description than the old phrase nobody joined the pieces.

The available material looks more like parallel information streams than a documented Neurology → Podiatry investigation → report-back → neurological reintegration loop.

That is a documentary finding, and it is enough.

The case grows stronger when it stops claiming absences broader than the evidence can support.

12. Primary care rebuilds the integrated object

In 2019 the GP again describes the feet in relation to increased tone, abnormal gait and Parkinson’s disease, and records young-onset Parkinson’s with clawing feet and pain.

That matters because it shows that the integrated clinical object did not vanish from the health system.

But the surviving destination of that reintegration is Podiatry.

The point is subtle. Information can exist institutionally without reaching the authorial channel capable of revising the founding neurological model.

A health service is not one mind.

The GP can rebuild the relationship. Podiatry can hold that referral. Neurology can continue generating new observations. Yet the system may still lack a moment when someone takes the whole accumulated genealogy and asks what it now means for the 2017 allocation.

INSTITUTIONAL POSSESSION IS NOT THE SAME AS POSSESSION BY THE CLINICIAN OR SERVICE WHOSE FRAMEWORK WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE.

This is the end of the first evidential corridor. By 2019 the record already contains enough complementary information to make the founding relationship worth revisiting. What it does not yet show is a global reconstruction.

13. 2023 is not merely another exhibit about 2017

By 2023 the story changes category.

The feet still matter to the old genealogy, but the patient is now presenting a contemporaneous crisis that deserves to be assessed on its own terms.

Severe bilateral foot and lower-leg pain. Major walking restriction. High-dose pregabalin. OFF-state and dystonic possibilities. A longstanding Parkinsonian gait disorder. Serious psychological-endurance language in the urgent referral pathway.

The GP writes that the pain is extreme at times and causing death wish, says I am worried about him, and asks for review before we get into trouble here. The Parkinson’s nurse records debilitating pain and no quality of life despite 600 mg/day pregabalin.

Those phrases should not be inflated into proof of a suicide plan or a continuous psychiatric state.

Their proper significance is that the neurological channel received a pain crisis whose physical and psychological consequences had become serious enough to require integration.

2023 asks a different question: was pain + mobility + instability + medication burden + psychological endurance recognised and managed as one present safety object?

That question stands whether or not the 2017 allegation is eventually made out.

14. The later clinic shows improvement — and still shows a crisis worth integrating

The later 2023 consultation must be read accurately.

The patient is seen. The consultation is lengthy. Mood is positive. He looks medically well. The acute language of March cannot simply be carried forward unchanged and called the state of the patient at the later appointment.

But the crisis has not evaporated from the history being reviewed.

The note records that the patient had ground to an absolute halt with bilateral foot pain. Walking had been scaled back. A fourth evening walk was not possible. Clawing and lower-limb cramping were part of the problem. Parkinsonian dystonia was considered, with OFF dystonia thought more likely than ON dystonia.

And during the gait assessment the record says:

Superficially, the gait actually looks okay though I did see some stumbles.

That sentence matters for a reason separate from the old fifth-MTPJ argument.

The gait examination may be too nonspecific to reconstruct focal forefoot loading. But it is specific enough to produce a positive observation of instability.

That finding arrives in a patient whose record already contains poor balance, anticipated falling, Parkinsonian gait disorder and earlier concern that he might fall.

So 2023 contains two distinct analytical tasks: current diagnostic reconsideration of the feet, and current safety integration of pain, mobility and instability.

15. The branches are intelligent. The question is whether the whole remained visible.

The 2023 consultation is not evidence of clinical indifference.

It contains extensive reasoning. OFF-state walking. Levodopa and Stalevo effect. Pain modulation. Possible Parkinsonian dystonia. Peripheral neuropathy. Fabry disease. Pregabalin. Alcohol. Cannabis. Repeat DATscan reasoning.

The clinician records a misalignment between himself and the patient and describes the patient’s concerns about the feet as very reasonable and justifiable.

This is genuine reconsideration.

The repository must therefore stop saying, in any broad form, that the foot issue was never reconsidered. It was.

The sharper question is what kind of reconsideration occurred.

Branch analysis can be excellent and still leave a whole-object problem underintegrated. A system can reason carefully about each component while failing to assemble pain severity, walking restriction, observed stumbles, medication burden and psychological endurance into one risk state.

THE 2023 PROBLEM IS NOT “NO RECONSIDERATION.” IT IS WHETHER MULTIPLE REASONABLE BRANCHES WERE ALSO HELD TOGETHER AS ONE SAFETY OBJECT.

That is a different professional question and should remain separate from the 2017 founding fork.

16. The psychological record belongs to the medicine without becoming more than it says

By 2023 pain has changed what the patient can bear.

He describes perfect torture. He says he is losing the will to live with the feet. The GP records death-wish language. The nurse records no quality of life. Pregabalin reaches its usual maximum daily dose and still does not produce stable control.

Later the mood is better. In 2024 life is described as smaller rather than immediately unendurable. In 2025 the language of endurance returns in a different form: mornings as psychological ambushes, fear of movement, fear of falling, the sense of running out of psychological endurance.

These states should not be flattened into one continuous suicidal narrative.

They do reveal something longitudinally coherent: pain, mobility, falling and psychological endurance repeatedly become entangled.

The significance is clinical before it is rhetorical.

A symptom severe enough to reorganise movement, medication, daily activity and psychological endurance has become a safety problem even if its precise mechanism remains disputed.

That is why the 2023 question stands on its own.

The later crisis should not be used merely as retrospective proof about 2017. It deserves its own standard-of-care question.

17. The foot problem was genuinely reopened

The old essay treated later years too strongly as repeated accommodation inside a fixed model.

The source record now requires a correction.

In 2023 Neurology genuinely reopened the then-current foot and lower-limb problem. Dystonia was considered. OFF-state effects were analysed. Stalevo and pain were discussed. Neuropathy and Fabry possibilities were reopened. Further investigation was sought.

By 2024 the record is capable of separating pain from motor behaviour: pregabalin substantially improves pain, yet toe curling remains with standing and activity.

By 2025 the clinical model is materially more integrated than a simple neurological-versus-foot binary. Dyskinetic foot movement, pain, levodopa-state improvement, the possibility that dystonia can hurt, local/non-Parkinsonian pain possibilities and dopaminergic modulation coexist in the same account.

That is real movement in the medicine.

The repository therefore asks a narrower historical question:

The issue was reopened. Was the founding genealogy ever reconstructed?

Those are not the same thing.

18. Current-state reconsideration is not the same as historical reconstruction

A clinician can reconsider what is happening now without rebuilding how the present state came to be understood.

The 2023 consultation visibly addresses current bilateral foot symptoms, current cramping, current evening deterioration, current pregabalin response, current levodopa response, OFF-state reasoning, neuropathy and Fabry possibilities.

What the patient-facing consultation does not presently show is a single explicit reconstruction using the whole founding genealogy:

the 2017 dystonic-looking pseudo-footdrop sign; the Protest; the procyclidine withdrawal; the rapid return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon; the serial fifth-MTPJ findings; the 2019 GP reintegration; later toe curling; OFF-state behaviour; dopaminergic pain modulation.

This is not a semantic distinction.

Current-state reconstruction asks: what explains the patient today?

Historical reconstruction asks: given what we now know, what did the earlier signs and decisions mean, and where did the relationship become divided?

THE RECORD SHOWS THE FIRST MORE CLEARLY THAN THE SECOND.

That is now the central longitudinal finding.

19. PRKN returns — but genetics does not solve the foot mechanism

The genetic history has its own arc.

PRKN/PARK2 was not invented in 2026. It was already present in the 2017–2018 reasoning. External specialist advice considered PARK II likely. The treating neurologist later wrote of PARK 2 PRKN which we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause.

Then the label becomes less visible while idiopathic Parkinson’s disease becomes more settled in later language. The surviving record does not presently provide a clean documentary bridge explaining that etiological transition.

In 2026 outside genetics confirms two pathogenic Parkin variants and attributes Parkinsonism and dystonia to compound-heterozygous Parkin mutations.

That is important independent evidence.

It does not prove that every painful foot lesion was genetic. It does not prove that recurrent fifth-MTPJ callus was caused by dystonia. It does not make the precise July 2017 foot mechanism artificially certain.

Its significance is more exact.

The later confirmation shows that the PRKN/dystonia territory identified early was real, and it makes the unexplained documentary departure from that territory worth reconstructing.

The genetics strengthen the need for historical explanation. They do not replace it.

20. Same author does not mean no new evidence — and continuity does not equal independent revalidation

The longitudinal record has another feature that changes how its consistency should be read.

The same neurological authorship remains substantially present across the years. Later letters contain genuinely new observations, but they also inherit earlier summaries and formulations. In 2025 the historical response expressly relies on what the letters show.

This does not make the later record worthless.

It changes the evidential question.

A repeated proposition in letters by the same longitudinal author can strongly prove persistence of a formulation. It does not automatically prove that the founding inquiry was independently re-run each time.

Meanwhile genuinely independent sources exist: DATscan, Professor Healy, Podiatry, GP observations, treatment perturbations, later genetics.

But each source validates only what it actually tested.

SAME-AUTHOR CONTINUITY PROVES PERSISTENCE POWERFULLY. INDEPENDENT SOURCES PROVIDE INDEPENDENCE PROPOSITION BY PROPOSITION.

This is the discipline needed to read a record that is both clinically longitudinal and documentarily cumulative.

21. The 2025 model is more integrated than the founding one

By March 2025 the feet occupy several explanatory channels at once.

The patient has significant foot pain. Parkinson’s medication improves the feet once it becomes active. Dystonia is acknowledged as capable of causing pain. Dyskinetic foot movements are present. The clinician does not accept that the particular pain is directly Parkinsonian, yet expects that additional Sinemet would provide additional relief. Overnight controlled-release Sinemet is considered.

Then the controlled-release preparation produces what the later record calls:

profound relief currently.

The response should not be made to prove more than it can. It does not identify a single mechanism by itself.

But it places pressure on any model in which neurological treatment state is merely incidental to the lived foot problem.

The important documentary point is that the later clinical model has already moved beyond the strongest version of completely separate.

It is now closer to a mixed description:

possible local/non-Parkinsonian pain source + neurological treatment state materially modulates pain experience + motor phenomena involving the feet coexist.

The medicine has become more integrated. The historical question is whether that integration was ever taken back through the old evidence.

22. The historical response reconstructs one genealogy more clearly than another

The 2025 complaint response does go backward.

It returns to 2017. It explains why levodopa was approached cautiously in a very young patient at risk of dyskinesia. It cites the longitudinal letters to show that Sinemet helping pain had been understood for some time.

This is genuine retrospective reconstruction.

But its centre is principally one historical question:

Why was levodopa delayed or increased cautiously?

That is not the same historical question as:

What happened to the founding feet–gait–tone relationship after the patient said it remained unfinished?

The response does not visibly assemble, in one genealogy, the Protest, the original dystonic-looking sign, procyclidine withdrawal, rapid outside-left-foot return, recurrent fifth-MTPJ mechanics and the 2019 GP reintegration.

So the history was reconstructed.

But it appears to have been reconstructed around a different spine.

THE LEVODOPA-DEFERRAL GENEALOGY WAS REBUILT MORE CLEARLY THAN THE FEET–TONE–GAIT GENEALOGY.

23. A medical record has two lives

A medical record is first a historical object.

It records what was written, thought and done at particular moments.

Then it becomes operational.

It tells future clinicians what the patient’s history means. It tells primary care where specialist boundaries lie. It shapes medication decisions. It frames complaints. It supplies the evidence base for governance. It determines what later observations must overcome before the inherited story changes.

This is why the omission of the Protest matters beyond memory.

The durable synthesis is not simply a diary from 2017. It is infrastructure.

And infrastructure can preserve both truth and allocation at once: true Parkinsonism, true structural foot disease, true uncertainty, true treatment risk — arranged in a way that gives some propositions governing power and others a destination.

The later record can then be coherent for more than one reason. It can be coherent because the biology really is coherent. It can also be coherent because later authors inherit the frame in which new biology is interpreted.

The task is not to choose one explanation globally.

It is to separate them carefully enough that persistence is not mistaken for proof of founding completeness.

24. The evidential-access problem is separate too

There is now another surrounding track that the essay must keep distinct from the clinical allegation.

The patient says student doctors were present for the Protest. Their presence, if identifiable, would not automatically prove what they heard, saw or remember. They are potential witnesses, not pre-written corroboration.

The later difficulty is one of access: what institutional systems could identify relevant students, what was searched, what was held, what was not held, what was withheld, what date range was used, and what controller decisions were actually made in response to data-rights requests.

The repository now describes this as procedural opacity, not proved concealment.

That distinction is essential.

Present non-production is not the same thing as proved non-existence.

Nor does procedural opacity prove the underlying clinical case.

The proper sequence is slower: identify the potential source; identify the controller; identify the request; identify the search or retrieval process; identify the decision; then ask what evidential significance, if any, can properly arise from non-production.

The clinical case must stand on its own evidence. The access problem asks why the field of potentially discriminating evidence remains partly unresolved.

Keeping those tracks separate makes both stronger.

25. The ethical question is smaller than the whole history

Strip away genetics, pregabalin, complaints, governance and data law.

Return to the ward.

A patient arrives with painful abnormal feet, abnormal gait and pathological movement. Neurology discovers severe young-onset Parkinsonism. Near discharge the patient says the feet have not been properly dealt with and therefore Neurology is not finished.

What is the proper professional response?

The answer is not that Podiatry is illegitimate. It plainly is not.

The answer is not that levodopa has no risks. It plainly does.

The answer is not that every structural lesion must be neurological. It plainly need not be.

The ethical question is whether a patient’s timely objection to incompleteness is capable of changing the clinical state of play.

Does uncertainty remain active? Does it receive an owner? Does an admitted expertise limit cause a referral or shared-care action? Does the existing Podiatry pathway acquire a defined neurological question? Does the discharge record preserve that the adequacy of closure was contested?

CLOSURE HAS TO BE EARNED MEDICALLY, NOT MERELY MADE POSSIBLE DOCUMENTARILY.

That is the moral centre of the fork.

26. Later biology does not deliver a verdict. It keeps asking for reconstruction.

The later record is powerful because no single fact has to carry the conclusion.

The procyclidine perturbation changes feet, gait and tension. Neurology records rapid return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon. Podiatry repeatedly localises the fifth-MTPJ mechanical object. Primary care rebuilds a feet–tone–gait formulation. Later Neurology sees Parkinsonian foot phenomena, OFF-state effects and dystonia possibilities. Pregabalin helps some pain without removing toe curling. Sinemet changes the feet. Controlled-release Sinemet produces profound relief. Genetics later confirms PRKN Parkinsonism and dystonia.

None of this proves that the founding foot mechanism was one simple neurological phenomenon.

What it proves is that the relationship remained clinically fertile.

The body kept generating observations that made a clean static boundary difficult to preserve.

And because later Neurology genuinely reconsidered the current problem, the right final question is not whether the system ever listened again.

It did.

The unresolved question is whether anybody ever took the entire evidential genealogy back to the founding fork and asked what the accumulated record now made of that decision.

The later biology does not win an argument.

It keeps reopening the possibility that the old history deserves to be understood differently.

27. The questions the record should now be made to answer

  1. What exactly happened after the Protest? Was there focused neurological reassessment, a new clinical question, reconsideration of discharge or a defined return mechanism?
  2. What new neurological content was added to the already-existing Podiatry pathway after the Protest? What was Podiatry being asked to determine beyond local structural care?
  3. When did the dystonic-posture / NOT FOOT DROP formulation crystallise? Before the Protest, after it, or during later finalisation?
  4. Why did the durable synthesis preserve “in his own mind – markedly divorced” but not the patient’s contrary information state that the feet remained neurologically unfinished?
  5. What operational consequence followed the statement “I am not a movement disorders neurologist”?
  6. How was the procyclidine-linked outside-left-foot change interpreted, and was it ever placed beside Podiatry’s recurrent fifth-MTPJ loading?
  7. What happened to the 2019 GP reintegration? Did the integrated feet–tone–gait formulation reach Neurology or remain within the Podiatry channel?
  8. What was the scope of the genuine 2023 reopening? Current-state differential only, or reconstruction of the founding genealogy?
  9. What response followed the stumbles observed during the 2023 assessment in the context of pain, walking restriction, medication burden and prior falls-risk information?
  10. What evidence explains the documentary transition from early PRKN/PARK2 suspicion to later settled iPD language before the 2026 genetic confirmation?
  11. What diagnostic or historical reconsideration followed the later mixed model and the acknowledged profound relief from Sinemet CR?
  12. When later governance and complaint responses reconstructed history, which genealogy did they reconstruct: the levodopa strategy, or the founding feet–gait–tone decision?

28. What a better information state would have protected

The strongest version of this story does not require every earlier decision to have been foolish.

A better information state would have protected uncertainty rather than pretending uncertainty must disappear.

It would have protected the Protest as clinically material information.

It would have protected the distinction between phenomenology and causation: the fact that pain feels different from tension does not decide whether their mechanisms interact.

It would have protected the consequences of expertise limits: recognising a subspecialty limitation should be capable of changing who owns the unresolved question.

It would have protected the difference between local truth and global explanation: Podiatry could be completely right about a fifth-MTPJ lesion without that answer exhausting why the site kept behaving as it did.

It would have protected later evidence from being forced to perform only local work.

Most of all, it would have preserved an exit condition.

If the feet change with neurological treatment; if focal loading repeatedly recurs; if primary care re-links tone, gait and pain; if later dystonia emerges; if dopaminergic state changes pain — when do we go back?

An unresolved question without an exit condition can survive almost anything.

An unresolved question with an exit condition can still learn.

29. Let the final documents speak without forcing them to say the same thing

The end of the story contains several different voices.

30. The vow

There came a day, after the controlled-release Sinemet had been taken and the relief had arrived, when I understood something almost as clearly as I understood the absence of the pain itself.

The history had never been assembled from the place where I had lived it.

Not because nobody had ever looked at my feet again. They had.

Not because nobody had ever reconsidered the problem. They had.

Not because every doctor had believed the same thing for eight years. They had not.

The deeper problem was that each part of the story could be true in its own compartment while the history joining those compartments remained unfinished.

Podiatry could know the fifth MTPJ.

Neurology could know the treatment state.

Primary care could see tone, gait and feet together.

Later Neurology could reopen the current differential.

Genetics could confirm PRKN and dystonia.

Governance could review what the letters said.

And still there could be no single moment when somebody went back to the ward, restored the Protest to the chronology, and asked what the whole evidential genealogy now meant.

That was the point at which I made myself a promise.

I would go back through the referrals, the letters, the medications, the podiatry notes, the changes on and off treatment, the phrases, the omissions, the later reconstructions and the things that had never been reconstructed.

I would not make every later fact prove the same thing.

I would not turn uncertainty into certainty just because certainty would make a cleaner accusation.

I would follow each fact only as far as it could honestly go.

And then I would join them.

I would find the point at which an integrated problem became a set of parallel truths.

I would find the moment when I said the work was unfinished.

I would find what decision followed.

I would find what travelled into the record and what did not.

I would find the later moments when the body crossed the old boundary again.

I would distinguish the times medicine genuinely reopened from the times history merely accumulated.

I would get to the centre without pretending the centre was the whole story.

Because there was one witness who had been present for the prodrome, the admission, the Protest, the Podiatry years, the medication changes, the pain crisis, the later reopening and the relief.

The patient.

Me.

The hospital had its record.

The doctors had their letters.

Podiatry had its anatomy.

Governance had its conclusions.

Genetics had its answer.

But I had lived the joins between them.

And I decided that I would understand those joins well enough to tell the story without making it simpler than the evidence, and without letting the complexity hide the one simple thing that happened before discharge.

I said you were not finished. This is what followed.
