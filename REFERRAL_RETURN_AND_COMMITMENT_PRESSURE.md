# Referral Return and Commitment Pressure

## 1. The first paragraph is the control

The 29 June 2017 GP referral begins with the concrete presenting problem: an unusual stiff/festinant gait in a patient attending with bilateral foot problems, callouses and corns, with the explicit observation that his feet were “not in a good state.” The referral then explains why the GP had become concerned that this was not merely a foot problem: impaired movement initiation, marked increased tone, brisk reflexes, toe-clawing, inability to relax, worsening dexterity and occupational failure under an open neurological differential.

The referral therefore has a simple architecture:

> **Paragraph one: this is the problem through which the patient arrived.**
>
> **The remainder: this is why the referrer now suspects a neurological process and asks Neurology to explain the whole presentation.**

The case does not require criticism of a neurologist for initially being drawn toward the striking neurological material. Severe very-young-onset Parkinsonism, rigidity/bradykinesia, atypicality, dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenology, imaging, possible PRKN/PARK2 and treatment strategy are all substantial neurological findings. It is entirely understandable that they became the intellectual centre of the admission.

The evidential problem begins later.

## 2. The asymmetry is observable without reading the clinician’s mind

The surviving record shows an enormous difference in the amount and kind of clinical attention given to the neurological syndrome compared with the painful abnormal feet through which the referral began.

The Parkinsonian side receives extensive examination, imaging, differential diagnosis, complex-case discussion, genetic reasoning, treatment-risk reasoning, staged medication planning and counselling.

The feet are present in the record. Structural abnormalities are observed. Their history is recorded. The gait is described as footdrop-like and suggestive of dystonic posture. But the surviving record identifies only limited direct examination of the painful feet and no equivalent post-discovery remapping of the structural and dynamic foot phenomena before closure.

The correct proposition is therefore not:

> **The neurologist did not read the first paragraph.**

That is a mental-state claim the record cannot prove.

The stronger proposition is:

> **The record shows asymmetric clinical weighting of the referral: the findings pointing toward very-young-onset Parkinsonism were investigated and elaborated far more completely than the painful abnormal feet and gait problem through which the GP’s referral began.**

That is visible in the record itself.

## 3. The Protest restores the referral as a whole

After days in hospital, the patient expressly says in substance that the feet have not been properly dealt with and that the neurological work therefore cannot be complete. His NCT / bald-tyres analogy is not a diagnostic theory. It is an inquiry-completeness objection.

The patient is not introducing a new problem.

He is restoring paragraph one of the GP referral to the centre of the encounter.

The sequence is therefore:

> **GP refers the whole problem → Neurology solves the striking neurological component → patient points out that the originating foot problem remains insufficiently worked through → the referrer’s whole question is back in play before closure.**

This is the decisive hinge.

Initial narrowing can be understandable. After the Protest, the original underweighted material is no longer merely something that might have been missed, under-read or overtaken by the neurological diagnosis. It has been expressly restored to view by the patient before the case is closed.

The question then becomes:

> **What did Neurology do once the patient explicitly restored the first paragraph of the referral after days of specialist work?**

## 4. Why the timing creates commitment pressure

By the time the patient restores the first paragraph, the admission already contains substantial diagnostic and therapeutic investment:

- severe very-young-onset Parkinsonism has been identified;
- atypical features and possible PRKN/PARK2 have been considered;
- a dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon has been described;
- senior neurological input has been obtained;
- a cautious non-levodopa treatment strategy has begun to crystallise;
- the case has consumed days of specialist attention;
- the patient raises the incompleteness in a teaching environment.

The record itself places Professor Counihan and senior Neurology colleagues inside the treatment-policy discussion around delaying levodopa because of youth and dyskinesia risk.

This does not prove “pride” as a private motive.

It does establish an objective setting in which reopening the first paragraph would require acknowledging that a comparatively elementary part of the referring problem remained insufficiently worked through despite substantial specialist effort elsewhere.

The safer explanatory term is:

> **professional-coherence / commitment pressure.**

The hypothesis is not that the clinician was vain. It is that, by the time the patient restored the neglected part of the referral, the team already possessed a highly developed and professionally coherent neurological account. Reopening the feet would require the existing account to be expanded, qualified or partially reworked at the point when the admission was approaching closure.

That is a plausible mechanism to investigate. It is not a proved motive.

## 5. Reopening the feet did not require abandoning Parkinsonism or the treatment strategy

This is important.

The patient’s objection did not require Neurology to reject Parkinsonism, reject possible PRKN/PARK2, or immediately abandon a cautious approach to levodopa.

The available alternatives included:

- focused reassessment of the painful feet after the neurological discoveries;
- explicit mapping of fixed structural abnormalities versus dynamic toe/foot posturing;
- multidisciplinary review;
- a defined neurological question to Podiatry;
- preservation of the structural–dynamic issue as an active neurological question;
- a return criterion tied to treatment-state change;
- or later planned remapping while preserving the existing treatment policy.

Therefore the Protest did not create a binary choice between “keep the Parkinsonism plan” and “accept the feet.”

It created a more uncomfortable but narrower problem:

> **Can the admission legitimately be treated as complete if the problem that initiated the referral has still not been adequately integrated into the newly discovered neurological phenotype?**

## 6. The discharge synthesis is the answer to that problem

The discharge synthesis contains the necessary elements. It is not a record in which the feet disappeared.

It contains:

- severe very-young-onset Parkinsonism;
- a lower-limb phenomenon suggestive of dystonic posture — “NOT FOOT DROP”;
- structural foot disease;
- longstanding painful corns/callouses/burning;
- possible PRKN/PARK2;
- possible peripheral neuropathy;
- the patient’s historical distinction between internal leg tension and foot pain;
- the forward neurological treatment strategy.

The relevant forensic question is therefore not:

> **Why did nobody join the pieces?**

The synthesis itself joins them.

The question is:

> **Why does the synthesis arrange the joined pieces so that they point in this direction?**

Its directional construction is visible:

- Parkinsonism / PRKN / treatment strategy receive extensive explanatory development;
- the painful structural feet are made compatible with a separate or peripheral-neuropathic route;
- the relationship ends “Not clear”;
- Podiatry becomes the operational destination;
- the patient’s limited phenomenological distinction becomes “in his own mind – markedly divorced”;
- the patient’s contrary information state — that the feet remain unfinished — does not travel;
- “Plan/Recommendations: As per above” makes the resulting arrangement operational and portable.

The synthesis therefore solves the closure problem by making these two propositions coexist:

> **The neurological admission was sufficiently complete to close.**
>
> **The painful feet could remain unresolved because they occupied a separate / peripheral / Podiatry-compatible channel.**

That is the information architecture that requires explanation.

## 7. The record’s repeated behaviour

The longitudinal record repeatedly behaves in a way consistent with this founding architecture:

1. **Integrated information enters.**
2. **One explanatory pathway is developed more intensively than another.**
3. **The relationship between pathways remains thin or qualified.**
4. **Uncertainty acquires an operational destination.**
5. **The narrative arrangement becomes the plan.**
6. **Later information crossing the boundary is absorbed locally.**
7. **The founding arrangement is not globally reconstructed.**
8. **Later continuity is then available as evidence of longstanding understanding.**

This is why the case is better described as an **information-arrangement problem** than a simple missing-information problem.

The record does not generally delete inconvenient facts. It contains them, assigns them a status, gives them a destination and controls what they are allowed to mean.

## 8. The controlling reformulation

The case does not depend upon proving that Neurology behaved unreasonably by initially focusing on severe young-onset Parkinsonism. Nor does it depend upon proving a private motive such as pride.

The stronger proposition is:

> **The GP referred an integrated problem beginning with painful abnormal feet and gait and escalating into pathological neurological signs. Neurology understandably concentrated on the striking neurological syndrome and developed a sophisticated young-onset Parkinsonism/PRKN treatment architecture. After days of that work, the patient expressly restored the underweighted first paragraph of the referral by stating that the feet had not been properly dealt with and the work could not be complete. At that point the originating problem was squarely back in view. The later-finalised discharge synthesis contained all of the relevant elements but arranged them so that the neurological admission could be treated as complete while the painful feet remained separate, possibly neuropathic, “Not clear” and operationally Podiatry. The Protest that exposed the closure problem did not travel. Later information repeatedly crossed the resulting boundary without an identified global reconstruction.**

The shortest formulation is:

> **The patient did not discover a new problem at discharge. He restored the referrer’s original problem after days in which the record had overwhelmingly valued something else. The discharge synthesis then had to decide what that restored problem was allowed to mean.**

## 9. Evidential discipline

Keep these levels separate:

**Proved / directly observable from the record**

- the architecture of the GP referral;
- the extensive neurological work-up;
- the limited surviving direct foot examination relative to that work-up;
- the Parkinsonism / dystonic-looking gait / structural feet / PRKN material;
- the senior treatment-policy context;
- the patient’s direct account of restoring the feet before discharge;
- the contents and directional arrangement of the final synthesis;
- the omission of the Protest from that synthesis;
- the later boundary-crossing observations.

**Reasonable inference to investigate**

- asymmetric clinical weighting of the referral;
- accumulated diagnostic/therapeutic commitment by the time of the Protest;
- professional-coherence pressure when the patient restored the first paragraph;
- the possibility that the final synthesis functioned to reconcile that restored problem with an already-developed neurological closure state.

**Not presently proved**

- private pride, embarrassment or vanity;
- a deliberate plan to mislead;
- deliberate concealment as motive;
- that every foot symptom had one neurological mechanism;
- that reconsideration necessarily required levodopa.

The record should now be investigated for behaviour, not merely for isolated sentences:

> **What enters? What is amplified? What is compressed? What is separated? What becomes actionable? What is allowed to remain uncertain? What is given a destination? What contrary information travels? What later evidence is allowed to change the founding model?**
