# Defence Case

The consolidated repo must preserve the strongest defence, not merely the claimant formulation.

## Standard of care

- Parkinsonism was a serious and clinically compelling discovery; prioritising it may have been entirely reasonable.
- The foot problem was considered rather than ignored: the record expressly contemplated a separate/peripheral explanation.
- Reasonable specialists can differ on investigation and management. A different preferable course is not enough under Dunne.
- The patient-derived separation language may have reflected a genuine history rather than diagnostic anchoring.
- Referral to Podiatry may have been a reasonable response to a potentially mechanical problem.

## Protest proof

- The alleged bedside Protest is presently anchored in a patient-authored 2025 account of a 2017 encounter, not a contemporaneous 2017 clinical note.
- Eight-year retrospective recollection will be challenged on precision, wording, context and completeness.
- The alleged supervising consultant response and four trainees require independent proof if relied upon.

## Later evidence / hindsight

- Medication-state changes do not establish one mechanism; Parkinsonian motor state, dystonia, pain perception and mechanical loading can interact.
- Later Sinemet benefit does not establish that levodopa was indicated for the same foot complaint in 2017.
- PRKN confirmation/taxonomy does not prove that the 2017 diagnosis or management fell below standard.
- Podiatry evidence may affirm a genuine independent mechanical component rather than disprove the original differential.

## Documentary theory

- Similar wording across letters may reflect accurate longitudinal summarisation rather than prejudicial inheritance.
- A missing Protest from a letter does not prove it was clinically material or that its inclusion would have changed care.
- The claimant must prove actual downstream reliance, not textual similarity.

## Causation

- The underlying neurological disease was not caused by any alleged breach.
- Even if earlier reassessment was warranted, it must be proved that it probably would have produced materially earlier effective treatment.
- A delay without proof that earlier intervention would probably have avoided damage is insufficient.
- Mechanical and neurological contributors may make attribution of pain/function loss difficult.
- Damages must exclude inevitable progression and symptoms that competent care would not have prevented.

## Limitation

The defence will identify the earliest date on which the claimant knew or ought reasonably to have known the injury was significant and attributable in whole or part to the alleged act/omission. Knowledge of legal negligence is not required. Each alleged later breach requires separate analysis.

## Case-killing questions

1. Can an independent expert identify a Dunne breach at a defined date?
2. Can the Protest be proved to the civil standard, or is a viable breach present without it?
3. Can the information bridges between GP/Podiatry/neurology actually be proved?
4. Can an expert identify a probable earlier treatment pathway rather than a possible one?
5. Can avoidable differential injury be quantified?
6. Is each cause of action in time?

If any central answer is no, the pleading must narrow accordingly.
