# Discrete Breach Architecture

**Status:** EXPERT / COUNSEL MAP — 9 September 2026

This map is subject to [`TEMPORAL_FIREWALL_AND_DRIFT_AUDIT_2026_09_09.md`](TEMPORAL_FIREWALL_AND_DRIFT_AUDIT_2026_09_09.md). Each alleged breach must use **only the information demonstrably available at that threshold**. Final-synthesis knowledge and post-discharge treatment evidence must not be projected backwards.

## 1. One clinical history does not mean one negligence allegation

The repository has one longitudinal clinical history and one founding documentary centre, but it may contain several distinct alleged breaches occurring at different decision points.

Those alleged breaches should not be collapsed into one proposition such as:

> **Neurology failed to realise that the feet were Parkinsonian.**

The cleaner structure is:

> **one history → several information thresholds → several separate expert questions.**

Each alleged breach must be tested independently for the information available at that date, reasonable neurological standard at that date, what reasonable care required next, whether the actual response fell below that standard, and what difference the alternative response would probably have made.

## 2. Breach A — July 2017 bedside assessment / formulation

### Information state

The GP referred an integrated presentation involving painful abnormal feet, abnormal gait, impaired movement initiation and pathological lower-limb tone.

During the admission Neurology documented marked rigidity/bradykinesia, a strikingly abnormal gait, a lower-limb appearance **“somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture – NOT FOOT DROP”**, pes planus and hammertoes. The DAT request described the walk as **“almost dystonically unusual”** and suspected an autosomal-recessive Parkinsonian disorder.

The final discharge synthesis later contains more detailed PRKN/PARK2 reasoning. That final sophistication is an internal comparator, but it must **not** be assumed to have existed in completed form at every earlier bedside point.

### Alleged breach

> **On the information actually available while the patient remained available for examination, was the neurological assessment and reasoning directed to the feet–gait–tone relationship sufficient?**

This is an **assessment / formulation** allegation.

### Dependencies

This allegation does **not** depend on proving the Protest.

It requires expert movement-disorders evidence about what a reasonably competent neurologist should have examined, differentiated or recorded in July 2017 using the contemporaneous bedside information.

### Causation

If breach is found, the next question is what a proper July assessment would probably have demonstrated and whether management would probably have changed.

---

## 3. Breach B — July 2017 decision after express notice

### Information state

The patient's account is that, immediately before discharge, he expressly said in substance that the disabling foot problem had not been dealt with.

The patient was not objecting to the later PRKN-rich discharge synthesis, the wording **“Not clear”**, or a sophisticated documentary frame he had not seen. The asserted notice concerned the clinical work he had actually experienced during the admission.

The occurrence, wording and circumstances remain matters for ordinary evidential proof and corroboration.

### Alleged breach

If the Protest occurred substantially as described:

> **Did reasonable neurological practice, using the information then available and while the patient remained present, require the clinician to reopen, repeat, clarify or otherwise address the unresolved feet–gait–tone question before proceeding to closure?**

This is a **decision-after-notice** allegation.

### Dependencies

This allegation depends heavily on factual proof of the Protest.

It can exist even if the original assessment had been reasonable up to the moment of notice.

### Causation

The counterfactual begins at the point of notice: what reassessment, explanation, specialist input or handover would probably have followed, and what difference would that probably have made?

## 4. Breach C — final discharge communication / continuity record

### Information state

This threshold is later than the Protest threshold.

The final portable synthesis presents an unusually extensive specialist process: prolonged inpatient assessment, detailed neurological history and examination, imaging and laboratory work, consultant/team involvement, Complex Case Meeting material, senior neurological input, neurogenetic/PRKN reasoning, treatment strategy and a forward plan.

The exact timing and authorship of individual sophisticated elements remain to be reconstructed. What matters for this breach is the **final communication actually exported downstream**.

Inside that high-authority communication, the relationship of the painful feet is left **“Not clear.”**

If the Protest occurred, the portable synthesis does not preserve the asserted fact that before closure the patient had challenged whether the foot problem had actually been neurologically dealt with.

### Alleged breach

> **Did the final discharge communication fairly represent the provenance of the specialist uncertainty and the decision process by which the unresolved foot relationship became an operationally separate downstream state?**

The documentary proposition is narrower than dishonesty:

> **If the Protest occurred as described, its omission could allow challenged bedside incompleteness to travel downstream as apparently considered specialist uncertainty.**

### Dependencies

This allegation depends on proof of the Protest and on expert/legal evidence about what information was clinically material to continuity of care and reasonable discharge documentation.

It does **not** require proof of conscious concealment, bad faith or motive.

### Causation

Any causal case must identify actual downstream reliance or loss of a reasonable opportunity to reopen the issue. Omission alone does not establish later injury.

## 5. Breach D — discharge disposition / inter-specialty reintegration plan

### Information state

Podiatry was a genuine and pre-existing GP-origin pathway and was clinically appropriate for structural foot pathology.

The final discharge material describes the Podiatry task generically, including:

> **“review issues with feet reported by the patient.”**

The presently identified material does not show a neurological question to Podiatry about dystonia, tone, gait, loading or the structural–dynamic relationship, nor a defined mechanism by which Podiatry findings were to return and resolve Neurology's **“Not clear.”**

### Alleged breach

> **Even if Podiatry referral was appropriate, did reasonable neurological care require a more specific handoff, return mechanism or other method of integrating neurological and mechanical findings?**

This is a **care-coordination / disposition** allegation.

### Dependencies

This allegation does not necessarily depend on the Protest, although the Protest would make the need for a defined answer more acute if proved.

### Causation

The counterfactual is not “no Podiatry.” It is whether a more specific neurological question or feedback loop would probably have produced clinically material reintegration earlier.

## 6. Breach E — post-discharge 2017/2018 failure to reopen after new treatment-state evidence

### Why this is the independent backstop

This allegation is analytically and temporally distinct from the Protest and from whether July 2017 uncertainty was initially reasonable.

The evidence did **not** exist at the Protest threshold.

By September 2017 Professor Healy recorded a **dramatic improvement in motor symptoms** with low-dose procyclidine and rasagiline.

During the planned October 2017 procyclidine interruption, the contemporaneous patient account recorded:

> **“I am feeling the tension building up, my feet are restless and my walk is falling apart....but my eyesight is back to normal.”**

Then, in April 2018, the treating neurologist recorded that during the earlier procyclidine withdrawal:

> **“the first thing he noticed changing and changing rapidly was the return on the foot, of the outside left corn.”**

The same note records broader worsening and that he was more **“shifty”** and **“unrelaxed”** off procyclidine.

### What this evidence does and does not prove

It does **not** prove that procyclidine physically removed and regenerated a keratotic corn.

It does **not** prove that dystonia caused the fifth-MTPJ lesion.

It does **not** prove that one neurological mechanism explains all foot pain.

It creates a new clinical question:

> **What did the rapid treatment-state alteration of a focal foot phenomenon mean in a patient whose lower-limb motor state was neurologically abnormal and whose feet had previously been left “Not clear”?**

Possible mechanisms requiring clinical interpretation include altered loading, posture, dystonia/tone, gait, pain generation at an existing lesion, sensory processing, or a mixed mechanism.

### Alleged breach

> **Even if the July 2017 “Not clear” position was initially within reasonable practice, once the treating neurologist recorded that withdrawal of procyclidine was followed by rapid return of the focal outside-left-foot phenomenon, did reasonable neurological practice require that original relationship to be reopened and investigated? If not, why not?**

This is a **failure-to-update / failure-to-reconsider** allegation.

### Dependencies

This allegation barely depends on the Protest.

It therefore survives if the Protest cannot be proved, July 2017 **“Not clear”** is held initially reasonable, Podiatry referral is held appropriate, the Parkinsonism diagnosis is correct, and the fifth-MTPJ lesion is accepted as genuinely mechanical.

### Causation

If breach is found, the expert must identify what reassessment should then have occurred, what it would probably have shown, whether management would probably have changed, and what later harm would probably have been avoided or reduced.

## 7. The mechanical target makes the 2018 question concrete

Podiatry subsequently developed a stable focal mechanical object: recurrent left fifth-MTPJ HD/callus, repeated debridement/enucleation, direct offloading, clawed toes, pronating foot type and repeated insole/toe-prop management.

That establishes genuine focal mechanical pathology.

It does not establish that the forces producing or aggravating that pathology were neurologically independent.

The integration question is:

> **Was the structural lesion mechanically independent, neurologically influenced, or mixed?**

Neurology possessed dynamic treatment-state evidence. Podiatry possessed focal mechanical evidence. The expert question is whether reasonable care required those streams to be brought together.

## 8. Breach F — later years: possible threshold-specific failure to integrate accumulating evidence

Later evidence continued to cross the original boundary: recurrent focal Podiatry pathology; 2019 GP wording linking feet with increased tone and abnormal Parkinsonian gait; later OFF/dystonia considerations; 2023 movement-state and pain observations; later dopaminergic modulation of foot pain; and a mixed later model in which pain and motor phenomena could be partly separated.

The repository should **not** present this as an undifferentiated continuous breach. The record contains genuine later reconsideration, especially by 2023.

The proper question is:

> **At what later information threshold, if any, did reasonable neurological practice require a substantive revision of the working formulation that did not occur?**

Any such allegation needs a date, defined information state and defined expected response.

---

## 9. Breach G — 2023 separate contemporaneous safety / reassessment question

The 2023 episode remains analytically separate.

The neurological channel received severe pain, major walking restriction, high-dose pregabalin, prior falls-risk information and serious psychological-endurance language. The later clinic review directly observed stumbles and considered OFF/dystonic, neuropathic, Fabry and other possibilities.

The review also documented improved mood/pain and substantial genuine reassessment, which are important defence facts.

The separate question is:

> **Was the combined pain–mobility–falls–psychological safety state adequately recognised, assessed and managed as a contemporaneous risk object?**

It should not be used to prove the 2017 allegations by analogy.

---

## 10. Why the routes do not stand or fall together

Failure to prove the Protest defeats or weakens Breach B and Breach C but does not determine Breach A, D or E. A finding that July 2017 **“Not clear”** was initially reasonable does not determine whether new post-discharge treatment-state evidence required reconsideration. A finding that Podiatry was appropriate does not determine whether the handoff was sufficiently integrative. A correct Parkinsonism diagnosis does not determine whether the feet–motor relationship was adequately investigated. Genuine 2023 reconsideration does not retrospectively answer what should have happened in 2018. A finding of breach at any stage does not establish damages causation from that stage.

## 11. The master expert exercise

For **each** alleged breach, the instructed expert should identify:

| Question | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Earliest possible breach date | The first date on which reasonable practice arguably required a different response |
| Information then available | Only findings, observations, referrals and treatment-state evidence actually available at that point |
| Later information excluded | Which later synthesis, treatment, genetic or review evidence must not be imported backwards |
| Standard required | What a reasonably competent movement-disorders neurologist should ordinarily have done |
| Expected action | Examination, reformulation, treatment trial, handoff, multidisciplinary review, follow-up or other step |
| Breach opinion | Whether failure to take that step fell below reasonable neurological practice |
| Counterfactual | What would probably have happened if the step had been taken |
| Causation | Which later harms would probably have been avoided or reduced, if any |
| Confidence / limitations | What cannot now be determined from surviving documentation |

The expert should not be required to accept the repository's narrative wholesale.

The task is to identify **discrete clinical decisions, each using only the information reasonably available at that time.**

## 12. The single hardest independent question

> **Even if the July 2017 conclusion that the relationship between the foot problem and the neurological disorder was “Not clear” was initially within reasonable practice, once the treating neurologist recorded that withdrawal of procyclidine was followed by rapid return of the focal outside-left-foot phenomenon, did reasonable neurological practice require that original relationship to be reopened and investigated? If not, why not?**

The importance of **“If not, why not?”** is that the evidence need not prove neurological causation before reconsideration becomes reasonable.

## 13. Repository hierarchy after the temporal correction

### Layer 1 — Narrative centre

> **Integrated referral → inpatient bedside work → Protest, if proved → professional closure → final synthesis/allocation → later consequences.**

### Layer 2 — Discrete breach architecture

> **A. bedside assessment → B. decision after notice → C. final discharge communication → D. inter-specialty handoff → E. post-discharge 2017/2018 failure to update → F. later threshold-specific updating → G. separate 2023 safety question.**

### Layer 3 — Causation

Each breach has its own counterfactual. No single breach automatically carries all later harm.

### Layer 4 — Documentary genealogy and evidential access

Only after the clinical thresholds are reconstructed should the repository examine how the final information state travelled, how later letters inherited or independently tested propositions, and what potentially corroborative evidence remains unavailable or untested.

---

## 14. Shortest formulation

> **One clinical history. Several decision points. Several possible breaches. Keep each information state in its own time. The Protest strengthens the 2017 case if proved; April 2018 supplies an independent clinical backstop; causation must be proved separately from each point.**
