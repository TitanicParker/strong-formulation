# Evidential Access and Procedural Opacity

**Status:** LIVE ANALYTICAL TRACK — 8 September 2026  
**Purpose:** Preserve the distinction between absence of corroboration and unresolved access to the institutional information capable of locating, testing or explaining corroboration.  
**Boundary:** This track does not prove the Protest, prove negligence, prove concealment, or prove intentional obstruction. It records an evidential-access problem and the observable procedural pattern that has developed around it.

---

## 1. Why this track exists

The central clinical/documentary case remains independent of GDPR. The patient says that shortly before discharge he directly told the treating neurologist that the feet had not been properly dealt with and that the neurological work could not therefore be complete. The durable synthesis does not record that interaction.

A separate question now arises:

> **Why is independent corroboration not presently available, who controlled the information capable of locating or testing it, should a trace of that information exist, and what significance—if any—can properly be attached to its non-production?**

The important discipline is that **present non-production is not automatically the same thing as proved non-existence**.

Potential corroboration has been identified, including the patient's account that four student doctors were present behind the curtain during the interaction. Their presence does not establish what any student heard, saw, remembered or understood. They remain potential contemporaneous witnesses unless and until their evidence is actually obtained.

The repository must therefore distinguish:

> **corroboration not presently produced**
>
> from
>
> **corroboration shown not to exist after the relevant institutional sources and potential witnesses have been properly identified and tested.**

Those are materially different evidential states.

---

## 2. The controlling formulation

The present pattern is not described as proved concealment or intentional obstruction.

The narrower and more defensible formulation is:

> **Repeated failure to convert clearly articulated data-subject rights into clear, correctly characterised, timely and operationally effective controller decisions, producing cumulative procedural opacity and preserving material factual uncertainty.**

The recurring sequence is:

> **right exercised → request identity or scope becomes unclear → delay or reframing → response does not cleanly map onto the right exercised → operational consequence remains uncertain → data subject seeks clarification → procedural closure remains incomplete.**

The practical effect is informational. Each unresolved procedural step preserves factual uncertainty about the evidential field.

---

## 3. Procedural opacity

For this project, **procedural opacity** means:

> **A condition in which the administration of an information right does not generate a sufficiently clear and traceable record of the request received, its characterisation, the search or assessment undertaken, the decision reached, and the operational consequences of that decision.**

This is an analytical description of the observable information state. It is not an allegation of purpose.

Where the pattern recurs across related requests, the result is a **cumulative informational consequence**:

> **uncertainties that the statutory processes were capable of resolving remain preserved, limiting the data subject's ability to reconstruct the underlying factual record.**

---

## 4. The seven controller-side audit questions

The present GUH procedural record is best treated as seven audit questions rather than seven allegations.

### Q1 — Which June Article 16 request did the 14 July acknowledgment address?

There were two separately identifiable Article 16 exercises: one dated **12 June 2025** concerning clinical data and one dated **15 June 2025** concerning QPS material. Both were separately followed up on 7 July. The 14 July acknowledgment referred only to **"your request for rectification"** and did not identify date, subject matter or reference.

The audit question is:

> **Was the acknowledgment intended to cover the 12 June request, the 15 June request, both, or neither in a properly identified administrative sense?**

### Q2 — What happened to the Article 12(3) timetable?

The June requests preceded substantive decisions dated **6 September 2025**. The 14 July correspondence did not, on the presently supplied material, identify itself as an extension notice. The clinical-data decision later apologised for delay.

The audit question is:

> **Does the controller hold evidence that any extension or other timetable decision was validly communicated within the relevant period?**

The legal characterisation belongs to the regulator. The chronology is a separate documentary question.

### Q3 — What Article 16 request did GUH understand itself to be deciding?

The 6 September clinical-data decision described the exercise as a request **"for the erasure of a record"** while also reproducing requested remedies to **rectify** and **annotate** records.

The audit question is:

> **Did GUH assess the Article 16 rectification/completion request actually made, or did it process the matter through an erasure frame?**

This question can be answered without deciding whether the requested rectification should ultimately succeed.

### Q4 — What operational treatment was given to the Article 18 request of 4 August 2025?

Article 18 was expressly invoked against identified disputed data and the anticipated SIMT processing. On the presently supplied record, no identified controller communication states whether restriction was accepted, refused, technically implemented, administratively flagged or otherwise operationalised.

The audit questions are:

> **Who assessed the Article 18 request? What decision was reached? What systems, users or review processes were told about it? What restriction, if any, was implemented? What processing was treated as permitted thereafter?**

This is especially important because later governance material continued to rely on the contemporaneous clinical record.

### Q5 — Did the 5 August Article 15 decision mean withholding or non-holding?

The decision reportedly said that access to **"some"** records was being refused and then stated that HSE **"does not hold any personal data relating to your request"**, followed by category-specific non-holding statements.

Those are different states:

- **identified but withheld**; and
- **not found / not held**.

The audit question is:

> **Which state applied to each requested category, and if the answer was non-holding, what systems, departments, repositories, custodians and search parameters were used?**

This is the gateway question for the corroboration problem because the data subject cannot independently prove the contents of repositories to which access is being sought.

### Q6 — Why did a July 2017 request receive a response referring to 10–11 July 2015?

The discrepancy may be clerical. It may reflect a description error while the correct 2017 material was searched. Or it may reveal a retrieval problem.

The repository should not choose among those possibilities without evidence.

The audit question is:

> **What date range and admission identifiers were actually searched, and what records were retrieved?**

This matters because confidence in a non-holding conclusion depends upon showing that the correct event was searched.

### Q7 — What happened to the renewed Article 16/18 exercise of 14 January 2026?

The renewed exercise was deliberately explicit: it identified Article 16, maintained Article 18, invoked Article 12(3), and stated that it was not a complaint, appeal or clinical request. The data subject later clarified that a QPS governance response was not understood to be the GDPR determination and that the DPO determination was still awaited.

The audit question is:

> **Where is the controller's GDPR determination, when was it made, and when was it communicated?**

If none exists, that absence is procedurally different from a substantive refusal.

---

## 5. What the seven questions have in common

Individually, one or more questions may have innocent explanations. The analytical force lies in the recurrence of the same information-state problem across **Articles 15, 16 and 18**.

The common features are:

1. **loss of identity between request and response**;
2. **delay without a clearly traceable procedural state**;
3. **reframing or narrowing of the right or subject matter**;
4. **formal invocation without visible operational effect**;
5. **responses that do not cleanly disclose whether data were held, withheld, not found or not searched**;
6. **discrepancies that prevent reconstruction of the search process**; and
7. **clarification by the data subject without clean procedural closure.**

The resulting questions are not merely administrative:

- Which request was processed?
- What was searched?
- What was actually held?
- What was withheld?
- What date range was searched?
- Was restriction implemented?
- What data did SIMT actually use?
- Was the renewed request ever determined?

Those are factual reconstruction questions.

## 6. The evidential access chain

Every potentially corroborative source should now be mapped through an **Evidential Access Chain**:

> **potential source → institutional holder/controller → request identifying source/category → acknowledgment/logging → search/retrieval process → controller decision → clarification/challenge → regulatory review → final ascertainable evidential state.**

The final state should use precise categories:

- **held and supplied**;
- **held but withheld / partially supplied**;
- **not held**;
- **no longer held**;
- **not found after identified search**;
- **search scope unclear**;
- **not answered / procedurally unresolved**; or
- **witness identified but testimony not yet obtained**.

The repository must not collapse these states into the single phrase **"no corroboration."**

---

## 7. Application to the student-witness issue

The student's evidential chain currently begins with the patient's direct account that four student doctors were present.

That does not prove independent corroboration. It identifies a possible route to it.

The relevant questions are therefore:

- what institutional systems could identify students attached to the ward, team, teaching round or relevant encounter;
- whether those systems were searched;
- which controller held which class of information;
- whether the correct July 2017 date and admission were used;
- whether records once existed but are no longer held;
- whether identifiable witnesses can now be approached through proper legal or professional channels; and
- what explanation exists if the relevant information cannot now be produced.

The correct present formulation is:

> **Potential independent witnesses have been identified in the patient's account, but the repository does not yet establish that the institutional information capable of identifying or testing them has been exhaustively located, searched or resolved.**

## 8. The two-controller information environment

The historical event may have generated information across more than one institutional environment.

The present project material records separate controller tracks involving:

- **HSE / University Hospital Galway**, including clinical records, DPO handling, QPS and SIMT; and
- **University of Galway**, potentially including student/placement/teaching information.

The important proposition is not that either controller necessarily holds the missing corroboration.

It is:

> **One historical event may have produced or depended upon information distributed across two controllers, while no single controller response presently supplies a complete account of the evidential field.**

The project material further records that the GUH complaint **C-25-7-2074** remains a separate DPC track and that, on **8 September 2026**, the University complaint **DPC0826812075** was reported as transferred to CAERU for review and assessment.

These procedural facts do not establish that either complaint will be upheld.

## 9. Why the absence of corroboration cannot yet be treated as a simple weakness

A superficial formulation would be:

> **The patient says four students were present but cannot produce them.**

The fuller evidential state is:

> **The patient identified a potentially corroborative institutional source and exercised statutory rights in an attempt to determine what information existed and how the event could be reconstructed. The resulting controller-side record has itself generated unresolved questions about request identity, search scope, holding, withholding, date range, restriction and procedural determination.**

Accordingly:

> **The continuing absence of corroborative material cannot yet be treated as a simple evidential vacuum attributable to the claimant.**

This does not reverse the burden of proof. It preserves the correct classification of the present uncertainty.

---

## 10. Stages of evidential absence

The repository should distinguish at least four stages:

### Stage A — presently unproduced

The evidence is not in the claimant's possession.

### Stage B — institutionally unresolved

The evidence capable of determining existence, identity or location may be controlled by another holder and has not yet been clearly resolved.

### Stage C — properly tested but unavailable

Relevant repositories, custodians, date ranges and potential witnesses have been identified and tested, but the material still cannot be produced.

### Stage D — legally significant non-production

Only after the prior stages are established should counsel consider what evidential or procedural inference, if any, can properly arise from non-production.

The repository should not jump from Stage A directly to Stage D.

## 11. Relationship to the 2025 retrospective review

This track has a second function beyond locating witnesses.

If the historical record was expressly disputed before or during later governance processing, the question becomes:

> **Did the later review independently reconstruct the disputed historical event and its evidential field, or did it treat the surviving record as the control against which the challenge to that record was judged?**

The answer depends on evidence about:

- source lists and evidence matrices;
- drafts and review inputs;
- clinician or witness evidence;
- what documents were actually before SIMT;
- whether accuracy disputes or restriction requests travelled with the data; and
- whether the founding synthesis was treated as a settled starting state.

This is why the Article 15 provenance request concerning PAR/SIMT remains important.

---

## 12. What must not be inferred

Do not state, without further evidence, that:

- GUH or the University concealed witnesses or records;
- either controller intentionally obstructed a negligence claim;
- the four students would corroborate the patient's account;
- requested records necessarily exist because a category was requested;
- records necessarily never existed because they are not presently supplied;
- the 2015/2017 discrepancy proves the wrong year was searched;
- Article 18 automatically prohibited every subsequent operation;
- a DPC complaint being assessed means a GDPR breach has been established; or
- procedural opacity itself proves the underlying clinical case.

Use instead:

> **The observable effect of the present controller-side handling has been continued uncertainty about information capable of reconstructing the historical evidential field.**

---

## 13. Consequence for negligence formulation

A GPT, lawyer or expert using the repository should not choose the "strongest negligence theory" merely by preferring whichever theory is easiest to prove from the surviving institutional material.

For any contested proposition, ask:

1. what is established now;
2. what is supported only as inference;
3. what depends on institutionally controlled information;
4. what has been specifically requested;
5. what controller-side answer is still missing or unclear; and
6. what additional evidence would discriminate between competing factual formulations.

The rule is:

> **Institutionally unresolved evidence must remain unresolved; it must not be silently converted into evidence against the claimant's account.**

---

## 14. Counsel / regulator questions

The most useful higher-order question is:

> **How did repeated, explicit exercises of Articles 15, 16 and 18 come to produce a record in which it remains unclear, across multiple requests, which right or request was being processed, what decision was reached, what information was searched or held, what was withheld, and what operational effect—if any—was given to the exercise of the right?**

For corroboration specifically:

> **What potentially corroborative evidence should reasonably exist or be identifiable; who held or controlled the systems capable of locating it; what attempts were made to obtain it; what searches or inquiries were actually performed; what explanation exists for non-production; and only then what inference, if any, can properly arise from its absence?**

---

## 15. Repository propositions

The clinical/documentary proposition remains primary:

> **The patient restored the originating problem before closure; the discharge synthesis arranged the known facts into a portable state that did not preserve that Protest.**

The separate evidential-access proposition is:

> **The absence of independent corroboration is not yet a simple proved absence. Some of the information capable of identifying, locating or testing corroboration lies or lay within institutional information environments that the claimant has specifically attempted to interrogate. Those processes have generated unresolved controller-side questions about what was processed, searched, held, withheld, restricted and determined.**

And the procedural formulation is:

> **Procedural opacity has cumulative informational consequences: it preserves uncertainty precisely where properly differentiated and traceable administration could have reduced it.**
