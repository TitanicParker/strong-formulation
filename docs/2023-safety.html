2023: a convergence crisis and safety-integration question.

This is not the 2017 liability theory repeated. It is a distinct contemporaneous question about whether pain, mobility, instability, medication burden and psychological endurance were recognised and managed as one clinical object.

The convergence

The neurological channel received an urgent pain crisis containing “death wish” and “no quality of life” language, reviewed a patient with major recent walking restriction and established Parkinsonian gait disorder, and directly observed stumbles during gait examination. Falls risk was already present in the longitudinal record.

The gait examination was too nonspecific to establish that the focal fifth-MTPJ mechanics had been investigated, but it was specific enough to produce a positive safety finding: observed instability.

What was analysed

The consultation contains substantial reasoning about OFF state, levodopa/Stalevo effect, pain modulation, possible dystonia, peripheral neuropathy, Fabry disease, pregabalin, alcohol, cannabis and repeat DATscan.

What is not presently identified

The patient-facing consultation presently identifies no specific falls-focused assessment, Parkinson's-specific balance/physiotherapy intervention, assistive-device assessment, explicit safety plan or monitoring criterion tied to further stumbling or falling.

This is a record-audit finding, not itself a breach conclusion.

The expert question

In a patient with established Parkinsonian gait disorder, prior balance/falls concern, severe current mobility restriction, high-dose pregabalin exposure and directly observed stumbles during a recent urgent pain crisis, what falls-risk assessment and intervention would ordinarily be expected?

The 2025 continuation

The 10 March 2025 Neurology letter records “I won’t live with it” as language used to express the intolerability of the foot-pain situation. A separate 18 March GP-facing briefing links fear of falling with the thought that, if a fall occurred, the patient might not want to get back up. These statements should not be converted automatically into suicidal intent, but they do show a recurring documentary connection between pain, mobility, falling and psychological endurance.

Keep the hierarchy clean: 2017 asks whether an identified incompleteness was closed rather than reopened and then omitted from the portable decision history. 2023 asks whether a later whole-risk state was adequately integrated and managed in its own right.
