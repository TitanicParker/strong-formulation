# Expert Question Register

## Movement-disorders / neurology expert

1. On the information available at initial assessment, what differential diagnoses reasonably arose for the painful foot/gait/tone presentation alongside Parkinsonism?
2. Was the recorded dystonic-looking posture clinically sufficient to require any particular examination, investigation, treatment trial, referral or follow-up?
3. Given the founding clinician's recorded uncertainty as to whether the feet were separate/peripheral, what would ordinary specialist practice require before discharge?
4. Assume the patient's account of the pre-closure Protest is true. Would an ordinarily competent neurologist have re-examined, investigated, referred, documented, allocated follow-up responsibility, or arranged review? Why?
5. If the Protest is not proved, does the contemporaneous record alone establish any departure from ordinary care?
6. Was the discharge/handover formulation adequate to communicate unresolved uncertainty and responsibility to subsequent clinicians?
7. What clinical significance, if any, should be attached to the October 2017 procyclidine-withdrawal report?
8. What significance, if any, should be attached to the 2018 rapid foot return in treatment context?
9. Do the Podiatry findings support an independent mechanical condition, a mixed condition, a Parkinsonian/dystonic mechanism, or no reliable conclusion without examination?
10. At what point, if any, did later evidence make reassessment of the inherited foot formulation mandatory under ordinary specialist care?
11. What significance should be attached to 2023 OFF-related pain/shuffling and severe bilateral burning pain that improved with better Parkinson's control?
12. What significance should be attached to 2025 improvement when Sinemet takes effect and reported profound relief with controlled-release medication?
13. Does later PRKN information change the interpretation of earlier clinical decisions? Separate what was knowable then from hindsight.
14. Identify each specific breach, date, actor and competent alternative. Do not answer in general terms such as 'anchoring' unless tied to a clinical act/omission.
15. For each breach, state on the balance of probabilities what would have happened under competent care and when.
16. Identify what pain, disability or treatment burden would probably have been avoided.

## Additional experts potentially required

- Podiatry/orthopaedic foot expert: mechanical findings, fifth-MTPJ loading, structural contribution and counterfactual treatment.
- Clinical genetics expert: PRKN significance and limits of retrospective inference.
- Psychiatry/psychology expert: recognised psychiatric injury, causation, prognosis and separation from ordinary distress.
- GP expert only if a distinct primary-care breach is alleged.
- Records/health-informatics evidence only if metadata, authorship, amendment, routing or actual documentary reliance becomes contested.
