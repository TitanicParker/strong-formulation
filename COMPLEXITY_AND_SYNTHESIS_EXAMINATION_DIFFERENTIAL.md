# Complexity and the Synthesis–Examination Differential

**Status:** CONTROLLING EXPERT / DOCUMENTARY ANALYSIS — 9 September 2026

This file preserves a specific internal comparator within the July 2017 record. It is intended to answer, with evidential discipline, any broad contention that the unresolved neurological–foot relationship is adequately explained simply by the complexity of the presentation.

It is subject to the temporal firewall in [`TEMPORAL_FIREWALL_AND_DRIFT_AUDIT_2026_09_09.md`](TEMPORAL_FIREWALL_AND_DRIFT_AUDIT_2026_09_09.md): **the final synthesis is a later evidential state and must not be projected backwards into the patient's pre-discharge Protest or assumed to have existed in completed form before patient departure.**

It does **not** assert that the case was clinically simple, that PRKN/PARK2 was wrongly entertained, or that negligence is proved. It identifies a disparity inside the hospital's own record that requires expert explanation.

## 1. Complexity is real, but the final discharge synthesis supplies its own comparator

The July 2017 presentation was complex. Complexity can legitimately produce uncertainty.

But the final discharge synthesis demonstrates that the department was capable of constructing a sophisticated neurological phenotype from the admission material.

The final product selected and combined features such as very young onset, akinetic-rigid Parkinsonism, symmetry, family-history pattern, cognitive preservation, treatment considerations and recessive inheritance reasoning into a serious PRKN/PARK2 hypothesis.

The exact timing and authorship of each element of that construction remain to be reconstructed. The comparator does **not** require an assumption that the completed PRKN formulation existed at the bedside or before the Protest.

It asks instead:

> **How did the final service product reach high-resolution PRKN/PARK2 construction while the surviving record of the period when the patient was available for examination shows much lower-resolution testing of the recognised dystonic lower limb and painful abnormal foot?**

The defence cannot be tested only against an abstract proposition that the patient was complex. It can be tested against what the same department ultimately demonstrated itself capable of doing with the same presentation.

## 2. The synthesis–examination differential

The critical asymmetry is:

> **high-resolution final phenotype construction versus low-resolution documented bedside testing of the disputed lower-limb/foot relationship.**

The discharge synthesis appears to be more than a transcript of a single bedside encounter. It is a later clinical synthesis assembled from observation, investigation, discussion and reflection. That is ordinary medical practice.

But it means at least three things must be separated:

> **what was actually tested while the patient was available at the bedside;**

> **what was known at the pre-discharge decision / Protest threshold;**

and

> **what the department ultimately inferred and assembled in the final portable synthesis.**

The record appears capable of supporting the final retrospective inference:

> **young onset + rigidity + bradykinesia + symmetry + family-history pattern + preserved cognition → PRKN/PARK2 plausibility.**

What is not equally visible is whether that developing or final hypothesis generated a return journey to the patient before departure:

> **If PRKN/PARK2 was seriously entertained while the patient remained available, what did that hypothesis require the clinician to ask again about dystonia, lower-limb posture, toe posture, painful abnormal feet, gait, loading and the relationship between them?**

If the detailed PRKN construction arose only later in the synthesis process, a different but equally important question follows:

> **Did the service acquire a level of phenotypic insight after the bedside opportunity had closed that would, if available earlier, have warranted return examination or a defined process for later reintegration?**

Thus:

> **The document may be phenotypically more complete on paper than the surviving examination was in real time.**

Or:

> **The synthesis may have outrun the examination.**

## 3. Why this weakens a generic complexity defence

A generic complexity defence would say that the presentation was unusually difficult and uncertainty about the feet was therefore unsurprising.

The internal record permits a more discriminating question:

> **Why did the final neurological phenotype become sufficiently interpretable to support sophisticated PRKN/PARK2 reasoning while the surviving bedside record does not demonstrate equivalent interrogation of the recognised dystonic-looking lower limb and painful abnormal foot?**

That converts the issue from mere difficulty into a question about **selectivity and timing of inquiry**.

The safe proposition is:

> **The sophistication of the final neurological synthesis makes the relative absence of documented interrogation of the disputed lower-limb/foot relationship more, not less, significant.**

This is not a claim that every element of a complex phenotype must be solved before discharge. It is a question about whether the unresolved feature that sat directly beside the suspected movement disorder received a level of phenotypic inquiry proportionate to its significance while the patient remained available, or was placed within a process capable of resolving it afterwards.

## 4. The left foot outside the eventual strong frame

The eventual neurological frame was not weak.

It became strong enough to organise severe young-onset Parkinsonism, dystonic-looking gait, recessive inheritance reasoning, PRKN/PARK2 and treatment strategy.

The painful left foot was not absent from the record. It was visible, structurally abnormal and part of the presenting problem. Yet its relationship to the recognised lower-limb motor abnormality ultimately remained **“Not clear”** and became operationally capable of being treated as separate.

The human formulation is:

> **The left foot was caught outside a very strong frame.**

That is a retrospective description of the final and longitudinal architecture. It is **not** a claim that the patient perceived or challenged a fully formed sophisticated frame at the moment of Protest.

The more formal formulation is:

> **A clinically important part of the phenotype may have been left outside an explanatory structure that ultimately became strong enough to organise almost everything that followed.**

## 5. Professional closure before clinical completion

The alleged founding problem can be stated conditionally as:

> **professional closure before clinical completion of the neurological–foot phenotype.**

Complex cases do not have to be fully solved before discharge. But if an important relationship remains unresolved, reasonable care may require that the relationship remain clinically open, be expressly investigated, or be transferred through a defined process capable of resolving it.

If the patient's account of the Protest is proved, the issue becomes sharper. The patient did not challenge PRKN/PARK2 or the later final synthesis. He simply identified before discharge that the feet had not been dealt with while he remained available for further examination.

The controlling conditional formulation is:

> **The service may have completed the professional act of discharge before completing the clinical act of integration.**

The temporal asymmetry is:

> **the patient left carrying the unresolved phenotype; the institution retained the capacity to continue constructing the record.**

The earlier shorthand **“the record left with the problem already partitioned”** should no longer be used where it implies that the final partitioned synthesis necessarily existed simultaneously with patient departure.

## 6. The expert question that complexity cannot answer by itself

The strongest neutral expert question is:

> **Given the level of phenotypic sophistication ultimately demonstrated in the discharge synthesis, including serious PRKN/PARK2 reasoning, was the documented assessment of the dystonic lower-limb/foot relationship while the patient remained available proportionate to the clinical significance of that feature? If not, what further examination, synthesis or follow-up would reasonable neurological practice have required?**

A sharper form is:

> **How did the case become sufficiently intelligible to construct PRKN/PARK2 without the surviving record demonstrating an equivalently deliberate interrogation of the abnormal and dystonic-looking foot/lower-limb phenotype?**

If the Protest is proved:

> **Once the patient said that the feet had not been dealt with before discharge, what did reasonable practice require at that decision-live threshold, using only the information actually available then?**

The purpose is to compare two evidential states without collapsing them:

> **what the final synthesis ultimately knew** versus **what the surviving bedside record shows was actually tested while the patient was present.**
