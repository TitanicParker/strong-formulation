Counsel entry point

One mistake. One Protest. One decision. One omission.

The central 2017 case should be pleaded and tested in its simplest form first. The later evidential genealogy explains consequences and durability. The 2023 safety episode is a separate later question and should not complicate the founding allegation.

Primary 2017 theory

One compact causal sequence

Mistake: the GP sent an integrated feet–gait–tone problem into Neurology. Severe Parkinsonism was extensively developed, but the painful abnormal feet through which the referral began were not equivalently reconstructed after the neurological discoveries.

Protest: before discharge the patient directly identified that incompleteness: the feet had not been properly dealt with and Neurology could not therefore be finished.

Decision: the clinician answered Podiatry and proceeded to closure. Source-level review now shows Podiatry was already an active GP-origin pathway, while the surviving corpus identifies no distinct post-Protest neurological question or return mechanism added to it.

Omission: the final synthesis made the closure architecture portable but did not carry the Protest that had challenged the adequacy of closure.

Consequences: later readers inherited that information state; subsequent mechanical and neurological truths developed in parallel; later evidence repeatedly tested the durability of the founding allocation.

Before the Protest, the problem can be described as incomplete construction. After the Protest, closure becomes a decision made with the incompleteness expressly raised.

What must be proved first

  1. Referral object: the GP sent one integrated feet/gait/tone presentation.
  2. Investigative asymmetry: the Parkinsonian syndrome received substantially more development than the painful abnormal feet.
  3. Protest: before closure the patient expressly challenged completeness.
  4. Decision: the response was Podiatry/closure rather than identified neurological reopening.
  5. Portable omission: the Protest does not appear in the final synthesis that later readers inherit.

Only after those propositions are established should the later longitudinal evidence be used to show consequence, persistence or missed reconstruction.

What the case does not need at the outset

It does not require proof that Parkinson's was wrongly diagnosed, that every foot lesion was dystonic, that levodopa had to be started in July 2017, that later clinicians never reconsidered the feet, or that any private motive is proved.

The central allegation is incompleteness identified while the decision was live, followed by closure and omission.

Why the synthesis matters

The synthesis contains the major facts. Its significance is directional: Parkinsonism/PRKN/treatment receive extensive explanatory force; the feet are made compatible with separate/peripheral-neuropathy/Podiatry territory ending “Not clear”; “markedly divorced” travels; the Protest does not.

The issue is not that the document failed to join the facts. The synthesis is the founding join. The question is why it allowed the admission to close while the restored feet problem remained unresolved.

Later evidence: consequence and test

Procyclidine withdrawal changed feet, gait and tension. The April 2018 note records rapid return of an outside-left-foot phenomenon. Podiatry repeatedly documented and mechanically offloaded the fifth-MTPJ site. Primary care later regenerated an integrated feet–tone–gait formulation. Later Neurology genuinely reopened the current problem and developed a mixed treatment-state-sensitive model.

Use these facts to test the durability and consequences of the founding decision, not to make the first sentence of the case more complicated.

Continuity question

Later reopening occurred. The narrower genealogical question is whether the original foot inquiry was ever historically reconstructed using the accumulated mechanical and neurological evidence.

The issue was reopened. The founding genealogy was not yet shown to have been reconstructed.

Separate 2023 safety question

The 2023 episode should be analysed independently. An urgent pain crisis contained serious endurance language; the patient had major walking restriction; falls risk already existed in the neurological record; and the clinic directly observed stumbles.

The separate expert question is whether the combined pain–mobility–falls–psychological safety state was recognised and managed as one contemporaneous risk object, and what response would ordinarily be expected after observed instability in that context.

Do not use this later safety question as a substitute for proving the 2017 allegation.

2025 chronology discipline

The 10 March 2025 Neurology letter records “I won’t live with it” as the patient's way of expressing the intolerability of the foot-pain situation. The separate 18 March GP-facing briefing links fear of falling with the thought that, if a fall occurred, he might not want to get back up.

Do not collapse those phrases into proof of suicidal intent or attribute the GP briefing to Neurology without proof of receipt.

Expert tracks

Track 1 — 2017: Was the referred feet–gait–tone problem sufficiently investigated and integrated before closure once the patient expressly challenged completeness?

Track 2 — causation/consequence: What difference, if any, would proper reopening/handover/follow-up have made?

Track 3 — 2023: What falls/safety assessment or intervention would ordinarily be expected in the later convergence crisis?

Keep the tracks separate so one weak proposition cannot destabilise the others.

Central evidential consequence

The shortest case is not eight years long. It occurs at the end of the July 2017 admission: an incompletely constructed part of the referred problem is expressly identified by the patient; the clinician chooses closure rather than identified reopening; the final synthesis makes that choice portable and omits the event that challenged it.

One mistake. One Protest. One decision. One omission. Then the consequences.
