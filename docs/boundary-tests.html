Boundary crossings — and the question of integrative revision

Later treatment-state observations do more than test the founding allocation retrospectively. At some point they may create a new clinical decision threshold. April 2018 remains the principal independent backstop. The further longitudinal question is whether successive observations were used locally without equivalent global revision of the neurological–foot relationship.

2017 — Dystonic lower-limb phenotype. Neurology documents a strikingly abnormal gait and lower-limb appearance suggestive of dystonic posture; the DAT request calls the walk almost dystonically unusual while autosomal-recessive Parkinsonism is suspected. The sign enters the neurological phenotype, but its relationship with the painful structural foot is not clearly reconstructed.
2017 — Procyclidine response. Professor Healy records dramatic motor improvement with low-dose procyclidine and rasagiline. This establishes pharmacological modifiability of a substantial motor component.
October 2017 — planned withdrawal. Contemporaneous evidence records deterioration of tension, feet and gait together during withdrawal: “tension building up, my feet are restless and my walk is falling apart.” E009
April 2018 — Neurology records rapid return. The treating neurologist records that the first thing changing, and changing rapidly, during the earlier withdrawal was the return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon, together with broader worsening and being more “shifty” and “unrelaxed.” This is not proof of mechanism. It is new neurological treatment-state evidence bearing on a relationship previously left “Not clear.”
2017–2019 — fifth MTPJ. Podiatry repeatedly treats and mechanically offloads the recurrent left fifth-MTPJ site, with clawed toes and later pronating foot type. The structural stream identifies a stable mechanical target against which the treatment-state observation could potentially have been investigated.
2019 — GP reconnection. Feet issues are recorded in relation to increased tone and abnormal gait relating to Parkinson's disease. E010
2023 — severe bilateral pain. An urgent GP referral records burning in both feet/lower legs and recalls an earlier pain episode settling with better Parkinson's control. E014
2023 — OFF-related. Movement Disorders documentation states leg pain and shuffling appear OFF-related. Treatment changes accordingly. E013
2024 — pain and motor components separate. Pregabalin removes much of the pain while activity-dependent toe curling persists. The observation can separate components without proving the systems are independent.
2025 — Sinemet state. Neurology records that the feet feel better once Sinemet acts and acknowledges that further Sinemet would probably produce further pain relief, while also offering non-exclusive pain-modulation explanations. E015
2025 — profound relief. The complaint response accepts that Sinemet CR provided profound current relief. E016

The independent 2018 question

Even if the July 2017 conclusion that the relationship between the foot problem and the neurological disorder was “Not clear” was initially within reasonable practice, once the treating neurologist recorded that withdrawal of procyclidine was followed by rapid return of the focal outside-left-foot phenomenon, did reasonable neurological practice require that original relationship to be reopened and investigated? If not, why not?

The observation need not prove neurological causation before it becomes clinically relevant. The question is whether it made continued unexamined separation unsafe as a working formulation.

Possible explanations include altered loading, posture, dystonia or tone, gait, pain generation at an existing lesion, sensory processing, or a mixed mechanism. The repository does not select among them without expert evidence.

Local updating, global non-revision

Selective utilisation without integrative revision.

The later evidence was not necessarily ignored. Much of it was clinically used: phenotype, treatment response, mechanical offloading, OFF-state reasoning, pain control and dopaminergic modulation all changed locally. The longitudinal question is whether those observations were ever assembled into a revised global formulation of the relationship between the neurological motor disorder and the painful foot.

Did later evidence revise the founding formulation, or did the founding formulation principally govern what later evidence was allowed to mean?

This remains an expert-dependent synthesis question, not proof of confirmation bias, lock-in or negligence.

Formulation-dependent uncertainty

The repository distinguishes unavoidable uncertainty from a possible structural form of uncertainty in which relevant observations are compartmentalised and used narrowly rather than assembled into a revised model.

The uncertainty may have persisted not only because the medicine was difficult, but because the formulation was not adequately updated by the evidence it generated.

That stronger proposition becomes available only if an independent movement-disorders expert concludes that reasonable practice required integrative revision at one or more points.

The later missing operation

The founding join existed: it was the discharge synthesis. The later question is when the new evidence required a re-join.

Podiatry accumulated local mechanical evidence. Neurology accumulated dynamic treatment-state evidence. Primary care later regenerated an integrated feet–tone–gait formulation. The audit question is where those streams were explicitly brought together and whether reasonable neurological practice required that integration earlier than it occurred.

The biology updated. The expert question is whether the clinical formulation was required to update with it.
