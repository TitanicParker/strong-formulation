The moment the first paragraph came back into play.

The patient did not introduce a new complaint. After days of neurological work, he restored the concrete foot-and-gait problem with which Brennan’s referral had begun.

Before

The GP had referred one problem: bilateral foot issues and abnormal gait, followed by the neurological signs that made the presentation urgent. Neurology then developed the severe young-onset Parkinsonian syndrome, dystonic-looking lower-limb sign, possible PRKN/PARK2 and treatment strategy far more extensively than the painful feet.

The Protest

Direct patient evidence: before closure, the patient says the feet have not been properly dealt with and the work cannot therefore be complete.

“full NCT … send me out on bald tires?”

The point is not a diagnostic theory. It is a restoration of the referrer’s opening problem. The neurologist answers Podiatry.

After

The discharge synthesis contains the neurological and foot elements together but arranges them directionally: Parkinsonism/PRKN/treatment strategy are extensively developed; the painful feet are compatible with a separate/peripheral-neuropathy/Podiatry channel ending “Not clear.” “Markedly divorced” travels; the Protest does not.

Initial narrowing may be understandable. Once the patient restores the originating foot problem before closure, the whole referral is squarely back in play.

Why this matters: by this point substantial diagnostic and treatment commitment has accumulated. The record does not prove pride, but it does create a professional-coherence problem: reopening the first paragraph means acknowledging that the presenting problem remains insufficiently worked through despite days of sophisticated specialist work.

The patient did not discover a new problem at discharge. He restored the referrer’s original problem after days in which the record had overwhelmingly valued something else.
