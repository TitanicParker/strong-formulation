# Healy Diagnostic Specificity and Constructed Continuity

**Status:** CONTROLLING DOCUMENTARY / CONTINUITY ANALYSIS — 9 September 2026

This file preserves a document-only finding that materially refines the repository's recent work on continuity, resilience, reliance, selective utilisation and global non-revision.

It does not infer motive. It does not allege dishonesty. It asks a narrower question:

> **What happened to the specific content of Professor Daniel Healy's 2017 second opinion when that opinion was later invoked in the March 2025 complaint response as part of a claim of longstanding diagnostic continuity?**

---

## 1. The contemporaneous 2017 second opinion was specific

Professor Daniel Healy personally reviewed the patient on 12 September 2017.

The repository's canonical transcription records that Healy's letter stated that the patient **“probably has Park II”**, recommended testing PARK II first and moving to a broader panel if negative, recorded dramatic motor improvement with procyclidine and rasagiline, and discussed the treatment behaviour of PARK II.

The opinion was therefore not merely:

> **This patient has Parkinson's disease.**

It was materially more specific:

> **This patient probably has PARK II.**

That aetiological specificity matters.

---

## 2. Monaghan subsequently preserved that specificity

The later neurological record did not immediately flatten Healy's opinion into generic Parkinson's disease.

Monaghan subsequently wrote that the specific **PARK2/PRKN** disorder was what **“we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause.”**

Thus, at that stage, the working aetiological state was:

> **Parkinsonism / Parkinson's disease with a specific suspected genetic cause: PARK2/PRKN.**

This is not the same causal proposition as idiopathic Parkinson's disease.

The distinction is important because “idiopathic” denotes the absence of an identified specific cause, whereas PRKN/PARK2 is a specific genetic aetiology.

---

## 3. The DATscan did not resolve this in favour of idiopathic disease

The 2017 DATscan request itself described:

> **“Walk is almost dystonically unusual. We suspect an Autosomal recessive ‘Idiopathic’, Parkinsons.”**

The radiology report then stated that the scan was consistent with:

> **“idiopathic Parkinson's disease or Lewy body dementia.”**

The treating neurologist later wrote that the scan was abnormal **“as was expected”** and that the report said Parkinson's disease or Lewy body disease, but that this was **“obviously not LBD.”**

Critically, the treating team did **not** treat the DATscan wording as excluding a genetic PRKN/PARK2 aetiology. After the scan, the record continued to develop recessive neurogenetic reasoning and later specifically recorded PARK2/PRKN as what Monaghan and Healy suspected to be the cause.

Therefore:

> **The DATscan cannot retrospectively be used to convert the 2017 record into a settled idiopathic diagnosis. The clinicians' own subsequent conduct shows that they did not understand it that way.**

---

## 4. The March 2025 complaint response changes the level of specificity

The 25 March 2025 complaint response states:

> **“Whatever the underlying cause for the peripheral neuropathic pain symptomatologies that [Patient] has, it is our belief and understanding that [Patient] has idiopathic Parkinson's disease.”**

Later in the same response, Monaghan writes:

> **“In essence we have known that [Patient] has Parkinson's; we have known it would be Sinemet or other dopamine agonist responsive...”**

The response also invokes Professor Healy in this way:

> **“We have had a second opinion for [Patient] in the very early stages of his condition, from Professor Daniel Healy, a noted Parkinson's Disease expert in Dublin at Beaumont Hospital.”**

That is a materially different use of the Healy evidence.

In 2017, the substance of Healy's opinion was:

> **probably PARK II.**

In March 2025, the response preserves:

> **the fact and authority of the second opinion**

but not:

> **the specific aetiological proposition supplied by that second opinion.**

The documentary transformation is therefore:

> **specific expert proposition retained in 2017 → expert authority retained in 2025 → specific proposition omitted from the 2025 continuity account.**

Shortest form:

> **Healy's authority survived; Healy's proposition did not.**

---

## 5. Why this matters to continuity

The repository has recently distinguished:

- continuity of care;
- continuity of documentation;
- continuity of formulation;
- and independent revalidation.

The Healy sequence shows why those distinctions are essential.

There was plainly continuity of **Parkinson's/Parkinsonism management**.

But the documents do **not** show stable continuity of the **aetiological formulation**.

The aetiological record moves through:

> **2017 Galway: recessive Parkinsonism / PRKN-PARK2 seriously entertained**

> **2017 Healy: probably PARK II**

> **later Monaghan: PARK2/PRKN is what “we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause”**

> **2023: iPD increasingly used as the baseline diagnosis**

> **25 March 2025: categorical idiopathic Parkinson's disease + “we have known” Parkinson's + Healy invoked as a validating Parkinson's expert.**

That is not straightforward diagnostic continuity.

It is continuity at a **broader level of abstraction**.

---

## 6. Continuity can be preserved by moving upward in abstraction

This is the critical systems finding.

PARK II and idiopathic Parkinson's disease conflict at the level of aetiology.

But both fit under the larger category:

> **Parkinson's disease / Parkinsonian disorder.**

Therefore an apparent longitudinal continuity can be preserved if the historical account is moved upward from the specific causal proposition to the broader disease category.

The sequence is:

> **PARK II → Parkinson's**

> **iPD → Parkinson's**

and then:

> **“In essence we have known that [Patient] has Parkinson's.”**

That formulation can be true at the broad syndromic level while obscuring a material change at the specific aetiological level.

The repository should therefore preserve this distinction:

> **Continuity of care is not continuity of formulation; continuity of wording is not continuity of evidential content.**

---

## 7. This is illustrative of selective utilisation without integrative revision

The repository already uses the concept **selective utilisation without integrative revision**.

The Healy sequence is a concrete example.

The useful part of the earlier evidence survives:

> **A noted Parkinson's expert supplied an early second opinion.**

The destabilising specificity does not:

> **That expert thought the patient probably had PARK II.**

Thus the evidence is not simply ignored.

It is **used selectively**.

Its authority contributes to the later defence of continuity, while the proposition most capable of complicating the categorical IPD account is not carried forward into that defence.

No motive need be inferred to observe that documentary operation.

---

## 8. This is also illustrative of local updating and global non-revision

The repository has repeatedly identified:

> **local updating, global non-revision.**

Here that pattern appears diagnostically.

Local diagnostic content changes:

- PARK II becomes specifically suspected;
- PRKN/PARK2 is carried as a possible cause;
- later iPD becomes the operative baseline;
- treatment response and pain modulation are repeatedly updated.

Yet the global retrospective account remains:

> **we have known Parkinson's throughout.**

That broader continuity survives even while the specific explanatory content beneath it changes.

Thus:

> **local diagnostic content changes; global Parkinson's continuity remains.**

---

## 9. Relationship to documentary resilience

This finding materially strengthens [`DOCUMENTARY_RESILIENCE_CONTINUITY_AND_RELIANCE.md`](DOCUMENTARY_RESILIENCE_CONTINUITY_AND_RELIANCE.md).

Previously the repository's concern was that a founding synthesis could become resilient because later records inherited and relied upon it.

The Healy finding adds a further mechanism:

> **resilience can be preserved not only by repetition, but by selective loss of specificity.**

A broader proposition can remain stable even when narrower propositions underneath it change.

That means later statements such as **“the letters show”** or **“we have known”** cannot safely be treated as proof that the underlying formulation itself remained constant.

The proper response is:

> **Continuity of what?**

That question should now be treated as a controlling evidential question in the repository.

---

## 10. Relationship to the March 2025 Sinemet CR response

The March 2025 complaint response also records that Sinemet had been known to help the patient's pain and that recent escalation to Sinemet CR had provided **“profound relief.”**

This treatment response does not genetically prove PRKN/PARK2.

But it is phenotype-relevant information in a patient whose 2017 record already contained:

- very-young-onset Parkinsonism;
- a dystonically unusual gait;
- a dystonic-looking lower limb;
- serious recessive neurogenetic reasoning;
- a specific PRKN/PARK2 hypothesis;
- and an independent second opinion that the patient probably had PARK II.

The document-only question is therefore:

> **Why did the profound later dopaminergic improvement in the painful foot not cause the March 2025 historical response to revisit the specific PRKN/PARK2-dystonia proposition already present in the founding record?**

Instead, the response states categorical idiopathic Parkinson's disease and presents Parkinson's/dopaminergic responsiveness as matters essentially known throughout.

Again, no motive need be inferred.

The observable effect is enough:

> **new phenotype-relevant information did not restore the earlier diagnostic specificity; the retrospective account instead relied on a broader Parkinson's continuity.**

---

## 11. Why this is potentially fatal to a strong claim of diagnostic continuity

A defence can still properly say that there was continuous recognition and treatment of a Parkinsonian disorder.

What becomes difficult is any stronger proposition that:

> **the diagnostic understanding itself was stable and the longitudinal letters therefore demonstrate continuous validation of one coherent historical formulation.**

The documents do not support that without qualification.

The aetiological account materially changes.

More importantly, when the history is challenged in March 2025, the response cites Healy's second opinion while not preserving the specific content of that opinion.

Thus the later continuity claim does not simply rest on an unchanged historical proposition.

It appears to rest on a **broader reformulation capable of containing both the earlier PARK II hypothesis and the later categorical iPD position under the generic heading “Parkinson's.”**

The strongest supportable conclusion is:

> **The record demonstrates continuity of Parkinsonian care, but not unqualified continuity of diagnostic formulation. The March 2025 rebuttal preserves the authority of Professor Healy's second opinion while omitting its specific PARK II content, and then relies on the broader proposition that Parkinson's had been known throughout. That is evidence that the apparent continuity was at least partly produced by abstraction and selective preservation of historical content rather than by stable continuity of the underlying aetiological formulation.**

---

## 12. Controlling formulations

> **Healy's authority survived; Healy's proposition did not.**

> **Continuity of care is not continuity of formulation; continuity of wording is not continuity of evidential content.**

> **When specificity threatens continuity, continuity can be preserved by retreating to a broader category.**

> **The proper question is not simply whether the record is continuous. It is: continuity of what?**

And the fullest formulation:

> **The March 2025 rebuttal does not merely demonstrate resilience of the founding record. It illustrates one mechanism by which that resilience could be maintained. A historically specific proposition—Professor Healy's opinion that the patient probably had PARK II—loses its diagnostic specificity while the authority of the expert who supplied it is retained. The resulting broader proposition, that Parkinson's had been known throughout, is then capable of supporting an appearance of continuity that the more specific aetiological record does not in fact possess.**

---

## Evidential discipline

This file does **not** establish that:

- the March 2025 response was intentionally misleading;
- Professor Healy was deliberately misrepresented;
- idiopathic Parkinson's disease was medically impossible;
- PRKN/PARK2 had been genetically confirmed by March 2025;
- Sinemet CR response proves a PRKN genotype;
- the Parkinson's diagnosis was wrong;
- or the change in diagnostic specificity was consciously undertaken to protect the historical record.

It establishes a narrower documentary fact: **the specific PARK II content of the 2017 expert second opinion is not preserved when that same second opinion is later invoked in support of a broader claim of longstanding Parkinson's continuity.**
