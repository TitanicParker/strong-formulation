# Documentary Investigation Report — 8 September 2026

**Status:** SOURCE-LEVEL AUDIT SNAPSHOT  
**Scope:** Patient-facing documentary corpus presently identified in the repository.  
**Purpose:** Trace what happened to the original feet–gait–tone problem after the July 2017 neurological admission, distinguishing later discussion, branch testing, current-state reconsideration and full retrospective reconstruction.

---

## 1. Governing distinction

This investigation does **not** decide negligence, one medical mechanism, dystonic causation of the foot lesions, the existence or absence of peripheral neuropathy, or private motive.

It asks four narrower questions:

1. what information existed;
2. through which clinical/documentary channel it travelled;
3. what later questions were actually asked of it; and
4. whether the founding feet–tone–gait allocation was ever reconstructed using the accumulated evidence.

Keep four different events separate:

> **later discussion of the problem**
>
> **investigation of one explanatory branch**
>
> **fresh/current-state clinical reconsideration**
>
> **retrospective reconstruction of the complete evidential genealogy**

The corpus demonstrates the first three in varying degrees. The principal unresolved finding is the fourth.

---

## 2. Executive finding

The record does not support either extreme formulation:

> **Nobody ever reconsidered the feet after 2017.**

or

> **The founding foot allocation was repeatedly independently retested and validated.**

The more accurate source-level position is:

> **Substantial later information was reconsidered, but largely through particular explanatory branches and current-state questions: neuropathic pain, OFF-state phenomena, dopaminergic pain modulation, movement phenomena and rare overlap disorders. The patient-facing corpus presently does not identify a single reconstruction that assembles the focal Podiatry anatomy, the original dystonic-looking lower-limb sign, the procyclidine treatment-state evidence, the 2019 GP reintegration and the later motor/pain response and asks what those facts collectively mean for the founding 2017 feet–Neurology allocation.**

## 3. Podiatry predates the Protest and discharge disposition

The GP record on **10 April 2017** documents bilateral foot pain, abnormal gait, mallet toes, callus/pressure areas and a plan for Podiatry. See `CLIN-000018–000028`.

By **8 June 2017**, the GP was considering Neurology specifically for the patient's **gait, feet and “muscle tension” issues**. See `CLIN-000064–000074`.

During the neurological admission, on **6 July 2017**, the GP telephoned Podiatry and recorded that the case would be marked as a priority and **“as Parkinson’s.”** See `CLIN-000236–000245`.

The later Podiatry system distinguishes:

- **Referral Date:** 10 Apr 2017;
- **Registration Date:** 10 Jul 2017;
- **First Treated:** 14 Sep 2017;
- **Patient Last Seen:** 25 Mar 2019.

See `CLIN-002819–002839`.

The surviving referral material identifies **Dr Aaron Brennan** as referrer. See `CLIN-003014–003016`.

The hospital discharge material separately says a Podiatry referral **“has been sent”**, while the longer neurological plan requests outpatient Podiatry and records that an SHO had tried to arrange it. See `CLIN-000341–000345`, `CLIN-001225–001229`.

Accordingly:

> **The post-Protest answer “Podiatry” intersected with an already-existing GP-origin Podiatry pathway. The surviving patient-facing Podiatry corpus does not presently demonstrate a distinct Neurology-origin handover carrying a defined neurological question about tone, gait, dystonia, loading or the structural–dynamic relationship.**

This does not prove that no verbal or missing written Neurology communication ever existed.

## 4. The post-Protest question is therefore more precise

Podiatry was already clinically legitimate and already in motion.

The source-level question is not simply:

> **Was Podiatry an appropriate service?**

It is:

> **What additional neurological problem-solving, question or return mechanism was added to that pre-existing pathway after the patient said that Neurology had not properly dealt with the feet?**

The patient-facing corpus presently does not answer that through a distinct integrative handover document.

## 5. Podiatry developed a stable focal anatomical object

Podiatry's later record becomes highly specific.

The first documented treatment identifies a **left fifth-MTPJ callus** and tight gastrocnemius/soleus. In October 2017 the same site is enucleated/debrided to pin-prick bleeding, directly offloaded and used to guide an insole designed to redistribute pressure. The same fifth-MTPJ territory recurs repeatedly through 2018 and into 2019, together with clawed-toe and biomechanical observations. See `CLIN-002866–002988`.

Thus:

> **Podiatry acquired a stable local mechanical object: site + recurrence + lesion + repeated debridement + targeted offloading.**

This establishes focal loading pathology without establishing why that loading occurred.

## 6. Neurology acquired a different kind of fact about the same broad territory

The neurological plan itself contemplated a procyclidine test-stop. See `CLIN-001215–001221`.

During withdrawal the patient contemporaneously reported tension building, **restless feet** and his **walk falling apart**. See `CLIN-001770–001790`.

In April 2018 the treating neurologist recorded that the **first thing** noticed changing, and changing **rapidly**, was the **return** of the outside-left-foot corn phenomenon, together with greater shiftiness/unrelaxed behaviour off procyclidine. See `CLIN-001887–001919`.

Therefore:

> **Neurology acquired dynamic treatment-state information affecting the same broad lateral-foot territory in which Podiatry was repeatedly documenting focal mechanical pathology.**

This does not prove the mechanism of the corn or fifth-MTPJ lesion.

## 7. The central 2017–2019 documentary finding is a missing join

By 2018 the health system held complementary information:

**Podiatry:** repeated focal fifth-MTPJ loading and mechanical treatment.

**Neurology:** rapid outside-left-foot change during neurological treatment perturbation.

The patient-facing corpus presently reviewed does not identify a document that expressly combines those two observations into a single question about posture, tone, dystonia, gait mechanics, pain modulation or mixed mechanisms.

Nor does the available Podiatry material presently demonstrate a substantive biomechanical report back to Neurology that is then visibly integrated into neurological reasoning.

The correct record-audit proposition is:

> **The available material looks more like parallel specialty information streams than a documented Neurology → Podiatry investigation → Podiatry report-back → neurological reintegration loop.**

This remains subject to unproduced correspondence.

## 8. The 2019 GP reintegration demonstrably travelled through Podiatry

The 2019 GP referral states that the patient had previously attended Podiatry because of feet issues **“in relating to increased tone and abnormal gait relating to Parkinson’s disease”** and records young-onset Parkinson’s with **“clawing feet”** and pain. See `CLIN-003083–003108`.

The referral is preserved in the Podiatry corpus, and the Podiatry system records attendance on **25 March 2019**.

The source therefore establishes:

> **Primary care reconstructed an integrated feet–tone–gait–Parkinsonism object and sent that reconstruction through the Podiatry channel.**

It does not presently establish that Neurology received and reconsidered that 2019 integrated referral.

This demonstrates why **institutional possession is not the same as possession by the clinician whose framework would need updating**.

## 19. What generated the longitudinal consistency

The record's consistency appears to arise from several processes operating together:

1. **real biological consistency** — objective and later independent evidence supports Parkinsonism;
2. **longitudinal clinical observation** — genuinely new findings and treatment-state responses accumulate;
3. **authorial continuity** — the same treating neurological author remains substantially present;
4. **documentary inheritance** — later letters expressly rely on earlier summaries;
5. **local assimilation** — later evidence is handled through OFF-state, pain, neuropathy, dystonia and medication branches;
6. **partial/current-state reconsideration** — especially from 2023 onward, the model visibly broadens.

What the corpus presently does not identify is a seventh process:

> **a documented global retrospective reassembly of the founding feet–tone–gait–loading question.**
