Primary 2017 theory

One compact causal sequence

Alleged mistake: the GP sent an integrated feet–gait–tone problem into Neurology. Severe Parkinsonism was extensively developed, but the painful abnormal feet through which the referral began were not equivalently reconstructed after the neurological discoveries.

Protest: the patient's account is that, before discharge, he directly identified that incompleteness: the feet had not been properly dealt with and Neurology could not therefore be finished. The occurrence and surrounding circumstances remain open to ordinary evidential testing and corroboration.

Decision: the pathway closed through Podiatry. Source-level review shows Podiatry was already an active GP-origin pathway, while the surviving corpus identifies no distinct post-Protest neurological question or return mechanism added to it.

Omission: the final synthesis made the closure architecture portable but does not carry the asserted Protest.

Consequences: later readers inherited that information state; subsequent mechanical and neurological truths developed in parallel; later evidence repeatedly tested the durability of the founding allocation.

If the Protest occurred as described, closure thereafter becomes a decision made with the incompleteness expressly raised.

What must be proved first

  1. Referral object: the GP sent one integrated feet/gait/tone presentation.
  2. Investigative asymmetry: the Parkinsonian syndrome received substantially more development than the painful abnormal feet.
  3. Protest: the patient's account of the pre-closure challenge is factually established to the required standard.
  4. Decision: after that challenge, the response remained Podiatry/closure rather than identified neurological reopening or a defined neurological handover.
  5. Portable omission: the challenge does not appear in the final synthesis later readers inherit.

Only after those propositions are established should the later longitudinal evidence be used to show consequence, persistence or missed reconstruction.

What the repository presently proves best

It presently makes a stronger case for a credible documentary and clinical fault-line than for ultimate negligence.

The fault-line is visible in the integrated referral, Neurology's own dystonic-looking lower-limb observation, the directional founding synthesis, the pre-existing Podiatry route and the later evidence repeatedly crossing a clean structural/neurological boundary.

A coherent documentary story is not the same thing as proof of breach or causation.

The strongest defence

Severe very-young-onset Parkinsonism legitimately commanded substantial neurological attention. Structural foot disease was objectively real. Podiatry was already active and appropriate for mechanical pathology. The founding synthesis preserved uncertainty rather than dogmatically declaring the feet non-neurological. Treatment caution was genuine. Later evidence supports mixed mechanisms. Later Neurology genuinely reconsidered the current problem.

A competent defence can therefore say that documentary asymmetry and later dopaminergic response do not by themselves prove what reasonable neurological care required in July 2017.

Why the synthesis matters

The synthesis contains the major facts. Its significance is directional: Parkinsonism/PRKN/treatment receive extensive explanatory force; the feet are made compatible with separate/peripheral-neuropathy/Podiatry territory ending “Not clear”; “markedly divorced” travels; the asserted Protest does not.

If the Protest occurred and closure was nevertheless chosen, the document also had an ordinary objective justificatory / professional-coherence function: it explained why diagnosis, disposition and treatment remained coherent.

The question is not whether professional documentation justifies decisions. It ordinarily does. The question is whether a material challenge to completeness should also have travelled.

Later evidence: consequence and test

Procyclidine withdrawal changed feet, gait and tension. The April 2018 note records rapid return of an outside-left-foot phenomenon. Podiatry repeatedly documented and mechanically offloaded the fifth-MTPJ site. Primary care later regenerated an integrated feet–tone–gait formulation. Later Neurology genuinely reopened the current problem and developed a mixed treatment-state-sensitive model.

These facts test the founding allocation. They do not prove one exclusive mechanism.

Documentary genealogy

The later record contains both inheritance and genuine independent evidence. “As per older letters” and the 2025 reliance on what “the letters show” make provenance relevant. DATscan, Professor Healy, Podiatry, GP observations and new treatment-state findings remain genuinely independent inputs for the propositions they actually test.

The correct question is which propositions were independently retested and which gained force mainly through inherited continuity.

Causation: the major unresolved bridge

Even if the Protest is proved and a neurologist concludes that reasonable care required further reassessment or a defined integrated handover, the claim must still show what would probably have happened differently.

What would probably have been discovered? What treatment or referral would probably have changed? When? Which later pain, disability, functional loss or psychiatric injury would probably have been avoided or reduced?

The repository presently tells the information-state story more convincingly than it proves that counterfactual.

Separate 2023 safety question

The 2023 episode should be analysed independently. An urgent pain crisis contained serious endurance language; the patient had major walking restriction; falls risk already existed in the neurological record; and the clinic directly observed stumbles.

The later review also documents improved mood/pain and substantial active reconsideration, which are important defence facts.

The separate expert question is whether the combined pain–mobility–falls–psychological safety state was recognised and managed as one contemporaneous risk object, and what response would ordinarily be expected after observed instability in that context.
