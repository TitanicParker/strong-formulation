Controlling simplification

One mistake. One Protest. One decision. One omission. Then the consequences.

The later record is important, but it is not the liability centre. The 2017 case can be understood first as one compact decision sequence.

incomplete neurological construction→Protest identifies it→decision to close rather than reopen→Protest omitted from portable synthesis→later consequences and tests

1. The mistake

The GP sent one integrated clinical problem: painful abnormal feet and gait, with pathological lower-limb tone, toe-clawing, movement difficulty and deteriorating function. Neurology identified severe very-young-onset Parkinsonism and developed that part extensively. The alleged mistake is not the Parkinson's diagnosis. It is the failure to complete the neurological construction of the presenting feet–gait–tone problem before closure.

2. The Protest

Before discharge, the patient says in substance that the feet have not been properly dealt with and Neurology cannot therefore be finished. He does not need to know the mechanism. He identifies the incompleteness while the admission is still live.

Before the Protest: incomplete construction. After the Protest: closure becomes a decision made with the incompleteness expressly raised.

3. The decision

The answer is Podiatry. Source-level investigation now shows that Podiatry was already an active GP-origin pathway. The patient-facing corpus presently identifies no distinct post-Protest neurological handover question or return mechanism that transformed that existing route into an investigation of the feet–tone–gait relationship.

4. The omission

The final synthesis contains the major facts but arranges them so that the feet can remain separate, possibly neuropathic, “Not clear” and operationally Podiatry. The Protest does not travel. That changes the represented decision history from “patient challenged completeness, clinician chose closure” to a more ordinary specialist-assessment-and-disposition story.

What later evidence is for

Consequences and tests, not front-loaded liability.

Later Podiatry mechanics, treatment-state changes, GP reintegration, OFF phenomena and dopaminergic pain response matter because they show that the unresolved relationship remained clinically meaningful and because they test the durability of the founding allocation. They do not need to be carried inside the first sentence of the 2017 case.
