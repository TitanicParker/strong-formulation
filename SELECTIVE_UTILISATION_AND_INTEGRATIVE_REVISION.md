# Selective Utilisation Without Integrative Revision

**Status:** LONGITUDINAL SYNTHESIS / EXPERT MAP — 8 September 2026

## 1. The recurring pattern

The longitudinal record may contain a more precise pattern than simple **failure to reconsider**.

Relevant evidence was not necessarily absent, ignored or disbelieved. Much of it appears to have been recognised and used.

The narrower question is whether successive observations were used for limited local purposes without acquiring enough weight to revise the founding neurological–foot boundary itself.

The controlling formulation is:

> **successive observations repeatedly acquired enough clinical significance to influence treatment, describe the phenotype or explain symptoms, but the surviving record does not demonstrate equivalent global revision of the original relationship between the neurological motor disorder and the painful foot.**

This repository calls that pattern:

> **selective utilisation without integrative revision.**

That is a structural and record-audit proposition. It is not a finding of negligence, bad faith, conscious confirmation bias or deliberate suppression. Whether reasonable movement-disorders practice required global revision at any particular point remains an expert question.

---

## 2. Local updating versus global revision

The distinction is simple.

### Local updating

A new observation changes one part of the clinical picture:

- dystonia helps characterise the neurological phenotype;
- procyclidine informs treatment-state response;
- Podiatry defines focal mechanical pathology and offloading;
- OFF-state reasoning explains pain or shuffling;
- Stalevo alters treatment;
- pregabalin alters pain control;
- Sinemet responsiveness informs pain modulation.

### Global revision

The higher-order formulation is reconsidered:

> **What is the relationship between the neurological motor disorder and the painful structural foot?**

The repository's audit question is whether the record shows repeated local updating without a correspondingly explicit global reconstruction of that relationship.

Shortest:

> **The system kept learning facts. The expert question is whether it adequately learned what those facts meant together.**

## 3. 2017 — dystonic lower-limb evidence enters the phenotype but not the foot relationship

Neurology documented:

- markedly increased lower-limb tone;
- a strikingly abnormal gait;
- a lower-limb appearance **“somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture – NOT FOOT DROP”**;
- pes planus and hammertoes;
- an **“almost dystonically unusual”** walk in the DAT-scan request;
- suspected autosomal-recessive Parkinsonism;
- later detailed consideration of PRKN/PARK2.

The dystonic-looking lower-limb feature therefore had recognised neurological meaning.

The record does not presently demonstrate equivalent integration of that feature into the painful foot problem before the feet were made compatible with:

> **“completely separate” / possible peripheral neuropathy / “Not clear” / Podiatry.**

The expert question is not whether the dystonic-looking sign proved the foot problem was neurological.

It is:

> **Why was the lower-limb dystonic feature clinically meaningful enough to help characterise an unusual very-young-onset Parkinsonian phenotype, yet not clearly used to examine its possible relationship to the painful abnormal foot at the end of that same limb?**

This is the first possible example of local utilisation without integrative revision.

---

## 4. 2017 — patient phenomenology may have been used as separation rather than mechanism

The founding synthesis says the patient had **“markedly divorced”** the feeling of lower-limb tension from the corns, calluses and painful/burning feet.

A patient's ability to distinguish one sensation from another can be clinically informative.

It does not by itself establish different pathophysiology.

In a patient with gross rigidity, abnormal gait and a dystonic-looking lower limb, different sensations could in principle still arise at different points within one interacting motor–mechanical system.

The record-audit question is therefore:

> **Was the phenomenological distinction investigated as evidence about the relationship, or did it mainly acquire weight as evidence preserving separation?**

That remains an expert interpretation, not a factual conclusion from wording alone.

---

## 5. October 2017 / April 2018 — dynamic treatment-state evidence crosses the boundary

During planned procyclidine withdrawal, contemporaneous evidence records:

> **“I am feeling the tension building up, my feet are restless and my walk is falling apart....but my eyesight is back to normal.”**

In April 2018 the treating neurologist himself then records that, during the earlier withdrawal:

> **“the first thing he noticed changing and changing rapidly was the return on the foot, of the outside left corn.”**

The same clinical period contains:

- foot cramps;
- gait lacking fluency and speed;
- marked relief from tension on treatment;
- explicit celebration of walking without tension;
- bilateral toe supports helping;
- the rapid outside-left-foot treatment-state observation;
- broader worsening off procyclidine, including being more **“shifty”** and **“unrelaxed.”**

The importance is not that procyclidine physically removed and regenerated a keratotic corn.

The importance is that **motor state, foot symptoms, gait and mechanical support all appear in the same neurological information state**.

The central question remains:

> **Even if July 2017 “Not clear” was initially reasonable, did this new neurological treatment-state evidence require the original relationship to be reopened and integrated?**

This is the repository's strongest independent backstop.

## 6. 2017–2019 — Podiatry supplies a stable mechanical target

Podiatry repeatedly documents and treats:

- recurrent left fifth-MTPJ HD/callus;
- repeated debridement/enucleation;
- direct offloading;
- clawed toes;
- pronating foot type;
- insoles, Poron cut-out and toe props.

That proves genuine focal mechanical pathology.

It does **not** prove neurological independence.

The relevant mixed-mechanism question is:

> **Was the structural lesion mechanically independent, neurologically influenced, or mixed?**

Podiatry possessed local mechanical information. Neurology possessed dynamic treatment-state information.

The record-audit question is where those two streams were explicitly assembled into one model.

---

## 7. 2019 — primary care verbally reconstructs the relationship

The GP later records foot problems in relation to increased tone and abnormal gait associated with Parkinson's disease, including language about young-onset Parkinson's, clawing feet and pain.

This is important because a downstream clinician expressly reconstructs:

> **feet ← increased tone / abnormal gait ← Parkinson's disease.**

The practical destination nevertheless remains Podiatry in the surviving corpus.

The safe proposition is not that Neurology received and ignored this formulation.

It is:

> **Primary care regenerated an integrated neurological–foot model, but the presently identified record does not show that this became a corresponding Neurology-owned historical reconstruction.**

---

## 8. 2023 — current neurological reasoning re-enters pain, gait and dystonia

The 2023 record contains genuine reconsideration and must not be described as clinical inaction.

Neurology considered:

- lower-limb cramping;
- OFF dystonia;
- gait deterioration;
- neuropathic possibilities;
- Fabry disease as a branch possibility;
- medication state;
- the possibility that Parkinsonian OFF states amplify pain;
- levodopa/Stalevo effects on walking and pain.

Later in 2023, severe pain and shuffling were assessed as appearing **OFF-related**, and treatment was altered.

That is genuine neurological use of the later evidence.

The narrower historical question is:

> **Did this current-state reasoning produce a reconstruction of the original 2017–2018 relationship, or was it used principally to manage the current presentation within the existing historical architecture?**

That distinction matters because present reconsideration is not automatically the same as historical integrative revision.

---

## 9. 2024 — pain and motor phenomena can be separated without being independent

The 2024 record reports that pregabalin removed much of the foot pain while activity-dependent toe curling persisted, including during standing/darts and after sustained physical effort.

That observation is capable of separating components:

> **pain state ≠ toe-curling / motor state.**

But separation of components does not establish independence of systems.

It may instead support a mixed model in which:

- motor posture persists;
- pain is separately modifiable;
- structural loading remains relevant;
- and those components may still interact at the foot-ground interface.

The expert question is what clinical significance should reasonably have been attached to that separation.

---

## 10. 2025 — dopaminergic pain response is acknowledged but contained

By 2025 Neurology records that once Sinemet **“hits his system”** the feet feel better from a pain perspective and states that increased Sinemet would probably produce further pain relief, while also recognising that dystonia can be painful.

The clinician also offers a medically plausible alternative interpretation: dopaminergic treatment may alter the experience of pain even where the underlying painful source is not itself Parkinsonian.

That explanation may be defensible in isolation.

The longitudinal expert question is different:

> **How should that interpretation be weighed against the pre-existing sequence of dystonic-looking lower-limb signs, PRKN reasoning, procyclidine dechallenge, recurrent fifth-MTPJ pathology, GP reintegration, OFF-related pain/shuffling and activity-dependent toe curling?**

The record again recognises treatment-state modulation.

The question is whether that observation is used only to explain symptoms locally or to revise the historical relationship globally.

## 11. Formulation-dependent uncertainty

The repository should distinguish **unavoidable uncertainty** from **formulation-dependent uncertainty**.

Unavoidable uncertainty means that reasonable clinical investigation has occurred and the remaining ambiguity genuinely cannot be resolved further at that stage.

Formulation-dependent uncertainty is a hypothesis about information structure:

> **the persistence of uncertainty may partly reflect the way relevant observations were compartmentalised, such that evidence capable of narrowing or restructuring the uncertainty was repeatedly used for narrower purposes without being assembled into a revised integrated model.**

This is safer than saying the uncertainty was deliberately manufactured or consciously maintained.

If an independent movement-disorders expert concludes that the later evidence should have required integrative revision, the stronger proposition becomes available:

> **The uncertainty was not simply the unavoidable residue of difficult medicine; it may have been prolonged because the clinical formulation was not adequately updated by the evidence it generated.**

That remains an expert-dependent proposition.

---

## 12. Lock-in as a systems hypothesis

The systems-level hypothesis is:

> **a founding synthesis creates a boundary → the boundary shapes the care pathway → later evidence is interpreted within that pathway instead of being allowed to test whether the boundary itself remains valid.**

The repository may use **diagnostic and management lock-in** as shorthand for that hypothesis, but it should not treat lock-in as proved merely because the chronology is coherent.

The observable record question is narrower:

> **Did later evidence cause the founding formulation to be reconsidered, or did the founding formulation principally govern how the later evidence was interpreted?**

This preserves structure without inferring motive.

---

## 13. The patient and clinician had different knowledge states

Neurological treatment produced marked motor improvement and was associated with improvement in the foot phenomenon about which the patient had complained.

From the patient's perspective, therapeutic success was capable of reinforcing confidence in the neurological care he had received.

Its potentially contrary significance — that a symptom allocated as possibly separate appeared to vary with neurological treatment state — was a matter requiring professional interpretation.

Thus:

> **The treatment reassured the patient while giving Neurology a reason to reconsider.**

A sharper but still conditional formulation is:

> **The tablets could mask the alleged error from the patient while simultaneously generating evidence of it for the neurologist.**

The repository should not use that sentence to imply that the patient lacked all awareness of continuing foot problems; its significance is narrower, concerning the patient's ability to recognise the professional meaning of treatment-state dependence.

## 14. The foot-ground interface

The lower limb and foot form one mechanically continuous system.

The unresolved motor abnormality acted through the same lower-limb system whose terminal load-bearing interface contained recurrent structural pathology.

That makes posture, tone, gait, loading, local tissue injury and pain clinically capable of interacting.

The repository should **not** infer from that alone that the neurological disorder caused the structural lesion or all later pain.

The safe formulation is:

> **The recurrent structural pathology arose at the principal load-bearing interface of a neurologically abnormal limb, making neurological, mechanical and structural interaction a clinically material question even though the relative contribution of each mechanism remained to be determined.**

---

## 15. Ownership of the relationship

The claimant does not need to prove that every corn, callus, deformity or episode of pain was itself a primary neurological manifestation.

The stronger question is whether Neurology was entitled to treat the foot problem as sufficiently outside the movement disorder without either determining or establishing a process capable of determining the respective contributions.

Adequate neurological ownership could have taken several forms:

- **integrated conclusion:** structural lesion, but neurological motor state contributes to loading or pain;
- **reasoned exclusion:** targeted assessment concludes neurological contribution is unlikely, with reasons;
- **defined uncertainty:** relationship unresolved, with a specific Podiatry/biomechanical question and a return mechanism for neurological reinterpretation.

Thus:

> **Neurology did not have to own every foot lesion. It did have to own the unresolved neurological relationship, if reasonable practice required that relationship to remain within neurological responsibility.**

Whether that standard was required is for expert evidence.

## 16. Expert questions

### Longitudinal synthesis

> **Across 2017–2025, the record repeatedly recognises dystonic lower-limb features, neurological treatment-state changes affecting foot symptoms and gait, recurrent focal mechanical pathology, OFF-related pain and later dopaminergic modulation of foot pain. Were these findings adequately integrated into a revised formulation of the relationship between the movement disorder and the patient's foot pathology? If not, at what point should reasonable movement-disorders practice have required such revision, and what should that revision have involved?**

### Local use versus global revision

> **Is the record better characterised as genuine repeated reconsideration of the original neurological–foot relationship, or as repeated utilisation of individual observations for treatment and symptom management without equivalent revision of the underlying formulation?**

### Mixed mechanisms / ownership

> **If you consider the claimant's foot disorder to have been multifactorial, please identify where the contemporaneous neurological record distinguishes the structural, mechanical and neurological contributions, or establishes a process by which those contributions were to be distinguished. If no such assessment or process is demonstrated, on what clinical basis was the foot problem treated as sufficiently separate from the movement disorder for Neurology not to retain responsibility for resolving that relationship?**

### 2018 stress test

> **Even if the July 2017 conclusion that the relationship between the foot problem and the neurological disorder was “Not clear” was initially within reasonable practice, once the treating neurologist recorded that withdrawal of procyclidine was followed by rapid return of the focal outside-left-foot phenomenon, did reasonable neurological practice require that original relationship to be reopened and investigated? If not, why not?**

## 17. Evidential discipline

This analysis does **not** itself prove:

- that PRKN necessarily caused the foot phenotype;
- that the fifth-MTPJ lesion was caused by dystonia;
- that procyclidine treated a corn;
- that every later pain state had one neurological mechanism;
- that structural pathology was unreal;
- that Neurology never reconsidered the feet;
- that the 2023 review was clinically empty;
- that Sinemet response proves the original foot pathology was Parkinsonian;
- that any clinician deliberately maintained uncertainty;
- that confirmation bias, concealment or self-protection occurred;
- or that any failure of integration caused later harm.

The source-level proposition is more limited:

> **The record repeatedly generated observations capable of bearing on the neurological–mechanical relationship. Those observations were often clinically used. The expert question is whether they were adequately assembled into a revised integrated formulation when reasonable practice required it.**

---

## 18. Shortest formulation

> **Local updating, global non-revision: the record repeatedly used new neurological, mechanical and treatment-state information without clearly demonstrating equivalent revision of the founding neurological–foot boundary. If an expert concludes that the later evidence should have broken that boundary, the question becomes whether the formulation itself prolonged the uncertainty it was supposed to resolve.**
