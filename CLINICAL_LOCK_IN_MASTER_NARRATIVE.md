# Clinical Lock-In — Primary Clinical Narrative

**Status:** PRIMARY CLINICAL NARRATIVE — 8 September 2026

This sits **beside** *The Professional Fork*. It does not replace or invalidate that essay. The two texts perform different jobs:

- **Clinical Lock-In** is the primary longitudinal clinical narrative: how an early neurological–foot boundary may have shaped later care and resisted revision as new evidence accumulated.
- **The Professional Fork** remains the deep documentary narrative: the Protest, closure, founding synthesis, omission, professional-coherence function and later documentary genealogy.

This case can no longer sensibly be reduced to a disagreement about whether one man's foot pain was **“Parkinsonian.”**

That formulation is too small for the record.

The real issue is whether a sophisticated neurological service created an insufficiently grounded boundary inside a disease process it was already treating, embedded that boundary in an authoritative founding synthesis, and then failed adequately to dismantle it when its own observations, its own treatments and the patient's subsequent course repeatedly crossed that boundary.

In July 2017, this was not an ordinary presentation. A 39-year-old man arrived after an extraordinarily long prodrome in a visibly severe neurological state. His gait was described as **“strikingly abnormal.”** His lower limb gave an appearance **“somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture – NOT FOOT DROP.”** He was grossly rigid and bradykinetic. His feet were abnormal.

The service did not overlook the neurological significance of the gait. It expressly told Nuclear Medicine that his walk was **“almost dystonically unusual”** immediately before stating that it suspected an autosomal-recessive Parkinsonism.

Nor was recessive Parkinsonism a passing thought. The eventual synthesis carefully developed the clinical indicators favouring PRKN/PARK2: very young onset, akinetic rigidity, absence of dominant family history, marked symmetry and exceptional cognition.

But something important happens inside that synthesis.

The dystonic lower-limb feature does not visibly become part of a neurological reconstruction of the painful foot problem.

And the painful feet go elsewhere.

They may be **“completely separate.”** They may represent peripheral neuropathy. The relationship is **“Not clear.”**

That is the founding boundary.

The problem is not that medicine tolerated uncertainty. Medicine must tolerate uncertainty.

The problem is that the document was **epistemically uncertain but operationally committed**.

The feet were treated as sufficiently separate from the neurological disorder to be routed toward Podiatry, without a clearly documented neurological question asking whether dystonia, rigidity, abnormal gait, posture or pathological loading was producing or aggravating the painful structural foot problem, and without an identified return mechanism by which Podiatry findings would resolve Neurology's **“Not clear.”**

The claimant does not allege that Podiatry was inappropriate. Genuine structural foot disease existed. The question is whether Podiatry was sufficient as the neurological response to an unresolved mixed-mechanism problem.

## The Protest

The patient's account is that, immediately before discharge, he said in substance:

> **You have not dealt with my feet.**

That account remains subject to ordinary factual proof and corroboration.

If proved, it is profoundly important. Not because the patient had solved the mechanism. He had not. He was identifying the missing clinical work.

After approximately eight days in a hospital full of neurological expertise, the patient pointed to the part of his own phenotype that the specialist synthesis had failed to integrate.

If reasonable neurological practice required that challenge to trigger reconsideration, the allegation becomes a decision-after-notice breach: the inadequacy had been expressly raised while the decision was still live, yet the pathway nevertheless closed.

The final communication then matters because it presents the admission as unusually extensive. It records that the prolonged stay allowed the team to **“get a lot done,”** to **“explore a lot of questions,”** and to obtain a **“firmer footing going forward,”** while also carrying Complex Case Meeting, senior neurological and neurogenetic reasoning.

Without the Protest, the receiving GP sees:

> **extensive specialist assessment → collective neurological consideration → “Not clear” → Podiatry.**

If the Protest occurred, the fuller decision history is:

> **extensive specialist assessment → patient says the central foot problem has not been dealt with → closure maintained → “Not clear” → Podiatry.**

Those are materially different information states.

The documentary allegation is therefore not merely that a patient complaint was omitted. It is that, if the Protest occurred, omission of the challenge could allow **challenged incompleteness to travel downstream as apparently considered specialist uncertainty**.

No conclusion of dishonesty, concealment or conscious self-protection follows automatically from that documentary function.

## Treatment reassures the patient and challenges the formulation

Then something happened which could hardly have been more reassuring to the patient.

Neurological treatment worked.

He experienced marked motor improvement together with improvement in the foot phenomenon about which he had complained. From his perspective, the natural conclusion was that the neurological treatment was helping the problem that had brought him into care.

That success reduced, rather than increased, his reason to question the specialist formulation.

Professionally, the same event potentially carried the opposite significance.

A symptom placed outside the neurological formulation appeared to vary with neurological treatment state.

Thus:

> **The treatment reassured the patient while giving Neurology a reason to reconsider.**

Or, more sharply but still conditionally:

> **The tablets could mask the alleged error from the patient while simultaneously generating evidence of it for the neurologist.**

Its mechanism was not for the patient to diagnose. Whether the change reflected dystonia, altered tone, gait, loading, pain processing or a mixed mechanism was a professional neurological question.

## The dechallenge

When procyclidine was withdrawn, the contemporaneous patient account recorded increasing tension, restless feet and deterioration of his walk.

More importantly, the treating neurologist later recorded that during the withdrawal the **first thing** changing, and changing **rapidly**, was the return on the foot of the **“outside left corn”** phenomenon. The patient became more **“shifty”** and **“unrelaxed.”** The same clinical period records foot cramps, altered gait, marked relief from neurological tension when treated and benefit from toe supports.

The claimant does not need that observation to prove that a keratotic corn physically disappeared under procyclidine and regenerated when the drug was stopped.

Its significance is different.

> **A focal foot phenomenon previously allocated outside the central neurological formulation appeared to vary rapidly with manipulation of neurological treatment.**

That did not prove mechanism.

It created a neurological question.

What changed at the interface between neurological motor state and the painful load-bearing foot?

Possible explanations included altered posture, dystonia or tone, gait, loading, pain generation at an existing lesion, sensory processing, or a mixture of mechanisms.

Even if July 2017 **“Not clear”** had initially been reasonable, April 2018 therefore creates an independent expert question:

> **Once the treating neurologist recorded rapid treatment-state alteration of the focal outside-left-foot phenomenon, did reasonable movement-disorders practice require the original neurological–foot relationship to be reopened and investigated? If not, why not?**

That is the principal clinical backstop of the case because it does not materially depend on proof of the Protest.

## The mechanical target

Podiatry then supplied a stable focal mechanical object.

Across 2017–2019 it repeatedly documented and treated left fifth-MTPJ HD/callus, clawed toes and pronating foot type, using debridement, enucleation, toe props, insoles and direct offloading including a specific cut-out at the fifth-MTPJ region.

That is genuine structural and mechanical pathology.

It is not proof of neurological independence.

The relevant question is:

> **Was the structural lesion mechanically independent, neurologically influenced, or mixed?**

Neurology possessed dynamic treatment-state information. Podiatry possessed focal mechanical information. The expert question is when reasonable care required those streams to be assembled into one formulation.

The claimant does not need to prove that every corn, callus, deformity or episode of pain was a primary neurological manifestation.

The allegation is narrower:

> **Neurology did not have to own every foot lesion. It did have to own the unresolved neurological relationship, if reasonable practice required that relationship to remain within neurological responsibility.**

## The boundary keeps being crossed

By 2019 primary care was explicitly describing the feet in relation to increased tone and abnormal gait associated with Parkinson's disease, including language about young-onset Parkinson's, clawing feet and pain.

That downstream formulation reconstructed:

> **feet ← increased tone / abnormal gait ← Parkinson's disease.**

The safe record-level point is not that Neurology received and ignored that wording. It is that primary care regenerated an integrated neurological–foot model while the surviving documentary destination remained Podiatry.

By 2022 the patient was doing remarkably well in important respects. The neurological clinic records that he had bought a dog and was walking **2.5–3 hours per day**. Yet the same note records that most mornings the feet took **20–25 minutes to “warm up”** and were sore around the corns and calluses. `CLIN-002378–002383`.

The future neurological treatment had restored a great deal of movement, but the unresolved foot system remained a recurring constraint within that restored mobility.

## 2023 exposes the cost

In March 2023 the GP urgently reported severe bilateral burning pain in the feet and lower legs, **“extreme at time and causing death wish,”** wrote **“I am worried about him,”** and requested review **“before we get into trouble here.”** Two days later the Parkinson's nurse separately recorded debilitating pain, **“no quality of life,”** and pregabalin exposure at 600 mg daily despite continuing symptoms.

This must not be overstated into a proved suicide plan or continuous suicidality.

But it is direct evidence that the physical pain had reached a level capable of producing profound psychological-endurance distress.

The claimant's formulation is therefore not that an unrelated psychiatric story appeared years later. It is that a longstanding lower-limb/foot problem became sufficiently painful and functionally restrictive to overwhelm a patient who had tolerated an exceptionally abnormal neurological state for many years.

That creates a separate 2023 safety question as well as a longitudinal harm question.

The later neurological review was not empty. It genuinely reconsidered current mechanisms. It recorded clawing, lower-limb cramping, inability to complete later walks, possible OFF dystonia, neuropathic and Fabry possibilities, medication state and the effect of Parkinsonian OFF states on pain.

Later in 2023 severe pain plus reduced mobility/shuffling was explicitly assessed as appearing **OFF-related**, and treatment was altered.

The important historical question is narrower:

> **Did that genuine current-state reconsideration reconstruct the original neurological–foot relationship in light of the 2017–2018 evidence, or did it manage the present symptoms without revising the founding boundary historically?**

## 2024 and 2025 separate components without resolving ownership

The 2024 record provides another useful natural experiment.

Pregabalin removed much of the pain while activity-dependent toe curling persisted, including during sustained physical effort and standing activities.

That separates components:

> **pain state ≠ toe-curling / motor state.**

But separable components are not necessarily independent systems. Pain can be pharmacologically modulated while a motor or postural driver persists.

By 2025 Neurology records that once Sinemet **“hits his system”** the feet feel better from a pain perspective, and states that increasing Sinemet would probably produce further pain relief, though at the cost of increased dyskinesia risk. The clinician also offers a medically plausible alternative explanation: dopaminergic treatment may alter the experience of pain even where the underlying painful source has another cause.

That explanation may be defensible in isolation.

But it must be judged longitudinally against the already-existing sequence:

> **dystonic-looking lower limb → recessive Parkinsonism / PRKN reasoning → procyclidine dechallenge involving the focal foot → recurrent fifth-MTPJ loading pathology and clawing → GP feet/tone/gait integration → OFF-related pain/shuffling → activity-dependent toe curling → Sinemet-responsive foot pain.**

The question is why these observations repeatedly acquired enough clinical significance to influence treatment, describe phenotype or explain symptoms without clearly producing an equivalent global revision of the original neurological–foot relationship.

That pattern is captured elsewhere in the repository as:

> **selective utilisation without integrative revision.**

Or more simply:

> **local updating, global non-revision.**

## Clinical lock-in

The leading allegation is therefore no longer one missed diagnosis.

It is a systems-level hypothesis of **clinical lock-in**:

> **a founding synthesis creates a boundary → the boundary shapes the care pathway → later evidence is repeatedly used within that pathway without clearly being allowed to test and revise the boundary itself.**

The word **lock-in** does not prove negligence. It describes the structure to be tested by expert evidence.

The strongest expert proposition would be:

> **The later evidence should have broken the original formulation, but the formulation instead governed how the later evidence was interpreted.**

If an independent movement-disorders expert reaches that conclusion, the issue is no longer merely whether the feet were once reasonably uncertain. It becomes whether the persistence of uncertainty was partly **formulation-dependent**.

The record repeatedly generated observations capable of bearing on the neurological–mechanical relationship. Those observations were often clinically used. The expert question is whether they were adequately assembled into a revised integrated formulation when reasonable practice required it.

## The patient remained inside the system he trusted

At the centre of this structure was a remarkably resilient patient who initially had every reason to trust it.

The tablets worked.

He regained movement.

He bought a dog.

He walked for hours.

Then the unresolved point where his neurological condition met the ground progressively became a constraint on the life that treatment had restored.

Eventually the pain became capable of overwhelming his longstanding capacity to cope.

The clinicians did not have to foresee, in July 2017, that foot pain would one day generate documented **“death wish”** language.

They had to do something considerably more ordinary.

They had to determine, or establish a process capable of determining, what the abnormal lower-limb motor disorder was doing to the abnormal foot attached to it, and what that foot was doing to the patient in return.

## The ultimate question

The ultimate question is therefore not:

> **Was every component of this man's foot pain Parkinsonian?**

It is:

> **How did a service sophisticated enough to recognise dystonia, recessive Parkinsonism and PRKN fail, if the expert evidence so finds, to take adequate ownership of the disabling feet through which that same abnormal movement was being lived?**

That is the clinical question.

The legal questions remain separate:

> **Fact:** what happened, including whether the Protest occurred?  
> **Breach:** at which information threshold did reasonable neurological practice require something different?  
> **Causation:** what would probably have changed, and which material harms would probably have been avoided or reduced?

The repository presently establishes the longitudinal clinical and documentary problem more strongly than it establishes ultimate liability. Expert evidence must still convert the identified fault-lines into breach and causation findings.
