# Limitation and Legal Routes

## Urgent limitation position

This file is a triage control, not a substitute for solicitor advice.

Under the presently commenced Irish limitation regime for personal injuries, the operative period is two years from accrual or the statutory date of knowledge, whichever is later. The Legal Services Regulation Act 2015 provision intended to create a three-year clinical-negligence period (s.221) must not be treated as operative unless commencement is re-verified at the time of use; as checked during compilation, it remained uncommenced.

Date of knowledge requires fact-specific analysis of knowledge that the injury was significant, attributable in whole or part to the alleged act/omission, and the relevant defendant identity. Knowledge that the conduct was legally negligent is not required. Constructive knowledge must also be assessed.

The repository therefore must not state a single limitation date as fact. Counsel should construct a limitation schedule for every candidate breach: 2017 closure/handover, later failures to reconsider, 2023 management, and any 2025 continuing-management allegation.

A March 2025 complaint that itself challenged historical framing may be relied on by a defendant as evidence of knowledge by that date for at least some propositions. Earlier knowledge will be argued. Later receipt of records does not automatically postpone time if the essential attribution facts were already known.

## Pre-action procedure

The Civil Liability and Courts Act 2004 letter-of-claim requirement should be checked and complied with urgently. Expert support should be obtained before advancing clinical-negligence particulars.

## Standard of care

Clinical breach must be translated to the Dunne principles: conduct is tested against an ordinarily competent practitioner of equal specialist/general status acting with ordinary care. Departure from a general practice is not automatically negligence; conformity with a practice is not necessarily a defence where an inherent defect would be obvious on due consideration. The court does not choose the treatment it prefers merely because medical opinion differs.

## Causation

The principal working route is conventional factual causation: prove that but for the actionable breach the relevant additional injury probably would not have occurred. Later deterioration or delay alone is not enough. Irish loss-of-chance concepts should not be used as a shortcut without specific counsel advice.

## HSE complaints

Health Act 2004 Part 9 distinguishes complaints concerning unfair/unsound administration from matters taken solely in the exercise of clinical judgment. Mixed complaints must keep those strands separate. Complaint/review handling may generate evidence, admissions, preservation duties or administrative issues; it does not itself prove the negligence case.

## Medical Council

The 8th edition professional guide is the relevant starting professional-standard context for 2017-era care; the 9th edition applies to later periods from 2024. Guidance on referral, handover, continuity, records and reports is relevant context but is not itself the Dunne legal test.

## GDPR

Article 16 supports rectification of inaccurate personal data and completion of incomplete data, including by supplementary statement. Historical bona fide clinical opinion is not automatically inaccurate merely because diagnosis or understanding later changes. Article 18 restriction and Article 19 recipient-notification issues should be considered where their statutory conditions are met. A damages claim requires infringement, damage and causal connection; infringement alone is insufficient.

## Required action

Solicitor/counsel should independently re-check commencement, limitation dates, defendants, procedural rules and current authorities immediately before any step is taken.
