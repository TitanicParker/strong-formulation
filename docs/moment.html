The moment omission became decision

The patient did not challenge a separation he already understood. He said, in substance: the feet have not been properly dealt with, so the neurological work cannot be finished.

Before

Integrated feet/gait/tone phenotype. Severe young-onset Parkinsonism. A lower-limb appearance suggestive of dystonic posture. Structural foot disease. No identified substantial neurological remapping of the feet after those discoveries.

The Protest

Established case fact: before closure, the patient says the feet have not been properly dealt with and the work cannot therefore be complete.

“full NCT … send me out on bald tires?”

The neurologist answers Podiatry. The record identifies no neurological reopening and no defined neurological handover question.

After

The discharge synthesis then allocates the feet to a separate/peripheral-neuropathy/Podiatry channel: “completely separate” or perhaps neuropathy, “Not clear.” It strengthens the patient-derived distinction into “markedly divorced” and omits the Protest.

Before the Protest: omission. After the Protest: decision. After the synthesis: durable partition.

Why this matters: the Protest did not merely add disagreement to an already-recognised unresolved relationship. It forced an unfinished part of the phenotype back into view. The later separation is the clinician-authored documentary answer that follows.

The patient said the feet had not been investigated. The neurologist answered Podiatry. The discharge synthesis then supplied the intellectual architecture that made that answer look medically complete.
