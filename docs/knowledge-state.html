The Patient's Documented Mind

Keep three things separate: what the patient actually said, what the clinician attributed to the patient's understanding, and what that attributed state later did for the management account.

Founding mirror

Patient-origin position: a limited phenomenological distinction — foot pain and internal leg tension were not experienced as the same problem.

Authored state: “in his own mind — markedly divorced”.

Documentary effect: a limited distinction becomes a stronger separation-compatible patient state.

The Protest is different

The patient does not say: “you have wrongly separated my feet from Parkinson's disease.” He says, in substance, that the feet have not been properly dealt with and the neurological work cannot therefore be complete.

Documentary effect: the state compatible with separation travels; the patient's warning that the phenotype is unfinished does not.
