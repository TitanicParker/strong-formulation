Neurological–Foot Integration

The expert is not asked whether every aspect of the foot pathology was caused directly by Parkinsonism. The question is whether Neurology adequately investigated, formulated and subsequently managed the relationship between a marked lower-limb motor disorder and painful abnormal feet.

Read the primary clinical narrative →

Five distinctions

  1. reasonable diagnostic uncertainty;
  2. inadequate investigation producing apparent uncertainty;
  3. reasonable separation of structural and neurological pathology;
  4. premature or unsupported clinical partitioning of the feet from the neurological disorder;
  5. failure to revise that partition when later evidence emerged.

1. Original neurological task

Address whether the July 2017 combination of painful abnormal feet, abnormal gait, increased tone, a dystonic-looking lower limb, suspected autosomal-recessive Parkinsonism and later PRKN/PARK2 reasoning required specific assessment of whether the feet, toe deformity/clawing, loading pattern and pain were manifestations of, consequences of, or materially influenced by the movement disorder.

Did omission of the dystonic lower-limb feature from the later enumerated PRKN/PARK2 phenotype represent a clinically significant failure of phenotypic integration?

2. “Not clear”

Identify what contemporaneous examination, investigation or reasoning demonstrates that the neurological relationship of the painful feet had been adequately assessed before the conclusion “Not clear” was operationalised.

Was this residual uncertainty after adequate assessment, or uncertainty resulting from inadequate investigation of the relationship?

3. Clinical partition and Podiatry

Consider whether Podiatry was appropriate for structural pathology but insufficient as the neurological response to unresolved dystonia/tone/gait/loading questions. Identify whether any defined neurological question or return mechanism existed.

4. Protest hypothetical

The claimant's account of a pre-discharge Protest is not assumed as fact. If accepted, state what reasonable neurological practice required before maintaining discharge, including whether reassessment, movement-disorders input, explanation, or defined follow-up was required, and whether the challenge and decision were material to the discharge record.

5. 2018 procyclidine dechallenge

In April 2018 the treating neurologist recorded that during withdrawal the first thing changing rapidly was the return on the foot of the “outside left corn” phenomenon, alongside broader worsening and being more “shifty” and “unrelaxed.” Do not assume a structural corn disappeared pharmacologically. Address what significance should reasonably have attached to a focal foot phenomenon changing rapidly with neurological treatment state.

Even if July 2017 uncertainty was initially reasonable, did the 2018 dechallenge require the original foot/neurological relationship to be reopened? If not, why not?

6. Recurrent structural pathology

Distinguish structural pathology from structurally independent pathology. Consider whether recurrent fifth-MTPJ callus/HD, clawed toes and mechanical offloading could have been influenced by abnormal posture or gait.

7. Later integration without reconstruction

Consider the later sequence: 2019 GP feet/tone/gait integration; 2022 prolonged walking with 20–25 minute morning foot warm-up and persistent corns/calluses; 2023 clawing, OFF dystonia, pain fluctuation and OFF-related pain/shuffling; 2024 pain relief with persistent toe curling; 2025 Sinemet-responsive foot pain.

Do these observations demonstrate genuine reconstruction of the original foot formulation, or treatment and explanation occurring within a framework that continued to preserve the original clinical separation?

8. Psychological distress and safeguarding

Address whether the March 2023 severe pain, “death wish” language, GP concern and nurse report of “no quality of life” required documented psychological-risk assessment, safeguarding or more urgent integrated neurological reassessment. Distinguish distress generated by severe persistent pain from a primary psychiatric explanation for the pain.

9. Breach propositions

For each potential breach identify the earliest date, what reasonable practice required, what was omitted and why the omission fell below acceptable neurological practice:

  1. inadequate July 2017 feet–gait–tone investigation;
  2. premature separation of the feet from the neurological formulation;
  3. failure, assuming the Protest occurred, to reconsider before discharge;
  4. inadequate neurological handoff to Podiatry;
  5. failure to reopen after the 2018 dechallenge;
  6. failure to integrate recurrent focal pathology with motor-state evidence;
  7. failure adequately to reassess when severe foot pain became associated with major psychological distress.

10. Causation

Keep breach and causation separate. Identify what competent integrated assessment would probably have led to, distinguishing possibility from probability: movement-disorders assessment, explicit dystonia examination, loading analysis, coordinated Neurology–Podiatry management, treatment-state testing, revised pharmacological strategy, earlier intervention or other justified measures.

Had the abnormal foot been maintained within, or subsequently restored to, the neurological formulation, is it more likely than not that the duration or severity of pain, structural injury, functional limitation or psychological distress would have been materially reduced?

Ultimate issue

The case should not be reduced to whether all foot pain was neurological.

Why did evidence repeatedly cross the neurological-foot boundary without the boundary itself being revised?

If reasonable neurological practice required that revision, the failure to perform it is the central alleged breach.
