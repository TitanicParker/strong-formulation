Prose layer

How a legitimate “Not clear” became a durable information state.

The case does not require Neurology to have known the exact foot mechanism in July 2017. It asks why an incompletely investigated uncertainty was finalized without a defined resolution mechanism, despite the patient directly challenging that disposition before closure.

The primitive event

The patient entered Neurology with an integrated feet–gait–tone–toe–function problem. Neurology identified severe young-onset Parkinsonism, a dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon and structural foot disease, but left the relationship “Not clear.” Before closure, the patient directly challenged the adequacy of the foot disposition. That Protest converted residual uncertainty into known contested uncertainty.

The Protest identified the updating failure before the updating failure had time to become longitudinal.

What the GP received

The specialist did not return the feet as “not neurological.” It proposed possible secondary peripheral neuropathy and left the relationship “Not clear.” That wording could reasonably leave the unresolved neurological component apparently within specialist ownership rather than clearly returning it to primary care.

“Not clear” did not return the question to the GP. It left the question apparently inside Neurology.

What Podiatry acquired

Podiatry repeatedly documented the left fifth-MTPJ site, debrided and enucleated it, offloaded it, and built insoles around that loading problem. It acquired a precise local mechanical truth, but the surviving record does not show a corresponding neuropathy diagnosis or a mechanism for feeding the structural findings back into Neurology’s unresolved relationship.

The fifth-MTPJ join

Podiatry

Where it was

On 26 October 2017 the HD at the left fifth MTPJ was enucleated to pin-prick haemorrhage, dressed, specifically offloaded, and a template was taken for an insole designed to unload that site. The same site recurred across later visits.

Neurology

What changed it

During the planned procyclidine withdrawal the patient contemporaneously reported restless feet and deteriorating gait. In April 2018 the treating neurologist recorded that the first rapidly changing foot phenomenon was the return of the outside-left-foot corn.

Podiatry knew where it was. Neurology knew it changed with neurological treatment. The record does not identify the point at which those facts were formally joined.

The unclosed hypothesis

Peripheral neuropathy entered the record as a possibility, not a finding: “perhaps a secondary peripheral neuropathy … Not clear.” No identified falsification pathway specified what test, examination, handover or return criterion would confirm or reject it.

That matters because later evidence could accumulate without automatically closing the hypothesis. Podiatry treated a recurrent mechanical site without documenting neuropathy; the GP later marked “No neuropathy”; neurological treatment changed the feet; high-dose pregabalin did not eliminate the later pain; and by March 2025 the consultant was still writing, “If there is a degree of peripheral neuropathy here…”

Peripheral neuropathy entered the record as a possibility requiring resolution, but because no criterion for resolving it was specified, it became capable of surviving indefinitely.

Care-pathway lock-in

Neurology did not resolve the feet and did not clearly declare them non-neurological. Primary care had reason to understand that the unresolved neurological component remained within specialist knowledge. Podiatry could treat the structural manifestation but did not own the neurological relationship.

The pathway had an exit, but no return route.

The longitudinal consequence

The relationship could have been reconstructed after three months, six months, one year, three years, six years or seven years. The later record repeatedly generated evidence capable of testing the founding boundary. What remains striking is that the founding “Not clear” state survived even after the clinical facts materially changed.

The current formulation

The defect was not that the foot relationship was uncertain in July 2017. The defect was that an incompletely investigated uncertainty was finalized into a care architecture without a defined mechanism for resolution, and that architecture proved capable of preserving the same uncertainty through repeated later evidence that should have forced reconstruction.

By 2025 the clinical facts had moved. The information state had not.
