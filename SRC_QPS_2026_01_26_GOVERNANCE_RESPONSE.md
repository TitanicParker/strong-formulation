**Date:** 26 January 2026  
**Sender:** Quality & Patient Safety Department, Galway University Hospital  
**Subject:** Governance Re-engagement: April 2025 Procedural Configuration and Continuing Reliance  
**Provenance:** Gmail correspondence located and read on 9 September 2026. Personal contact details and standard confidentiality footer omitted here; operative wording preserved.

The Quality & Patient Safety Department wrote:

> **“The complaint assessment and subsequent SIMT review were undertaken using the information, clinical presentations, and investigations available at the time, and the determinations reached were appropriate, specific to a predefined timeframe, and compliant with established governance frameworks.”**

It continued:

> **“A subsequent diagnosis of dystonia in December 2025 does not undermine the validity of those processes or outcomes. The SIMT assesses the quality and appropriateness of care based on contemporaneous data.”**

And then stated:

> **“In that regard, the updated diagnosis does not necessitate reopening governance reviews, altering prior determinations, or retrospectively amending historical medical records.”**

The response further stated:

> **“Updated diagnoses supersede earlier working diagnoses clinically, but do not render prior governance processes unlawful or unsafe.”**

Its operative governance conclusion was:

> **“On this basis, QPS does not identify a current governance risk arising from continued reliance on the completed processes, nor is escalation to another governance function indicated.”**

## Source significance

This source directly establishes the January 2026 QPS position. It does **not** by itself establish that the earlier clinical care was adequate, that the later dystonia diagnosis required reopening, or that QPS acted improperly.

It does establish that QPS expressly considered whether later information required reopening and concluded that:

- the prior complaint/SIMT determinations remained valid;
- reopening was not required;
- prior determinations need not be altered;
- and **continued reliance on the completed processes** did not create a current governance risk.
