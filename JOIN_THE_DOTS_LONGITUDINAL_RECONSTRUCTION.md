# Join the Dots — Longitudinal Reconstruction

**Status:** CONTROLLING RECONSTRUCTION LAYER — 9 September 2026

This file sits between chronology and argument. It is governed by [`TEMPORAL_FIREWALL_AND_DRIFT_AUDIT_2026_09_09.md`](TEMPORAL_FIREWALL_AND_DRIFT_AUDIT_2026_09_09.md).

Its purpose is not to introduce a new allegation. It reconstructs the clinical information state at successive thresholds and asks what happened to the unresolved neurological–foot proposition as new evidence accumulated.

The governing discipline is:

> **No individual observation is asked to prove more than it proves, and no later information state is permitted to travel backwards in time.**

At each threshold ask:

> **What was known then? What remained unresolved? What happened next? What later information must be excluded from this threshold?**

---

## 1. 29 June 2017 — integrated referral state

### What was known

The GP referral presented one integrated problem: painful abnormal bilateral feet, abnormal gait and neurological concern including increased tone and impaired movement.

### What remained unresolved

The relationship between the foot pathology and abnormal motor state had not yet been neurologically determined.

### What happened next

Neurology became responsible for examining the relationship between the presenting foot problem, gait and emerging motor syndrome.

The starting state was not:

> **Parkinson's plus an unrelated foot complaint.**

It was:

> **an integrated feet–gait–tone problem referred for neurological explanation.**

---

## 2. 3–10 July 2017 — inpatient bedside state

### What was known

The record identified severe young-onset Parkinsonism, marked rigidity/bradykinesia, striking gait, a lower-limb appearance **“somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture – NOT FOOT DROP”**, an **“almost dystonically unusual”** walk, and painful structurally abnormal feet.

### What remained unresolved

The relationship between the dystonic-looking lower limb / abnormal gait and the painful abnormal feet remained uncertain.

### What must not be imported backwards

The completed detailed PRKN/PARK2 synthesis, the final packet's departmental genetics article, Professor Healy's September opinion, and the later procyclidine treatment response cannot simply be assumed to have formed part of this bedside information state.

The DAT request does show contemporaneous suspicion of an autosomal-recessive Parkinsonian disorder. The exact timing and development of the more detailed PRKN/PARK2 construction remain to be reconstructed.

### What happened next

The patient remained available for further bedside examination until discharge. The next distinct threshold is the alleged Protest.

---

## 3. Pre-discharge Protest — decision-live state, if proved

### What was known

The patient's account is that immediately before discharge he said in substance:

> **You have not dealt with my feet.**

The occurrence and circumstances require ordinary factual proof.

### What the Protest was not

It was not an objection to PRKN/PARK2 reasoning, the final wording **“Not clear”**, the departmental genetics article or the sophistication of a discharge synthesis the patient had not seen.

It was an experiential objection to the clinical work actually received:

> **after the inpatient admission, the longstanding disabling feet had not, from the patient's perspective, been neurologically dealt with.**

### What remained unresolved

The mechanism of the feet remained unresolved, but the alleged incompleteness of the clinical work had now been expressly raised while the patient was still present.

### What happened next

Professional closure proceeded, if the Protest account is accepted.

This creates a discrete decision-after-notice question independent of what the final synthesis later contained.

---

## 4. Discharge / patient departure — professional closure

### What was known

The service had recognised a serious neurological syndrome and significant foot pathology. If the Protest is proved, it also knew that the patient said the feet had not been dealt with.

### What remained unresolved

Whether the painful structural foot state was neurologically influenced, mechanically secondary to the movement disorder, independent, or mixed.

### What happened next

The patient left hospital.

The repository must distinguish that event from the completion/finalisation of the sophisticated portable synthesis. The exact timing of particular synthesis elements, Complex Case Meeting input and departmental consultation remains an evidential target.

Thus:

> **professional closure and final documentary construction are connected but not automatically simultaneous.**

---

## 5. Final July 2017 discharge synthesis — the portable state

### What the final product contains

The final synthesis develops sophisticated Parkinsonian, recessive and PRKN/PARK2 reasoning and presents an unusually extensive specialist process. The final packet expressly includes departmental Parkinson-genetics material.

The foot relationship is left **“Not clear”** and compatible with being separate/peripheral, with Podiatry as an operational destination.

### The internal comparator

> **high-resolution final neurological synthesis versus relatively low-resolution documented bedside interrogation of the disputed foot–lower-limb relationship.**

Or:

> **the synthesis may have outrun the examination.**

### The timing question

> **When did the additional sophistication enter, who supplied it, and did any clinically material insight return to the patient as further examination before departure?**

The final packet proves the final information state. It does not itself answer that timing question.

### Operational consequence

The final state is:

> **epistemically uncertain, operationally committed.**

Podiatry could be entirely appropriate for structural pathology while still not answering the unresolved neurological ownership question.

## 6. DATscan and continuing aetiological uncertainty

The DAT request described a very young man with bilateral Parkinsonism, atypical features and an **“almost dystonically unusual”** walk, and stated suspicion of an autosomal-recessive Parkinsonian disorder.

The radiology report described findings consistent with idiopathic Parkinson's disease or Lewy body dementia. The treating neurologist regarded the abnormal result as expected and LBD as clinically inappropriate.

The treating record continued to develop recessive neurogenetics and PRKN/PARK2 reasoning.

Therefore:

> **the clinicians' own post-DAT conduct shows that they did not treat the scan as resolving the disorder into idiopathic Parkinson's disease.**

---

## 7. September 2017 — Healy adds post-discharge diagnostic specificity

Professor Daniel Healy personally reviewed the patient and recorded that he **“probably has Park II.”** He recommended PARK II testing first and a broader panel if negative.

This is a **post-discharge** information state and must not be projected into the Protest.

The later neurological record preserved the specific hypothesis: PARK2/PRKN was recorded as what **“we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause.”**

At this stage the aetiological state was:

> **Parkinsonism with a specifically suspected PRKN/PARK2 cause.**

---

## 8. September–October 2017 treatment response — a new biological crossing

Procyclidine/rasagiline treatment produced substantial improvement in motor state. During the October withdrawal, contemporaneous material records increasing tension, restless feet and deterioration of walking.

This evidence did **not** exist at the Protest threshold.

Its proper role is not to prove the July decision unreasonable by hindsight. It creates a **new later question** about whether the exported neurological–foot boundary remained tenable.

The correct threshold is not:

> **Did this prove dystonia caused the foot lesion?**

It is:

> **Did this create a reason to reopen a relationship already left unresolved?**

---

## 9. April 2018 — independent reopening threshold

The treating neurologist recorded that during the earlier procyclidine withdrawal the **first thing** changing, and changing rapidly, was return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon, alongside broader worsening and greater tension/restlessness.

The mechanism remained unproved.

The key expert question becomes:

> **Even if the July 2017 “Not clear” position was initially reasonable, did this treatment-state dechallenge require the original foot/neurological relationship to be reopened?**

Thus:

> **evidence sufficient to require investigation is not the same as evidence sufficient to prove mechanism.**

---

## 10. Podiatry — increasingly precise mechanical information

Podiatry repeatedly documented real focal structural pathology, including recurrent fifth-MTPJ callus/corn, clawed toes, debridement/enucleation and offloading.

What remained unresolved was whether the mechanical target was independent of, secondary to, or materially influenced by abnormal neurological loading/motor state.

The reconstruction question is:

> **Where was that increasingly precise mechanical information clinically rejoined to the neurological stream?**

Podiatry's appropriateness does not answer that question.

## 11. 2019 primary care — explicit reintegration appears outside Neurology

Primary care explicitly linked the feet with increased tone and abnormal gait related to Parkinson's disease.

The safe point is not that Neurology necessarily received and ignored this wording. It is that an integrated relationship was again articulated downstream while no identified corresponding global specialist reconstruction is yet visible.

## 12. 2022–2025 — repeated local updating

Later Neurology documented persistent morning foot limitation despite major restored mobility; clawing and OFF dystonia; pain and gait worsening in OFF states; toe curling persisting separately from pain; and dopaminergic improvement in the experience of foot pain.

These observations do not prove one mechanism.

They demonstrate repeated acquisition of information capable of bearing upon the original neurological–foot relationship.

The longitudinal audit question is:

> **What was each observation allowed to change?**

The repository describes the pattern as:

> **selective utilisation without integrative revision.**

Or:

> **local updating, global non-revision.**

## 13. Later reconsideration — reconsideration of what?

The later record contains genuine reconsideration. Current-state questions were revisited, including OFF state, pain modulation, neuropathy, Fabry disease, toe curling, levodopa response and medication balance.

A separate question remains:

> **Was the original 2017 clinical object reconstructed?**

That would require reassembling:

> **integrated referral → bedside dystonic-looking lower limb + painful abnormal foot → Protest, if proved → professional closure → final “Not clear” partition → post-discharge treatment-state crossings → recurrent focal mechanical pathology → later tone/gait reintegration → OFF/toe-curling phenomena → dopaminergic pain response.**

Thus:

> **“There was later reconsideration” does not by itself answer “Was the founding relationship reconstructed?”**

## 14. Diagnostic continuity — continuity of what?

The aetiological record changes materially:

> **July 2017 final Galway state: recessive PRKN/PARK2 seriously entertained**

> **September 2017 Healy: probably PARK II**

> **later Monaghan: PARK2/PRKN is what “we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause”**

> **later record: iPD increasingly becomes operative baseline**

> **25 March 2025: categorical idiopathic Parkinson's disease statement**

> **same rebuttal: Healy invoked as early validating Parkinson's expert**

> **same rebuttal: “In essence we have known that [the patient] has Parkinson's.”**

The specific PARK II content does not travel forward with Healy's authority.

> **Healy's authority survived; Healy's proposition did not.**

The controlling question is:

> **Continuity of what?**

There may be continuous recognition and treatment of a Parkinsonian disorder without stable continuity of the specific aetiological formulation.

## 16. Explanatory-model comparison

### Model A — ordinary longitudinal complexity

Genuine Parkinsonism and genuine structural foot disease coexist; uncertainty about their relationship is reasonable; Podiatry care is appropriate; the detailed PRKN hypothesis develops during ordinary synthesis and later loses favour; treatment responses are recognised appropriately; terminology evolves naturally; later reconsideration is adequate; complaint/governance reliance is reasonable.

### Model B — founding partition and resilient frame

The bedside neurological–foot relationship is insufficiently resolved; if the Protest is proved, that incompleteness is expressly raised before closure; professional closure proceeds; the final synthesis subsequently becomes a sophisticated authoritative state in which the foot remains operationally outside the principal frame; later neurological and mechanical evidence repeatedly crosses the boundary; evidence is used locally without clear global reconstruction; specific historical propositions can be lost or broadened while global **“Parkinson's”** continuity survives; the accumulated record later supports retrospective validation and institutional reliance.

The analytical question is:

> **Which model explains the documented sequence with fewer unsupported or undocumented transitions?**

Importantly, Model B no longer requires the sophisticated final frame to have existed before the Protest.

## 18. Relationship to documentary resilience

Documentary resilience can arise through inheritance of an authoritative final synthesis, local use of new evidence without global reconstruction, retention of expert authority while diagnostic specificity is lost, movement to broader categories capable of preserving apparent continuity, and later institutional reliance on the accumulated record.

The record need not be literally unchanged in order to appear continuous.

> **The record can remain globally coherent while material propositions underneath it change.**
