# Strong Formulation

A structured evidential reconstruction of a longitudinal neurological record.

## The crystal-clear centre

The patient did **not** enter July 2017 with an already-recognised “unresolved foot relationship.” He entered with an **integrated phenotype**: painful abnormal feet, abnormal gait, pathological lower-limb tone, toe-clawing, impaired movement initiation and deteriorating function.

The 29 June GP referral itself has a crucial internal architecture. Its opening paragraph begins with the concrete problem through which the patient arrived: bilateral foot problems, callouses/corns, an unusual stiff/festinant gait and the explicit observation that his feet were “not in a good state.” The remainder of the referral explains why the GP had become concerned that this was not merely a foot problem: impaired initiation, marked tone, toe-clawing, dexterity loss and functional deterioration under an open neurological differential.

Neurology then identified severe very-young-onset Parkinsonism, a lower-limb appearance suggestive of dystonic posture, genuine structural foot disease and a possible PRKN/PARK2 phenotype. The surviving record shows far greater investigative and explanatory development of the Parkinsonian syndrome than of the painful abnormal feet through which the referral began.

That initial narrowing may be understandable. The decisive event comes later.

Shortly before discharge, after days of specialist work, the patient directly said in substance that the feet had not been properly dealt with and that the neurological work could not therefore be complete. He was not introducing a new problem. He was **restoring the first paragraph of the GP referral to the centre of the encounter**.

That is now the controlling hinge:

> **Initial narrowing may be understandable. Once the patient restores the originating foot problem before closure, the question becomes what Neurology does with the whole referral.**

The neurologist answered **Podiatry**. There was no documented neurological reopening and no identified neurological handover carrying a defined feet–neurology question into Podiatry.

By then, substantial diagnostic and therapeutic commitment had accumulated around severe young-onset Parkinsonism, atypicality, possible PRKN/PARK2 and a cautious non-levodopa strategy involving senior Neurology. This does not prove private pride or bad faith. It does create an objective setting of **professional-coherence / commitment pressure**: reopening the restored first paragraph would require acknowledging that an elementary part of the referring problem remained insufficiently worked through despite days of sophisticated specialist work elsewhere.

The discharge synthesis is therefore not merely a document in which “the join is missing.” In an important sense, **the discharge synthesis is the join**. It contains the dystonic-looking lower-limb sign, possible PRKN/PARK2, painful structural feet, the patient’s phenomenological distinction, possible peripheral neuropathy and the treatment strategy. The question is how those joined elements are arranged.

The synthesis then supplies the durable architecture: Parkinsonism/PRKN/treatment strategy receive extensive explanatory development; the painful feet become compatible with a separate or peripheral-neuropathic route ending **“Not clear”** and Podiatry; the patient’s limited distinction is strengthened into **“markedly divorced”**; the Protest does not travel; and **“Plan/Recommendations: As per above”** makes the resulting arrangement portable.

The corrected causal spine is therefore:

> **integrated GP referral → striking neurological discoveries → asymmetric clinical weighting → days of specialist diagnostic/treatment commitment → patient restores paragraph one of the referral → Podiatry answer → no identified neurological reopening → discharge synthesis arranges all elements into a separation-compatible “Not clear” foot channel → Protest omitted → “As per above” makes that arrangement portable → later biological/mechanical evidence repeatedly crosses the boundary → local assimilation without identified global reconstruction.**

The shortest new centre is:

> **The patient did not discover a new problem at discharge. He restored the referrer’s original problem after days in which the record had overwhelmingly valued something else. The discharge synthesis then had to decide what that restored problem was allowed to mean.**

## Why the later record matters

The founding synthesis did not lose the feet. It decided what explanatory pathway they were allowed to inhabit.

Later biology repeatedly crossed that boundary. Procyclidine brought gait, tension and feet together; withdrawal destabilised them together. In April 2018 the treating neurologist recorded that the first thing to change rapidly off procyclidine was the return of the outside-left-foot corn phenomenon. Podiatry independently documented a recurrent left fifth-MTPJ loading problem and repeatedly offloaded it mechanically. Primary care repeatedly regenerated the integrated formulation. Later dopaminergic treatment again altered foot pain.

The record’s repeated behaviour is now itself part of the investigation:

1. integrated information enters;
2. one explanatory pathway is amplified;
3. the relationship to other components is comparatively thin;
4. uncertainty acquires an operational destination;
5. the prose arrangement becomes the plan;
6. later boundary-crossing information is absorbed locally;
7. the founding arrangement remains stable;
8. later continuity becomes available as evidence of longstanding understanding.

This is why the case is better described as an **information-arrangement problem** than a simple missing-information problem.

> **The biology updated. The document did not.**

## Constructive knowledge

By documentary closure the hospital possessed, at minimum, the architecture of the integrated GP referral, severe very-young-onset Parkinsonism, a dystonic-looking lower-limb sign, painful structural feet, a possible PRKN/PARK2 phenotype, uncertainty about the feet, senior treatment-policy input and a direct patient warning that the originating foot problem had not been properly worked through before discharge.

The hospital did not need to know the final mechanism. It knew enough to know that the whole referral had been put back in play before closure.

## What the case does not require

It does not require proof that the neurologist literally failed to read the first paragraph of the referral. It does not require proof that every structural foot abnormality had one neurological cause. It does not require proof that Neurology should have known the exact mechanism in July 2017. It does not require proof that reconsidering the feet required immediate levodopa. It does not require proof of pride, embarrassment, vanity, deliberate concealment or any other private motive.

The narrower proposition is stronger: the record shows that the neurological component of the referral received overwhelmingly greater clinical elaboration; after days of that work, the patient expressly restored the originating foot problem before closure; the final synthesis then arranged all known elements into a structure that permitted neurological closure while the painful feet remained “Not clear” and operationally elsewhere.

## Central evidential consequence

> **The hospital’s longitudinal record cannot serve as complete and self-sufficient proof of the material decision pathway merely by displaying continuity, because the founding synthesis itself is the object that must be explained: it was produced after the patient restored the underweighted first paragraph of the referral and it determined what the known facts were allowed to mean going forward.**
