# Controlling Case Formulation

## The case in one proposition

The actionable theory to test is a failure of adequate clinical reassessment, integration, handover and/or later reconsideration of an unresolved painful-foot/gait/tone problem, not negligence merely because Parkinsonism was diagnosed and not negligence merely because later treatment or genetics changed the explanatory picture.

## Evidential sequence

1. The GP referral presented abnormal gait, bilateral foot pain and muscle-tension/tone concerns as an integrated clinical problem.
2. Neurological assessment discovered major Parkinsonian features and also recorded lower-limb/foot phenomena whose relationship to the neurological syndrome was not fully resolved.
3. The founding synthesis itself preserved uncertainty: the feet could be separate or peripheral and the relationship was described as not clear.
4. The patient says that before closure he expressly restored the unresolved foot problem and sought further neurological attention. That encounter is presently evidenced by the patient's later account; it must not be misdescribed as a contemporaneous 2017 note. Corroborating witnesses and records should be sought.
5. The legal/clinical question is therefore what an ordinarily competent specialist should have done at that point: reassess, investigate, refer, define responsibility, document uncertainty, arrange review, or some combination. That is an expert question under the Dunne standard.
6. Subsequent treatment-state and longitudinal evidence is relevant in two different ways. It may assist retrospective expert analysis, but it cannot be used as hindsight proof of what was knowable in 2017. Independently, each later episode may itself have created a fresh duty-to-reconsider question on the information then available.
7. The later record includes changes in gait/feet with medication state, recurrent podiatry findings, GP reintegration of feet with tone/gait/Parkinsonism, later OFF-related pain/shuffling, severe bilateral burning pain, and 2025 reports of foot improvement with Sinemet/controlled-release treatment.
8. Documentary continuity matters only if it can be connected to clinical decision-making. Repeated language is evidence of inheritance; it is not itself negligence and is not itself causation.
9. Liability therefore requires expert evidence identifying at least one specific departure from ordinary specialist care at a defined date.
10. Causation then requires proof on the balance of probabilities that competent care would probably have changed investigation/management at a materially earlier time and thereby avoided identifiable additional injury.

## Candidate breach windows

- July 2017: handling of unresolved foot/gait/tone component at assessment and discharge, including reassessment, investigation, referral and handover.
- 2017-2019: response to dynamic treatment-state evidence and recurrent podiatry/GP information, if communicated to the relevant defendant.
- 2023: response to OFF-related pain/shuffling and severe bilateral foot/lower-leg pain.
- 2025: response to reproducible medication-linked relief and complaint/review information, insofar as ongoing clinical management remained affected.

Each window stands or falls independently on contemporaneous information, expert standard-of-care evidence, defendant knowledge and causation.

## Injury theory

The underlying Parkinsonian disorder and any independent structural foot disorder are not alleged to have been caused by negligent care. Recoverable injury, if proved, is differential injury: additional or prolonged pain, functional loss, psychiatric injury, treatment burden, earnings loss, care/expense or other damage that probably would have been avoided with competent earlier management.

## Bottom line

The strongest legally coherent case is not 'the document was wrong'. It is: unresolved integrated symptoms were separated operationally; the adequacy of the resulting reassessment/handover must be tested by an independent expert; later information may have supplied additional reconsideration triggers; and damages depend on proving a probable earlier management pathway and avoidable differential harm.
