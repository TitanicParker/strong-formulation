# Contemporaneous Departmental Knowledge and the 2025 Rebuttal

**Status:** CONTROLLING EVIDENTIAL / CONTINUITY ANALYSIS — 9 September 2026

This file preserves a pairing that must not be split apart in later analysis:

1. the July 2017 discharge packet did not merely mention PRKN/PARK2 in the abstract; it expressly enclosed a departmental article on Parkinson genetics written a couple of years earlier with Professor Timothy Counihan; and
2. the March 2025 rebuttal later relied upon the continuity of the clinical letters and a longstanding claimed understanding when explaining the historical pathway.

The significance is not that a journal article proves what any one clinician personally knew, or that the Parkinson's diagnosis was wrong. The significance is that the department's own published knowledge became part of the patient's contemporaneous discharge information state and must therefore be analysed alongside the later claim of longstanding clinical knowledge.

---

## 1. The 2015 genetics article was not merely external background

The July 2017 discharge material states, in the genetic-counselling section:

> **“Another article on the genetics of Parkinsons written a couple of years ago from the Department here with Prof Counihan is enclosed within.”**

The patient has identified the enclosed article as the 2015 Galway departmental article **“Genetics in Parkinson's disease — what have we learned?”**

That article discussed young-onset and genetic Parkinsonism, including PRKN/Parkin, and described distinguishing clinical features that included young age of onset, dystonia and symmetrical signs/symptoms.

The evidential consequence is important:

> **The 2015 departmental knowledge was not simply available somewhere in the literature. The discharge packet itself brought that knowledge into the patient's case.**

That changes the proper comparison.

The question is no longer only:

> **What did Galway Neurology as an academic department know in 2015?**

It is also:

> **What phenotypic knowledge was expressly selected for inclusion in this patient's July 2017 discharge package, and how was that knowledge applied to the phenotype actually observed at the bedside?**

---

## 2. This intensifies the synthesis–examination differential

The final synthesis displays sophisticated genetic and phenotypic reasoning. It entertains recessive Parkinsonism and PRKN/PARK2; it emphasises very young onset, marked symmetry and other features; it discusses genetic counselling, prognostication, future whole-exome/whole-genome analysis and treatment sensitivity.

At the same time, the bedside record had already described:

- a **strikingly abnormal gait**;
- a lower-limb appearance **“somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture – NOT FOOT DROP”**;
- an **“almost dystonically unusual”** walk;
- painful abnormal feet;
- pes planus and hammertoes;
- longstanding corns/calluses and foot pain.

The correct audit question is therefore:

> **If departmental PRKN/Parkin knowledge was sufficiently relevant to be enclosed within the discharge packet, where is the corresponding phenotypic operation that brought the recognised dystonic lower limb and painful abnormal feet back into the PRKN/Parkinsonian construction before the feet were left “Not clear” and operationally separated?**

This does not prove that dystonia or the feet would have established PRKN.

It makes the selectivity of the synthesis clinically significant.

---

## 3. The later rebuttal must be read beside this contemporaneous knowledge state

The March 2025 response to Quality and Patient Safety expressly relied upon the longitudinal record when explaining the historical management.

The repository presently preserves the exact statements:

> **“As the letters show, we have had a clear understanding for quite some time that Sinemet does help his pain.”**

and:

> **“In 2017 very openly and very explicitly…”**

The response also describes the historical position in terms including **“we have known”** and relies upon the accumulated letters and treatment history when defending the clinical pathway.

Where the exact rebuttal phrase concerning longstanding knowledge of IPD is relied upon externally, the primary rebuttal should be quoted verbatim rather than paraphrased. The patient's shorthand — **“we have always known Liam had IPD”** — should therefore remain a pointer to the rebuttal position unless and until the exact source wording is inserted here from the primary response.

The key analytical point does not depend on rhetorical compression:

> **The later rebuttal invokes longstanding knowledge and documentary continuity. The July 2017 discharge package itself shows that sophisticated departmental genetic knowledge had already been placed inside the patient's information state.**

These two propositions belong together.

---

## 4. Why the pairing matters

Read separately, each fact is capable of being minimised.

The 2015 article can be described as mere academic background.

The 2025 rebuttal can be described as a later clinician explaining an established Parkinson's diagnosis.

Read together, they create a more exact evidential question:

> **What exactly was “known,” what clinical work had earned that knowledge, and which parts of the phenotype were actually allowed to participate in the knowledge claimed later as longstanding?**

The Parkinsonian diagnosis and the neurological–foot integration question are different propositions.

A later statement that Parkinsonism/IPD had long been understood does not establish that the painful foot relationship had been adequately examined.

Indeed, the stronger the claim of longstanding Parkinsonian understanding, the sharper the unresolved comparator becomes:

> **If the department had long understood the Parkinsonian frame, why did the dystonic-looking lower limb and painful foot remain outside that frame without a documented return examination or integrative reconstruction?**

---

## 5. The crucial asymmetry

The discharge packet demonstrates two forms of knowledge operating at once.

### Theoretical / departmental knowledge

The packet contains or refers to:

- young-onset Parkinsonism;
- recessive inheritance;
- PRKN/PARK2;
- treatment sensitivities;
- neurogenetic counselling;
- future genomic testing;
- and an enclosed Galway departmental genetics article with Professor Counihan.

### Bedside / unresolved knowledge

The same packet preserves:

- dystonic-looking lower-limb behaviour;
- abnormal gait;
- painful structural feet;
- corns and calluses;
- pes planus;
- hammertoes;
- and the conclusion that the relationship may be separate / neuropathic / **“Not clear.”**

Thus:

> **The department could place sophisticated Parkinson genetic knowledge into the discharge packet while leaving the clinically adjacent foot relationship unresolved.**

That is the evidential fact requiring expert explanation.

> **departmental academic knowledge → selected into patient discharge packet → founding synthesis → longitudinal letters → later rebuttal invokes the letters as evidence of longstanding knowledge.**

This is not necessarily circular.

It is a provenance question.

The governing audit question is:

> **Which later claims of longstanding understanding rest on independently re-tested clinical propositions, and which rest on continuity of a documentary framework that had already selected what parts of the phenotype counted as evidence?**

## 8. Controlling formulation

> **The 2015 Galway genetics article should not be treated as external academic scenery. The July 2017 discharge packet expressly says that a departmental Parkinson-genetics article with Professor Counihan was enclosed. That makes departmental PRKN/Parkin knowledge part of the patient's contemporaneous information state. When the later 2025 rebuttal then relies upon the continuity of the letters and a longstanding claimed understanding of the Parkinsonian condition, the two facts must be read together. The resulting question is not whether Galway knew about Parkinsonism in general. It is what clinical work justified the particular phenotype it claimed to understand, and why the recognised dystonic lower limb and painful abnormal foot were not equivalently integrated into that understanding before the feet were left outside the frame.**

Shortest form:

> **The knowledge was in the packet. The later rebuttal says the understanding was longstanding. The unresolved question is what bedside work earned that understanding where the left foot was concerned.**

## Evidential discipline

This file does **not** establish that:

- the 2015 article proves what Dr Monaghan personally knew before reading or enclosing it;
- Professor Counihan personally examined the patient;
- the article required a particular diagnosis;
- dystonia or foot pain would have proved PRKN;
- the Parkinson's/IPD diagnosis was wrong;
- the 2025 rebuttal was dishonest;
- or documentary continuity alone proves circularity.

It establishes a paired evidential structure that should remain intact: **contemporaneous departmental knowledge selected into the discharge packet + later reliance on longstanding documentary/clinical understanding.**
