# Expert Instruction — Clinical Lock-In / Neurological–Foot Integration

**Status:** EXPERT-FACING COMPANION — 9 September 2026

This document sits beside [`CLINICAL_LOCK_IN_MASTER_NARRATIVE.md`](CLINICAL_LOCK_IN_MASTER_NARRATIVE.md) and must be read subject to [`TEMPORAL_FIREWALL_AND_DRIFT_AUDIT_2026_09_09.md`](TEMPORAL_FIREWALL_AND_DRIFT_AUDIT_2026_09_09.md).

The expert should **not** assume that the completed PRKN/PARK2 discharge formulation existed before the alleged Protest or before patient departure, and should **not** import September/October 2017 treatment response into the July admission. Where timing is material, the expert is expressly asked to reconstruct it.

---

## Purpose of instruction

The central issue is not whether every aspect of the claimant's foot pathology was caused directly by Parkinsonism. It is whether Neurology adequately investigated, formulated and subsequently managed the relationship between a markedly abnormal lower-limb motor disorder and painful abnormal feet at each relevant information threshold.

The expert is asked to distinguish reasonable diagnostic uncertainty; inadequate investigation producing apparent uncertainty; reasonable separation of structural and neurological pathology; premature or unsupported partitioning; and later failure to revise a partition when new evidence emerged.

## 1. The original neurological task — July bedside state

The claimant was referred with an integrated presentation involving abnormal gait, painful abnormal feet, increased muscle tone and progressive neurological dysfunction.

During the July 2017 admission Neurology documented a **“strikingly abnormal”** gait and a lower-limb appearance **“somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture – NOT FOOT DROP.”**

The DAT-scan request stated:

> **“Walk is almost dystonically unusual. We suspect an Autosomal recessive ‘Idiopathic’, Parkinsons.”**

The final discharge synthesis separately identified features supporting possible PRKN/PARK2: very young age of onset, akinetic rigidity, absence of dominant family history, marked symmetry and excellent cognition.

The expert should distinguish the **bedside information state** from the **final synthesis state**. The final PRKN reasoning may be used as an internal comparator for the sophistication the service ultimately achieved, but its detailed timing should not be assumed.

The expert should address whether the information demonstrably available while the claimant remained present reasonably required specific consideration of whether the painful feet, toe deformity/clawing, gait disturbance and loading pattern were manifestations of, consequences of, or materially influenced by the lower-limb movement disorder.

The expert should also address a separate timing question:

> **When does the record demonstrate that detailed PRKN/PARK2 reasoning entered the patient-specific case, and if it entered while the claimant remained available, did it reasonably require a return phenotype-directed examination of the lower limb and feet?**

If the detailed reasoning arose only during later completion/finalisation of the synthesis, the expert should state whether any clinically material new insight required a defined follow-up or reintegration process.

## 2. The final “Not clear” conclusion

The final discharge synthesis states that the leg symptoms including corns, calluses and burning may have been **“completely separate,”** perhaps secondary to peripheral neuropathy, and concludes **“Not clear.”**

The claimant does not contend that uncertainty itself constituted negligence.

The expert is asked to identify what contemporaneous examination, investigation or reasoning demonstrates that the neurological–foot relationship had been adequately assessed before that uncertainty acquired an operational consequence.

The expert should distinguish:

> **residual uncertainty after adequate assessment**

from

> **uncertainty resulting from inadequate investigation.**

The expert should also identify when the operative **“Not clear”** position can first be shown to have crystallised.

## 3. Clinical partition and disposition

The subsequent management placed the feet into a Podiatry pathway.

Podiatry referral may have been entirely appropriate for structural pathology. The expert question is whether Neurology had completed its own task, or established a process capable of completing it, before practical foot care was allowed to proceed separately.

The expert should identify whether the handoff contained a defined neurological question concerning dystonia, abnormal loading, posture, increased tone or gait, and whether Podiatry findings were intended to return to Neurology for integrated interpretation.

## 4. The alleged pre-discharge Protest — decision-live state

The claimant's account is that immediately before discharge he expressly complained that the principal disabling foot problem had not been dealt with.

That factual allegation requires separate proof and should not be assumed as established.

If accepted, the expert must analyse it using **only the information reasonably available at that time**.

The claimant was not objecting to the later completed discharge synthesis, detailed PRKN/PARK2 reasoning or the wording **“Not clear.”** His asserted complaint concerned the clinical work he had experienced: the feet had not been dealt with.

The expert is asked:

> **If that account is accepted, what would reasonable neurological practice have required while the claimant remained available and before maintaining discharge?**

Possible responses for the expert to consider include revisiting the assessment, examining the neurological relationship of the feet to the abnormal gait, obtaining movement-disorders input if reasonably available/required, explaining why no further neurological assessment was necessary, or arranging defined follow-up of the unresolved relationship.

The expert should also address whether such a challenge and the clinical decision made in response would ordinarily constitute material information for the final discharge record and subsequent GP.

## 5. Post-discharge treatment response and 2018 dechallenge

This is a separate and potentially independent breach issue. It must not be used to populate the July Protest information state.

By September 2017 Professor Healy recorded dramatic motor improvement with low-dose procyclidine and rasagiline.

During the October 2017 interruption, contemporaneous material records tension building, restless feet and deterioration of walking.

In April 2018 the treating neurologist recorded that during the earlier withdrawal:

> **“the first thing he noticed changing and changing rapidly was the return on the foot, of the outside left corn.”**

The note also records broader worsening and the claimant being more **“shifty”** and **“unrelaxed.”**

The expert is not asked to assume that a structural corn itself disappeared and reappeared pharmacologically.

The expert should address what clinical significance should reasonably have been attached to a focal foot phenomenon changing rapidly with neurological treatment state. Possible mechanisms may include altered dystonia, posture, tone, gait, loading, pain generation or pain modulation.

The central question is:

> **Even if the July 2017 uncertainty was initially reasonable, did the post-discharge treatment-state evidence, culminating in the April 2018 treating-neurologist record, require the original foot/neurological relationship to be reopened? If not, why not?**

## 6. Recurrent structural pathology

Podiatry repeatedly documented focal pathology at the left fifth MTPJ, including callus/HD, debridement, enucleation, clawed toes and mechanical offloading.

The claimant's case is not that structural pathology did not exist.

The expert should distinguish:

> **structural pathology**

from

> **structurally independent pathology.**

The question is whether repeated pathology at a load-bearing site could reasonably have been mechanically influenced by abnormal neurological posture or gait.

## 7. Later integration without necessarily reconstructing the historical boundary

The later record contains observations crossing the neurological/foot boundary.

In 2019 the GP described foot problems in relation to increased tone and abnormal gait associated with Parkinson's disease. In 2022 the claimant was walking 2.5–3 hours daily while the feet still took 20–25 minutes to **“warm up”** in the morning and remained sore around corns/calluses. By 2023 Neurology was considering clawing, OFF dystonia and Parkinsonian pain fluctuation; later severe pain plus shuffling was considered OFF-related and treatment altered. By 2024 pregabalin relieved pain while activity-dependent toe curling persisted. By 2025 Neurology recorded that once Sinemet **“hits his system”** the feet feel better from a pain perspective and that more Sinemet would probably provide additional pain relief, with dyskinesia risk.

The expert should consider whether these later observations demonstrate reconstruction of the original neurological–foot formulation or treatment/explanation of current symptoms without historical reconstruction.

No later observation should be used retrospectively to define what was known in July 2017. Its role is to identify later reopening thresholds and causation questions.

## 8. Psychological distress and safeguarding

By March 2023 the GP described severe bilateral burning pain in the feet/lower legs, **“extreme at time and causing death wish,”** stated **“I am worried about him”** and requested review **“before we get into trouble here.”** The Parkinson's nurse separately recorded debilitating pain and **“no quality of life”** despite pregabalin 600 mg/day.

The expert should distinguish psychological distress associated with severe persistent pain from a primary psychiatric explanation for the pain.

The relevant questions are whether severity required documented psychological-risk assessment/safeguarding, whether distress increased the urgency of neurological reassessment, and whether reasonable care required integrated consideration of pain, gait, OFF state, dystonia and functional deterioration.

The claimant does not contend that Neurology in 2017 was required to foresee later **“death wish”** language.

## 9. Breach propositions

The expert is asked to state separately whether any of the following constituted care below the standard of a reasonably competent neurologist:

1. failure adequately to investigate the feet–gait–tone relationship during the July 2017 bedside opportunity;
2. premature or unsupported operational separation of the feet from the neurological formulation;
3. failure, assuming the Protest occurred, to reconsider after express notice before discharge;
4. inadequate neurological handoff / reintegration mechanism with Podiatry;
5. failure to reopen after the post-discharge 2017/April 2018 treatment-state evidence;
6. any later **date-specific** failure to integrate recurrent focal foot pathology with motor-state evidence;
7. any separate failure adequately to reassess/safeguard when severe foot pain became associated with major psychological distress in 2023.

For each alleged breach, the expert should identify:

- the earliest date at which breach occurred;
- the information actually available then;
- later information that must be excluded from that threshold;
- what reasonable practice required;
- what was omitted;
- and why that omission fell below acceptable neurological practice, if it did.

## 10. Causation

Breach and causation should be addressed separately.

The expert should identify the likely counterfactual pathway for each breach found, distinguishing what was merely possible from what was probable.

Possible clinically justified steps may include movement-disorders assessment, explicit assessment of lower-limb dystonia, analysis of pathological loading, coordinated Neurology–Podiatry management, treatment-state testing, revised pharmacological strategy, earlier therapeutic intervention or other measures.

The ultimate causation question is:

> **At each breach threshold found, if competent care had occurred using the information then available, is it more likely than not that the duration or severity of pain, structural injury, functional limitation or psychological distress would have been materially reduced?**

## Ultimate issue

The case should not be reduced to whether all foot pain was **“neurological.”**

The expert should first reconstruct the information states, then decide whether a clinically unsupported or insufficiently investigated boundary arose, and then determine whether later evidence required revision.

The critical longitudinal question remains:

> **Why did evidence repeatedly cross the neurological-foot boundary without the boundary itself being revised?**

But the temporal rule is equally controlling:

> **No later evidence may be used to make an earlier decision look unreasonable unless the expert separately explains what was actually knowable and required at the earlier date.**
