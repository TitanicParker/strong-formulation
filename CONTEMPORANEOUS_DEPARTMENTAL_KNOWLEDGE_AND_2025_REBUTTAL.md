# Contemporaneous Departmental Knowledge and the 25 March 2025 Rebuttal

**Status:** CONTROLLING EVIDENTIAL / CONTINUITY ANALYSIS — 9 September 2026

This file preserves a pairing that must not be split apart in later analysis:

1. the final July 2017 discharge packet expressly enclosed a Galway departmental article on Parkinson genetics written a couple of years earlier with Professor Timothy Counihan; and
2. the 25 March 2025 complaint response later relied upon a claimed longstanding Parkinsonian understanding and upon the longitudinal record when defending the historical pathway.

A temporal qualification is controlling throughout this file:

> **Inclusion of departmental knowledge in the final discharge packet proves that it entered the discharge information state. It does not, by itself, prove when it entered the case, that it was available before the Protest, that the patient knew it, or that it generated further bedside examination before departure.**

The significance is not that a journal article proves what any one clinician personally knew, or that the Parkinson's diagnosis was wrong. The significance is that the department's own published knowledge became part of the patient's final July 2017 discharge state and must therefore be analysed alongside the later claim that the Parkinsonian condition had effectively been known throughout.

---

## 1. The 2015 genetics article was part of the final discharge information state

The July 2017 discharge material states:

> **“Another article on the genetics of Parkinsons written a couple of years ago from the Department here with Prof Counihan is enclosed within.”**

The article identified in the record is the 2015 Galway departmental paper:

> **Patrick Browne, Timothy Counihan, Patrick Cullinane and Aoife Corcoran, “Genetics in Parkinson's disease — what have we learned?”**

Public source: https://www.irishhealthpro.com/content/articles/print/name/genetics-in-parkinsons-disease--what-have-we-learned

The article discusses young-onset and genetic Parkinsonism, including PRKN/Parkin, and identifies features including young age of onset, dystonia and symmetrical signs/symptoms.

The evidential consequence is important:

> **The 2015 departmental knowledge was not merely available somewhere in the literature. The final discharge packet selected that knowledge for inclusion in this patient's case.**

But the chronology must remain open.

The correct questions are:

> **When was that article selected? When did its relevant phenotypic content enter the clinicians' patient-specific reasoning? Who supplied that reasoning? Did it return to the patient as further examination before he left?**

Thus the question is not merely what Galway Neurology knew academically in 2015. It is:

> **What phenotypic knowledge did Galway ultimately bring into this patient's July 2017 discharge package, when did it enter, and what clinical work was performed with it while the patient remained available?**

---

## 2. The department's contemporary academic environment is a capability context

Publicly available sources show that the Galway neurological environment around the material period contained active work in movement disorders, dystonia, PRKN/Parkin genetics, young/juvenile Parkinsonism, Parkinsonian gait and treatment-state movement assessment.

Examples include the August 2017 **“Clinical Reasoning: A demure teenager and her dystonic foot”** paper by Patrick Cullinane, Patrick Browne, Michael Hennessy and Timothy Counihan, and 2017 Parkinsonian gait research involving Browne and Counihan whose publication cycle crossed the period of the July admission.

These facts do **not** prove that any particular academic personally examined the patient, attended the Complex Case Meeting, supplied a particular sentence in the discharge synthesis, or contributed before the Protest.

They establish a narrower proposition:

> **The concepts needed to ask the neurological–foot integration question were within the contemporaneous intellectual and clinical capability of the department.**

Capability is context. Patient-specific use and timing still require proof.

---

## 3. This intensifies the synthesis–examination differential without collapsing time

The final synthesis displays sophisticated genetic and phenotypic reasoning. It entertains recessive Parkinsonism and PRKN/PARK2, emphasises very young onset and marked symmetry, discusses genetic counselling and future genomic analysis, and encloses departmental genetics material.

The bedside record had described a **“strikingly abnormal”** gait, a lower-limb appearance **“somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture – NOT FOOT DROP”**, an **“almost dystonically unusual”** walk, painful abnormal feet, pes planus/hammertoes and longstanding corns/calluses and foot pain.

The earlier formulation asked where the PRKN knowledge was “returned” before the feet were left **“Not clear.”** That question remains useful only if the relevant knowledge can be shown to have entered while the patient was still available.

The temporally correct audit is therefore two-branched:

> **If the sophisticated PRKN/Parkin reasoning was already active before departure, where is the corresponding phenotype-directed return examination of the dystonic lower limb and painful abnormal foot?**

> **If that sophistication developed principally during completion/finalisation of the discharge synthesis, why did the final product become more phenotypically sophisticated than the documented examination, and what process existed to act on any clinically material insight acquired after the bedside opportunity had closed?**

This does not prove that dystonia or the feet would have established PRKN.

It makes the synthesis–examination differential clinically significant without inventing chronology.

## 4. The 25 March 2025 complaint response must be read beside the actual 2017 sequence

The 25 March 2025 complaint response states:

> **“Whatever the underlying cause for the peripheral neuropathic pain symptomatologies that [the patient] has, it is our belief and understanding that [the patient] has idiopathic Parkinson's disease.”**

Later in the same response:

> **“In essence we have known that [the patient] has Parkinson's; we have known it would be Sinemet or other dopamine agonist responsive...”**

The same response invokes Professor Daniel Healy as an early second-opinion Parkinson's expert.

But Healy's September 2017 opinion was post-discharge and more specific:

> **the patient “probably has Park II.”**

The repository therefore distinguishes:

> **continuity of Parkinsonian care**

from

## 5. The pairing changes the meaning of the later “we have known” position

Read separately, the 2015 article can be called academic background and the 2025 response a later clinician describing an established Parkinsonian disorder.

Read together, they create a more exact set of temporal questions:

> **What exactly was known at each threshold? When was it known? What clinical work had earned that knowledge? Which parts of the phenotype were permitted to participate in the knowledge later described as longstanding?**

The later claim that Parkinson's had effectively been known throughout does not answer whether the neurological–foot relationship was adequately examined.

Nor does the final packet permit the repository to assume that the complete Parkinsonian/genetic frame was already present at the Protest.

The stronger formulation is:

> **The final discharge state was academically supported and genetically sophisticated, and the later rebuttal describes the Parkinsonian understanding as longstanding. The unresolved question is when that sophistication entered the case and what bedside work, if any, it generated where the recognised dystonic lower limb and painful foot were concerned.**

## 6. The department must remain part of the case, but not as a substitute for proof

The repository must distinguish four evidential layers:

### Patient-specific bedside state

What was observed, examined, investigated, discussed and known while the patient remained available.

### Final discharge information state

What the completed portable synthesis ultimately contained, including departmental literature and PRKN/PARK2 reasoning.

### Contemporaneous departmental capability

What concepts, expertise and research were demonstrably present in the department, without assuming that capability entered this patient's case at a particular time.

### Later institutional use

How the founding and longitudinal record was later used in complaint review, retrospective rebuttal and governance reliance.

The department context cannot prove that undocumented clinical work occurred or did not occur.

It can make broad explanations based on **conceptual unavailability, novelty or mere complexity** less persuasive where the department's own work shows the relevant conceptual tools were available.

Thus:

> **Institutional capability does not prove breach. It constrains explanations based on lack of available understanding; timing and patient-specific application still require proof.**

## 7. Relationship to complexity

A generic complexity defence becomes narrower once the department context is retained.

The case may still have been clinically difficult.

But the final hospital product demonstrates sophisticated retrospective phenotype construction, including PRKN/PARK2 reasoning, while the department was contemporaneously active in dystonia, genetic Parkinsonism and gait work.

The expert question is:

> **Did complexity explain residual uncertainty after adequate interrogation, or did sophisticated reasoning ultimately develop in one part of the phenotype while the disputed foot relationship received a materially lower-resolution documented inquiry?**

A second timing question must accompany it:

> **When did the additional sophistication arise, and was there a reasonable opportunity or mechanism for it to alter the clinical handling of the unresolved feet?**

## 9. Controlling methodological rule

At every material threshold, ask:

> **What did the patient-specific record show at that time?**

> **What did the patient know?**

> **What did the treating clinician demonstrably know?**

> **What did the department have the capability to understand?**

> **When did departmental knowledge actually enter this patient's case?**

> **How was the resulting record later inherited, interpreted or relied upon?**

The strongest formulation is:

> **The question is not simply what was knowable. It is what this department could understand, what it demonstrably knew at each threshold, when that knowledge entered this patient's case, what clinical work was performed with it, and what nevertheless remained outside the final frame.**

Shortest form:

> **Record + department + later reliance — never backwards in time.**

- the 2015 article was selected before the Protest;
- its content informed bedside care before patient departure;
- the completed PRKN/PARK2 formulation existed before the Protest;
